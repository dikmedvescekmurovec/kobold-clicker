## Sprites cut from bought packs (`tools/ui_kit.py`, `tools/loot_beam.py`)

**The interface is not generated, with one exception.** The exception is the combat nameplate's health bar: `hpbar.py` draws its nine parts (a left cap, a right cap and a track, in three tier colourways) in `hexlib.PALETTE`, `qa.py hpbar <tag>` previews and checks them, and `build_hpbar.py` writes them loose into `Assets/UI/` and reads every file back. They skip Aseprite and the atlas the way the slimes do, and they stay out of `ui_sheet.png` and `ui_sheet.json` the way the pip parts do — a part is drawn at its own size and never stretched, so it has no nine-slice, and `test_ui_theme` counts that sheet exactly. It is drawn rather than cut because no pack holds it: `Pixel UI pack 3/06.png` is the flat capsule the pips come off and its three tier bands differ only in colour, and `04.png` is on disk as `Assets/bars.png` already, turned down by `CombatScene._bar` for being a glossier style than the rest of the interface. Everything else is cut from the bought Craftpix pack under `Assets/Potential/2D Pixel UI/` by `tools/ui_kit.py`, which writes `Assets/UI/` (the sheet, the loose sprites and `ui_sheet.json`) and a tiling preview at `tools/qa/ui_kit_tiling.png`. The pack ships finished panels and buttons rather than a slice kit, so that file is where it is written down which rectangle of which pack sheet is which sprite and where its nine-slice margins fall. Both panels are the pack's own art, cut whole; the one thing that is not is the button, because the pack has one colour and three states — the danger and disabled faces are palette swaps of the green, mapped by hue and saturation with each step's lightness kept, the way `slimes.py` recolours the blue slime.
The same script also cuts the combat HUD's **kill-pip bar** from a second bought pack, `Assets/Potential/Pixel UI pack 3/06.png`, whose capsule the 2D Pixel UI pack has no equivalent of. The game needs that capsule ten and fifteen pips long and the pack ships it at five, so what is cut is not the bar but the three parts it is built from — a head, a body and a tail, each in the three tier colourways and in the pack's empty grey, twelve loose PNGs into `Assets/UI/` and never into the sheet, with a preview at `tools/qa/ui_kit_pips.png` showing the assembled bar at all eleven drain states. That is possible because the capsule sits on a rigid **4 px segment pitch** — measured off the pixels and checked against both the 1/5 and the 5/5 step, an n-segment fill is always columns `1 .. 4n+1`, with col 0 the black left edge, cols 1–4 the segment carrying the rounded left shoulder, cols 5–8 onward the repeating interior segment, and cols 25–26 the rounded right end. The right end is taken off the **empty** capsule for every colourway, because that is how the pack draws the end of its own full bar. The fill steps are left uncut (a pip is a whole enemy, not a fraction of one) and so is the silver colourway — there is no fourth tier.

The same file also cuts the eight **orb icons** (`ORBS`, `orbs()`) off `Assets/Potential/OreAndGem/OreGemSpritesheet.png`, a 10x5 grid of 50 gems on an exact 32 px pitch, so an entry is only ever a cell of it. They go to `Assets/Orbs/` rather than `Assets/Gear/` -- an orb is not gear and `OrbTable` loads it by its own `ROOT`. The picks are made for distinctness across the tray as much as for the colours Path of Exile trained the idea into: the eight stand side by side in one row, so no two may read as the same stone at a glance. The one thing `gear()` does not have to care about is that `orbs()` centres the trimmed art on an **even** offset -- `OrbSlot` draws at half size, and a 2:1 step keeps whichever column is even, so an odd offset shifts the art into the other phase and loses a column it did not have to. `tools/qa/ui_kit_orbs.png` shows every orb twice, at source size and at the 16 px the tray draws it, because the halving is the only thing about these icons that can go wrong.

The same file also cuts the **two marks the corner's square buttons wear** (`ICONS`, `icons()`) off the same `Icons.png` the ring and the amulet come from -- a chest (row 1, column 3) and a star (row 2, column 2), each its own measured extent on that 6-column, unevenly-spaced grid. Two things are decided there. They are cut at **scale 1**, unlike the gear: these stand on theme art, which is drawn at one source pixel per panel pixel, and doubling them would put a second pitch on the same button. And they are **recoloured** through `BONE_RAMP` -- `Icons.png` is drawn in the wood panel's own brown, because the pack means these marks to be engraved on wood (which is what `ui_doll` is), and a brown chest on a brown face barely reads; mapped onto the pack's cream they do. Both are centred on one `ICON_SIDE` square, because a Button takes its minimum size from its icon and two buttons wearing raw 12x11 and 13x12 marks would be two different sizes side by side. The face behind them is `ui_btn_brown_*`, the green button's **flat, tileable** rectangle in a brown key (`BROWN_HUE/SAT/DIM`). The pack does draw its own brown square button, at `Buttons.png` (336, 339) in four states on a 16 px pitch, and it is **not cut**: its face is a diagonal gradient, so no margin leaves a centre `check()` will pass, and at 11x12 it could not hold a 12 px mark anyway. What is taken from it is its colour -- played on the green face, the base step lands on #714c2a against the pack's own #70492a. `tools/qa/ui_kit_icons.png` is both marks on that face in all four states, on grass, which is where they stand.
The same file also cuts the **skill icons** (`SKILLS`, `skills()`) off `Assets/Potential/Ability Icons/`, whole 16 px files that carry their own frame: one colourway a tree -- red for Power, the gold-orange for Fortune -- plus each colourway's Locked mark, to `Assets/Skills/<skill id>.png`. `tools/qa/ui_kit_skills.png` draws both trees fresh and part-spent at the 2x they are shown at.
The **loot beam** is cut the same way and for the same reason, by `tools/loot_beam.py`: the "Mini Falem" effect out of the bought pack under `Assets/Potential/Effects`, which ships as loose 16 px frames in nine colourways, written out as one fifteen-frame strip at `Assets/Effects/loot_beam.png`. Only the white colourway is taken -- see `LootBeam` for why one sheet covers every rarity -- and it never enters `ui_sheet.png`, being an effect rather than a theme sprite.

The same file also cuts the **gear icons** (`GEAR`, `gear()`), because it is where it is written down which rectangle of which bought sheet is which sprite and a second script saying that a second way is how the two drift apart. They are not theme sprites: they go to `Assets/Gear/` as loose 32×32 PNGs for `LootTable.ROOT` to load by path, never into `ui_sheet.png` or `ui_sheet.json`, and they skip `check()` for the reason the close button does — an icon is drawn, never stretched, so it has no nine-slice to hold flat. `PARTS` cuts the equipment page's own furniture the same way — the pack's silhouette and its two empty-socket marks — loose into `Assets/UI/`. The preview is `tools/qa/ui_kit_gear.png`, every gear icon the game has on the bag's tan socket, because the one thing that can go wrong is whether the new ones look pasted in beside the old. Ring and amulet come off `Icons.png` (a 6-column grid on a 16 px pitch, rows *not* evenly spaced — each entry's y and height are that one icon's measured extent), doubled to land near the size of the RPG pack's 28 px art, which doubles their pixel too; that is why only the two pieces no pack draws are taken from it, and the torch is the RPG pack's own.

`Main_tiles.png` is laid out as a family: four colourways (a brown or green header over a cream or a brown body) by three frame weights, plus header-less bodies, a big cream panel, scrollbar rails and thumbs, close buttons and slot tiles. The game takes the header-less brown body, the big cream panel, and the green title bar **cut off its panel rather than taken with it** — the pack draws bar and body as one sprite, which fixes the bar at 13 px, and Pixellari needs 16 to stay legible. Cut on its own the bar is a nine-slice like any other and grows to whatever the title needs, with the body panel stacked under it. That is what `main_scene._titled_panel` builds, and `_body_of` hands back the VBox to fill.

The close button is the pack's own drawn X, not the letter typed on a green button: one 9×10 icon the pack uses on every panel size, tinted to the surface it sits on, so the green one is cut for the green bar. The pack draws it once, so the other three states come from the pack's own rules — pressed is the face a pixel lower with its drop shadow gone, which is how the pack presses every button it has, and hover lifts the frame's lightness by the step measured off the pack's green face (#50a978 → #68c97e, the same lift that takes its shade #478773 to #50a978).


## Rules and gotchas
- **A nine-slice margin is only correct if what it leaves over is flat:** a `StyleBoxTexture` repeats the centre and the four edge cells, so every row the top and bottom bands tile horizontally, and every column the side bands tile vertically, has to be one colour or the seams show. `tools/ui_kit.py check()` re-reads each sprite and refuses to export one that is not — which is how the button margins were found (bottom 3 looked right and put one pixel of the corner bevel into a tiled column).
