## Sprites cut from bought packs (`tools/ui_kit.py`)
The interface is **cut, not generated**, from packs under `Assets/Potential/`. `tools/ui_kit.py` is the one place that says which rectangle of which pack sheet is which sprite and where its nine-slice margins fall; Python with Pillow and numpy, no Godot. The one thing it assembles that is mostly *drawn* is the gear: see the item bases' row. **Read `DESIGN.md` here before adding or re-cutting a sprite** -- it holds the pack layouts, the measured grids and what was tried and turned down.

Two things are generated rather than cut, because no pack draws them: the nameplate's health bar (`AI-sprites-generator/hpbar.py`, `build_hpbar.py`), and most of the gear icons (`AI-sprites-generator/gearlib.py`, `gear.py`), which `ui_kit.py` imports and writes itself.

`tools/cave_dungeon.py` cuts the dungeon's art, all of it Admurin's: the eight creatures of `Enemy_Galore_I` (each animation a grid of 64 px cells, laid out as the one row `EnemyRoster` wants, empty trailing cells dropped), Gollux from `Assets/Bosses/` (already strips, but every one except his idle leaves an empty 128 px cell either side of him: only the painted cells are kept), every creature frame given the hero's outline (`INK`, one pixel outside, `outlined`; it stops at a cell's edge where the art reaches it) and the cave parallax's seven layers, 1 (nearest) to 7, copied whole -- **not the pack's `0.png`, which is a preview of all seven put together** and hid the rest when it was drawn as a layer. No arguments writes `tools/qa/cave_dungeon.png` (a common, an elite and the boss in the cave at the fight's proportions, behind the nearest rock as the fight draws them; `GROUND` is `CombatScene.CAVE_GROUND`) and prints each creature's `frame` and `bounds` for the roster; `--export` writes `Assets/Enemies/<name>/<animation>.png` and `Assets/Area/cave/<n>.png`.

`tools/pips.py` **draws** the pips (the user's design, 2026-10-03, from their sketch): `SHAPES` as letter rows -- the round pip off a shaded mask (`shaded`), the user's skull for an elite, the skull under a gold crown with the Scowl's brow for a boss -- in `RAMPS` (common brown, bone, the crown's gold, and `spent`: dark greys one step above the ink, so a dead skull keeps its face), every sprite `WIDTH` (9) across and the round one centred in it, `MORE`, the caret in the rabble's brown that stands last in a bar longer than ten (`bar` previews `KillPips`' window), and `GIFT`, the box under a red bow a board's row ends in (`board_row`). `tools/npc_portraits.py` cuts the dialogue portraits out of `2D enemies/NPC Pack 2D Pixel Art/Sprites/`: `PORTRAITS` is speaker -> (sheet, frame width, crop), one crop for every frame of the idle since the speaker stands still, written as one strip of frames stacked top to bottom to `Assets/NPC/<speaker>.png` for `DialogueBox`; every crop is `HEIGHT` (52, `DialogueBox.PORTRAIT_HEIGHT`) tall and as wide as the speaker needs. The fortuneteller (the pack's enchanter) and `player`, the kobold's own `Assets/Player/idle.png` (the user picked the 52 px crop, head to the curl of his tail, over a tighter one), and `blacksmith`, 68 wide to keep his anvil (the user's ask), cut at the anvil's top so he is no taller than the rest; the pack's alchemist is there for more.

`tools/seeing_stone.py` draws the Seeing Stone -- **unused since 2026-10-03**, when `CaveSense` replaced the stone, and nothing loads its files -- a stand-in until the user's pixellab stone: the orb on its stand and, apart, the light inside it (white, stepped alpha) that `main_scene` tints by `FortuneTeller.warmth`, so five temperatures are one sprite and a tint. `--export` writes `Assets/UI/seeing_stone.png`, `seeing_stone_glow.png` and her badge `Assets/Fortune/stone.png` (the stone lit warm on `ui_kit`'s skill frame in the "teller" purple, so her hover glow rims it like her other spells instead of filling a clear square).

`tools/sound_levels.py` first cuts the game's sound effects out of the user's picks in `Sounds/Selected/` into `Sounds/Sfx/` (`CUTS`: out path -> pick, start, end, read off each pick's waveform -- from its onset, not the silence before it, several takes in one file split into `_1`, `_2`...; `LOOPS`: the beam's hum as ten whole pulses with its start crossfaded into what follows its end, looped by its `.import`), Vorbis at quality 8 (at 5 a quiet, noisy pick drifted past the tolerance on every re-level); a cut is rendered from its pick at its level in one encode (`make`: each try's gain is the last's plus however far it measured off, so a spiky clink reaches its level by having its peak limited -- raising a file in place stopped at its peak, and the drops stayed quiet), and again whenever it is off its level; a cut whose window changed has to be deleted. A cut may carry a filter run first: the jewel drop's gate, without which its level raised the pick's hiss to 11 dB under the clink. Then it sets the mix: no `AudioStreamPlayer` in the game sets a volume (a beam's hum fades in and out, ending at the file's level), so each file's own level is how loud it is beside the others (the whole SFX bus then sits `Settings.SFX_DB` under them), and `LEVELS` (path -> target) is where it is tuned. Music by integrated LUFS (-25, down from -20 at the user's ask, 2026-10-02), everything else by its loudest 400 ms (swing -18, punch and death -16, coins -19, footsteps -22, clicks and page flip -23; the drops -14 and a unique's -10, raised, and a coin -25 and an experience pip -27, lowered, the same day). It rewrites the files in place, same codec, rate and bitrate; a file within 0.5 dB is left alone, and a spike already at the -1 dBTP ceiling is not raised again (click1 and footstep05 stop short of theirs). A sound added to the game gets a line in `LEVELS`. Needs ffmpeg on the path; skips a file another program holds open.

`tools/pixel_flatten.py` is the other tool here that touches enemies: it flattens an AI-generated creature's frames to the bought packs' look (one median-cut palette across every frame, lightness stretched to the packs' range, despeckled). Preview only into `tools/qa/pixel_flatten.png`; `--export` overwrites the sprites.

## Rules and gotchas
- **A nine-slice margin is only correct if what it leaves over is flat:** the centre and edge cells tile, so every tiled row and column must be one colour. `check()` re-reads each theme sprite and refuses to export one that is not. Icons and parts skip `check()`: they are drawn, never stretched.
- **Only theme sprites go in `ui_sheet.png` / `ui_sheet.json`.** Pips, gear, orbs, marks, skills, character parts, health-bar parts and the beam are loose files; `test_ui_theme` counts the sheet exactly.
- **Everything written to `Assets/UI/` is in ENDESGA 64** (the user's call, 2026-09-30): `_e64` puts each sprite through `hexlib.to_e64` last, each colour to its nearest in OKLab and shades kept apart, with the pack's chrome pinned by hand in `UI_FIXED` (cream f6ca9f, slot e69c69, wood and brown face 8a4836, green face 33984b, red face c42430, outline 1c121c) -- keys, marks, pips and the character frame included, the hero's portrait not. `item_frames.py` and `build_hpbar.py` do the same. Gear, orb, skill and spell icons are not interface and keep their own colours. The red title bar and the body under it (`ui_bar_red`, `ui_panel_headed_red`, `ui_notch_bar_red_a`) are the green ones played through `GREEN_TO_RED` after the remap.
- **Every button face is a palette swap of the pack's one green button** (brown, red, pale tan for a dead one -- grey until 2026-09-30), mapped by hue and saturation with each step's lightness kept -- except `ui_btn_light_go_*`, the green itself unrecoloured, cut for the cream surface only (`GO_SURFACES`). The pack's own brown square button is not cut: its gradient face cannot tile.
- **The title bar is cut off its panel,** because the pack fixes it at 13 px and Pixellari needs 16; `UITheme.titled_panel` stacks bar over body.
- **A mark on a brown face is recoloured through `BONE_RAMP`; one bare on the cream keeps the pack's brown (`_brown`), and an open tab is `_green`.** Marks that stand on theme art are cut at scale 1, and every one is centred on one `ICON_SIDE` square so the corner buttons come out the same size whatever they wear. A mark no pack draws is written into `ICONS_DRAWN` in `ICON_KEY`'s five shades (outline plus a dark-to-light ramp) and goes through the same recolour, so a drawn one cannot be told from a cut one.
- **`Icons.png` rows are not evenly spaced:** each entry's y and height are that icon's measured extent.
- **Every gear icon ends in one outline (`_outlined`):** every opaque pixel that touches clear is ink -- the sprite's own darkest hue, lightness at most `OUTLINE_INK` (0.16) -- one pixel thick, standing where the pack's white border stood, or added round a piece that came without one. `base_gear()` refuses an icon with under `OUTLINE_FLOOR` (0.97) of its edge in ink, and `qa.py gear` counts the same over all of them.
- **Every base and unique icon ends in the game's palette (`_paletted`, after the outline, before the checks):** each colour is lifted (`PALETTE_LIFT`, lightness to the power 0.8: the pieces sat too dark on the bag and the doll) and goes to its nearest `hexlib.PALETTE` colour in OKLab with lightness weighted `PALETTE_LIGHT_WEIGHT` over hue, so shading keeps its values; neighbours more than `PALETTE_KEEP_APART` apart in lightness are never merged; every edge pixel is the palette's `ink`; alpha is never touched, so no shape moves. The palette has no dark purple, so violet pieces come out maroon or navy -- append a ramp to `hexlib.PALETTE` (never insert) if that ever matters. A torch's glow is drawn after and stays apricot.
- **No doubled art in a base:** `_doubled` (every 2x2 block one colour) stops the build. It is what the Raven pack and `Icons.png` were turned down for.
- **The build never reads what it writes.** Every source is under `Assets/Potential/` or drawn, so `python tools/ui_kit.py` twice leaves every file under `Assets/` byte for byte the same; a source read back out of `Assets/Gear` would be outlined again on each run.
- **No recolour may pass `GREY_CEILING` (0.88),** so a lifted recolour (glass, bleached bone) does not burn out to paper white. Both halves of `_shift` clamp to it, and the grey tuple's optional **third** number is a lightness multiplier -- without it an iron piece and a steel one come out the same value wherever a pack drew its metal colourless, which is most of the armour in them.
- **Orbs are centred on an even offset:** `OrbSlot` draws them at half size, and an odd offset loses a column.
- **Only the white colourway of the loot beam is cut:** `modulate` multiplies, so white times a rarity colour is exactly that colour.
- **Writing a pixellab prompt for an icon:** read `DESIGN.md`'s *Pixellab prompts* section first, and write the prompt in `tools/pixellab_prompts.py` (the source of every base prompt; it writes `tools/qa/pixellab_item_prompts.md`). The short of it: never an item's name, a comparison, the word "outline", black, a body word in a helmet, or a colour word that names a thing ("bone white" drew bones; light grey and neutral grey are the charts' only light colours that name nothing); colour words only from the user's colour charts; one kind at a time from a piece the user picks, tied to its tier's measured colours; judge on the real doll; change one line between tries.
- **After cutting, run the Godot import** (`--headless --path . --editor --quit`) and commit the `.import` files.
