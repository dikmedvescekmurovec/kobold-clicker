## Combat code (`Scenes/Combat/`, `Scenes/Enemies/`)
A tile has to be taken before it can be charted: a lineup of enemies against a clock, both of them the tile's own. What the world put there says which fight it is (`Encounter.PROFILES`, keyed by `MapBuilder.area_variant`): open land and roads field ten in thirty seconds ending on an elite, and a **settlement of any tier is a set piece** -- fifteen in a minute, an elite every fifth and a **boss** last, which is the only place a boss is ever rolled. A farm run on a settlement keeps that elite rhythm and never its boss. How hard they hit back grows with the walk -- see `level_of` and `HP_GROWTH` above. A click takes off what the player's gear is worth, crits at its crit chance for its crit damage on top, and the weapon swings itself `attack_speed` times a second beside that. Winning calls `MapBuilder.chart` exactly as pressing Chart used to; losing changes nothing and can be retried at once.

A tile already taken can be **farmed** instead (`MapBuilder.can_farm`, the Farm button beside Chart and Move here): the same arena and the same terrain's enemies, coming forever, with no clock and nothing riding on it. Nothing is won but what the bodies were carrying, and the only way out is the Terminate button, up in the top-right corner where a frantic click cannot reach it. Every body also rolls for an **orb**, on its own generator and its own curve, **beside** the gear rather than against it -- so one body can hand over both, and the two rates are independent numbers rather than one number split in two. An orb is thrown out of the body exactly as a find is (`_show_find`, which both go through), and plain: it has no rarity, and a glow borrowed off that ramp would say it had one. It never enters `_drops`, so the loot counter, `DropsView`, the discard flow, `bag_room` and the autodiscard filter have nothing to say about it, and `_orb_label` says `+n orbs` under the verdict beside the gold. Every body also hands over a **purse**, which is not rolled for: gold falls off every body where gear falls off about one, and the fight says once under the verdict what it earned. Both fights carry a **loot counter** in the bottom-right corner wearing the newest find and the count, which opens the run's whole haul, and everything a body drops -- its purse, its gear and its orbs -- **falls out of it into the arena** and lies there before it fades, which is the one thing said while the fight is still on; a run has no verdict to wait for, so the counter and its list are where a find is then read properly. Opening a find in either list gives a Discard button, which is how a run that has turned up more than the bag will hold is sorted out before it banks. How much room is left is said by the **counter's own face**, which ramps from the pack's green to its red as the bag fills, and the sentence explaining it stands inside the counter's panel rather than out in the arena -- a colour is read out of the corner of an eye without taking any room, and the words belong where the player has already asked what the run is carrying, which is also the one place they can do something about it. A level the player has ruled out never appears at all -- not in the counter, never thrown into the arena, not in either list -- and the number thrown away is said once, under the verdict.

| `encounter.gd` (`Encounter`) | The rules, with no nodes: `for_tile(cell, env, variant)` rolls the tile's lineup, seeded from the cell so a tile always fields the same fight, and keeps `env` -- the terrain picked the enemies and it picks the backdrop. `PROFILES` is what the `variant` buys: one table saying, per thing the world can put on a tile, how many enemies, how long a clock, how often an elite and whether the last one is a **boss**. Open land and roads are `ORDINARY` (the old ten in thirty, ending on an elite), and village, town and fortress share one `SETTLEMENT` entry -- fifteen in sixty, an elite every fifth, a boss last -- because what makes a town a longer fight is that people live there, not how many of them. A fight built and told nothing is the ordinary one, which is what every `Encounter` made by hand gets. `farm(cell, env, variant)` is the same fight `endless`: one enemy to start, and `_append_enemy` puts the next on the end of the lineup the moment the last one falls, so it is never finished; it takes the tile's `elite_every` and never its boss -- a boss is what a set piece ends on, and a run does not end. `_append_enemy` is the one place that knows how an enemy joins a fight, which is what keeps a tile's lineup and a run's thousandth enemy built alike -- both from `tier_for(position)`, the fight's own rule, which is also why `on_elite()` asks `EnemyRoster.tier_of` rather than counting to the end. `stop()` ends a run at the player's word (a win, because a run is not a thing that can be lost) and `kills()` is the score it shows instead of a countdown. A settlement's last enemy is the only boss the game rolls. `always_drop` makes every body carry something -- nothing in the game turns it on, it is what lets the tests and the screenshot scripts show a pouch with three finds in it without grinding out the hundred-odd kills a 3% chance takes. `arm(stats)` reads `Equipment.totals()` once as the fight opens — the bag is shut while a fight is on, so there is nothing to keep live — and keeps `drop_rate` beside the four combat numbers, which is handed to `LootTable.roll` on every death, and a fight nobody arms does `BARE_DAMAGE` a click with nothing swinging, which is the game this was before gear meant anything. `hit()` is one blow, `advance(delta)` runs the clock and the weapon's own swings, `won`/`lost` end it. Every death also emits `gold_dropped` and adds to `gold`, the fight's running purse -- `gold_of` is `base_gold(cell)` times the body's own `hp_modifier`, so a purse is worth exactly what the body was worth to kill and nobody keeps a second table in step with the first. `base_gold` grows with the walk **both ways at once**, `(BASE_GOLD + GOLD_PER_STEP * steps) * GOLD_GROWTH ^ steps`, off the same smooth distance the health does rather than the banded `level_of` -- so an ordinary common body in the middle of the map is worth exactly one gold, which is where the whole curve is pinned. `GOLD_GROWTH` is set near `LootTable.LEVEL_GROWTH` rather than near `HP_GROWTH`, so a purse keeps pace with the gear that has to kill for it rather than with the health it has to get through. `hit_landed(amount, crit, automatic)` is what the view draws numbers from. Crit damage is what a crit *adds*: 50 is half again, and `arm` clamps crit chance to `CRIT_CAP` so a pile of gear -- or a piece from a save written before chances stopped being multiplied -- can never make every hit a crit. An automatic swing only earns while an enemy is standing there, so nothing banks through a walk-in. Health is `hp_modifier` over a base that grows **exponentially** with the distance from cell (0, 0) -- `BASE_HP * HP_GROWTH ^ steps` -- so the frontier pulls away from whatever the player is carrying and farming is how they catch up. `BASE_HP` is tuned so the first ring is what it always was, and `HP_GROWTH` is the dial for how fast the map gets hard |
| `drops_view.gd` (`DropsView`) | What a fight has turned up, as a grid of `ItemSlot` squares one of which can be opened into an `ItemDetails` block. The verdict panel and the loot popup are both one of these rather than the same layout written twice; the grid and the details swap rather than stack, the way `_bag_scroll` and `_bag_detail` do on the item panel. `resized_contents` is how an owner that centres it knows to measure again. With `discardable` set, an open drop carries a Discard button over its Back — the view never touches its own list, it emits `discarded(item)` and the owner takes it out and fills again, so there is one place that knows what the run is carrying |
| `combat_actor.gd` (`CombatActor`) | An `AnimatedSprite2D` built from a sheet at runtime, as `PlayerToken` is. Serves both sides. Scaled and stood up by its *idle* frames, so `position` is the middle of its feet whatever the attack or death frames sprawl into |
| `combat_scene.tscn` / `combat_scene.gd` (`CombatScene`) | The view: backdrop, the two fighters, the HUD, the clicks, the verdict. **Centred on the bottom edge**, the nameplate: the enemy's name and a `HealthBar`, standing on the arena with **no panel behind them** exactly as the place's name and the clock do on the top edge and for the same reason — so the name is a `_hud_label` in bone with its dark outline, and the bar brings its own border. The bar wears that enemy's tier as its frame — `Encounter.tier_in`, the same call the pips read, so the two can never disagree about what is standing there. It is parked on an edge rather than floating over the enemy's head because health is a thing the player watches continuously and a panel that walks in with the enemy and slides about under it is the worst place to read a number off; it also stops the damage numbers and the nameplate competing for the air over the body. The bottom middle specifically, so that it sits directly under the pip bar and the clock and the whole of how the fight is going reads down one column. The column simply grows to hold the wider elite and boss bars, and `_place_corners` **centres** it every frame and has no opinion about how wide it is — growing it evenly either side leaves the channel where it was, which is the same thing `HealthBar.TROUGH` buys by growing its ornament outward. The top of the HUD says where the fight is and how it is going: the tile's name and its level (`place`, handed over by the main scene from `MapBuilder.name_of` -- two labels, a name in bone and a number in gold, rather than one line with a punctuation glyph Pixellari may not draw; it cannot change mid-fight, so it is filled once and never refreshed), then the pip bar, then the clock. How far through the fight the player is is said by the `KillPips` bar under it and **nowhere else** — there is no live count of enemies killed or left, because a number saying what the bar says is the one thing a player mid-fight has no time to read (the verdict's own `%d slain` stays: that is a finished run's score). The clock and the bar stand **centred on the top edge with no panel behind them** — they are what the player watches, and a panel only puts furniture round them — so each carries its own dark border instead (`BAR_BORDER`, and `LABEL_OUTLINE` on every word up there, through `_hud_label`), which is what has to read against a backdrop rather than against wood; `_place_corners` centres the column every frame, for the same `ui_scale` reason it places everything else. The clock bar is cut to `KillPips.WIDTH` less its border so the two are one column to the pixel. The pip bar is **above** the clock — the bar is the fight and the clock is what the fight is measured against, so the measure sits under the thing it measures — and inside the clock the number sits under its own bar, keeping the two bars adjacent. `_clock_color` ramps the bar **and the number together** from green through `Palette.GOLD` to the damage red as the clock runs out (`CLOCK_GREEN`, `CLOCK_AMBER`, as shares of the whole so retuning `Encounter.SECONDS` retunes them): a ramp rather than a switch at some number of seconds, because the colour is read out of the corner of an eye and an ambering bar says hurry a good deal earlier than one that turns red all at once. The HUD's two corner buttons -- the loot counter and Terminate -- are `_square_button`s: the map corner's `BrownIconButton` with a cream mark (`SACK_ICON`, `FLAG_ICON`, drawn in `tools/ui_kit.py` `ICONS_DRAWN`) and no word, so every icon button in the game is the same one. The corner counter (`_loot_button`) writes its count beside the sack in cream, and opens `_loot_panel`; it stands in the **bottom-right** corner, out of the way of the nameplate that has the middle of that edge. Its **face** is how full the bag is: `_tint_loot_button` ramps a tint over the brown button art from `FACE_BROWN` through `Palette.GOLD` to `FACE_DANGER` (the pack's own red, the key its danger button is played in) as `bag_room` runs out, a ramp rather than a step for the same reason `_clock_color` ramps. The tint goes on **copies of the theme's own styleboxes**, one per `UITheme.STATES`, rather than on the node — `modulate` on the Button would take the find's icon and the count with it, and what has to change colour is the frame behind them. A hit floats its number off the enemy at twice Pixellari's native size, and a crit at three times in the damage red with an exclamation mark, so which kind of blow landed is read without reading the number. `_show_find` throws each find and each orb out of the body instead of announcing it in the corner, and `_finds_shown` counts them, which is all anything downstream ever asks. `_place_corners` puts the counter and Terminate where they go every frame rather than anchoring them, because both are scaled by `ui_scale` and an anchor knows nothing about that. Endlessly the clock is hidden and the tally counts up. A `CanvasLayer` the main scene adds in front of the map rather than a scene change — the map holds a whole generated world in memory that a scene change would throw away, so it is hidden and its `process_mode` disabled instead. Drawn height comes from `SIZE_HEIGHT`, so a slime is knee-high and a boss looms. The backdrop is `Assets/Area/<env>_<variant>_<n>.png`, the environment from the encounter, the variant from `MapBuilder.area_variant`, and `n` from `layout_for(cell)` — every place is drawn `AREA_LAYOUTS` (4) ways, and the pick is seeded from the cell under its own `"backdrop"` tag, so a tile always fights on the same one however often it is attempted but which picture it is has nothing to do with who walks in. `backdrop_for` clamps a layout past the end and falls back to `grass_plain_1` rather than leaving a fight with no picture behind it. It is also where the player's autodiscard rule is applied: `_on_loot_dropped` is the only listener to `Encounter.loot_dropped` and forks into `loot_kept` and `loot_discarded`, which is what the rest of the game hears. A find at a ruled-out level is counted in `_auto_discarded` and nothing else — no square, no counter, nothing thrown — and the count is said once under the verdict. `gold_gained` is a body's purse passed on, for the reason `loot_kept` is. `_throw` is the one arc everything a body drops travels on -- across at a steady rate while the height goes up and comes back down, then lying where it fell before it fades and frees itself -- from `_drop_origin`, the enemy itself (a drop lands the frame it dies, so the body is still standing there). `_show_coins` throws a purse as `Coins.count_for` coins, so a richer body visibly pays more without the arena filling up; `_show_find` throws one find or orb, and above common stands a `LootBeam` over it in the rarity's **ring** colour (`BEAM_SCALE`, `BEAM_ALPHA`, `BEAM_LIFT`, which sets its foot on the piece rather than leaving it floating over one) -- the ring half of the ramp rather than the text half, because a find is thrown against a snowfield or a noon desert and that is what that half was picked for. It is the second hand-rolled tween in the file beside `_show_damage`, there being no effect helper, and it is what says something was found or earned while the fight is still on. `_gold_label` still says the total once under the verdict, behind a coin rather than the word and in the panel's own colour rather than `Palette.GOLD` -- that hex is the unique item step, and spending it here would put one colour on a currency and on an item's name in the same panel. `bag_room` is how much room the bag has left (`-1` when nobody has said), which is what the counter's face is tinted from and what puts `_warning` up inside `_loot_panel` — a line over the list rather than a panel of its own, flipped through `_show_warning` because it changes the popup's height and the popup is centred on what it holds; `drop_discarded` is a find the player threw away by hand from either list |

## Rules and gotchas
- **An endless run has no clock and no loss:** `Encounter.advance` does not touch `time_left` while `endless`, so the only two ways a run finishes are `stop()` and `give_up()`. Anything reading `time_left`, `remaining()` or `enemies` to decide something has to ask `endless` first -- the lineup grows, so `lineup.size()` is the only honest end of it. The one thing a run does keep off its tile is `elite_every`, which is the whole of its rhythm.
- **Loot belongs to the attempt, not the tile:** a tile's enemies are seeded from its cell, so the same tile always fields the same fight, but `Encounter.loot_rng` is left unseeded (a `RandomNumberGenerator` seeds itself, so there is no `randomize()` to add) and re-fighting a tile can turn up something else. Tests set the seed themselves.
