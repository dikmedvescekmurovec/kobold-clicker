## Combat code (`Scenes/Combat/`, `Scenes/Enemies/`)
A tile has to be taken before it can be charted: a lineup of enemies against a clock, both of them the tile's own. What the world put there says which fight it is (`Encounter.PROFILES`, keyed by `MapBuilder.area_variant`): open land and roads field ten in thirty seconds ending on an elite, and a **settlement of any tier is a set piece** -- fifteen in a minute, an elite every fifth and a **boss** last, which is the only place a boss is ever rolled. A farm run on a settlement keeps that elite rhythm and never its boss. How hard they hit back grows with the walk -- see `level_of` and `HP_GROWTH` above. A click takes off what the player's gear is worth, crits at its crit chance for its crit damage on top, and the weapon swings itself `attack_speed` times a second beside that. Winning calls `MapBuilder.chart` exactly as pressing Chart used to; losing changes nothing and can be retried at once.

A tile already taken can be **farmed** instead (`MapBuilder.can_farm`, the Farm button beside Chart and Move here): the same arena and the same terrain's enemies, coming forever, with no clock and nothing riding on it. Nothing is won but what the bodies were carrying, and the only way out is the Terminate button, up in the top-right corner where a frantic click cannot reach it. Every body also rolls for an **orb**, on its own generator and its own curve, **beside** the gear rather than against it -- so one body can hand over both, and the two rates are independent numbers rather than one number split in two. An orb is thrown out of the body exactly as a find is (`_show_find`, which both go through), and plain: it has no rarity, and a glow borrowed off that ramp would say it had one. It never enters `_drops`, so the loot counter, `DropsView`, the discard flow, `bag_room` and the autodiscard filter have nothing to say about it, and the verdict draws what was found as a row of `OrbSlot`s under the finds, on the same light panel the bag's tray stands on (`DropsView.fill(items, orbs)`; it was once a `+n orbs` line, which said how many and never which). Every body also hands over a **purse**, which is not rolled for: gold falls off every body where gear falls off about one, and the fight says once under the verdict what it earned. Both fights carry a **loot counter** in the bottom-right corner wearing a sack and the count, which opens the run's whole haul, and everything a body drops -- its purse, its gear and its orbs -- **falls out of it into the arena** and lies there before it fades, which is the one thing said while the fight is still on; a run has no verdict to wait for, so the counter and its list are where a find is then read properly. Opening a find in either list gives a Discard button, which is how a run that has turned up more than the bag will hold is sorted out before it banks. How much room is left is said by the **counter's own face**, which ramps from the pack's green to its red as the bag fills, and the sentence explaining it stands inside the counter's panel rather than out in the arena -- a colour is read out of the corner of an eye without taking any room, and the words belong where the player has already asked what the run is carrying, which is also the one place they can do something about it. A level the player has ruled out never appears at all -- not in the counter, never thrown into the arena, not in either list -- and the number thrown away is said once, under the verdict.

**Camping is that same run with nobody clicking, and it is deliberately not a second economy.** The obvious way to pay for hours the player is not there is to invent a rate -- gold an hour, experience an hour, a dial or two to tune against `TownPrices`. That is a whole economy to keep in step with the one the game already has, and it drifts the first time `HP_GROWTH` or a weapon's attack speed moves. So a camp is the tile's own `Encounter.farm`, armed off the player's gear and advanced with `advance()` alone: only the weapon's own swings land, because nobody is there to click. That run is played out for a couple of minutes (`Camp.rates`) and what it earned a second is what the camp pays a second. Every dial that moves what a tile is worth moves a camp with it, and there is no number in `Camp` to keep in step with anything.

Sampling rather than playing out the whole night is the one corner cut, and it buys two things beside speed: the arrays an endless run grows never reach a night's worth of bodies, and -- because the rates are written down when camp is made -- **what a shut window pays and what an open one pays are the same sum**. `earned()` is rate times the seconds since, so there is nothing accruing in memory, nothing to bank on the way out, and a game killed at a camp needs no exit handling at all. The price is that a night is an average night and never its own lucky roll of the dice, which is the right trade for hours nobody watched.

**What a camp does not pay is the whole of what makes it a second-best way to play:** no gear, no orbs, no bodies for a bounty board and nothing towards the kills that unlock the first orb and the first unique. Those are for going in with a sword in your hand. Gold and experience are what a camp is for -- the two things that pile up and are spent elsewhere -- so a night away closes some of the gap to the frontier without ever handing over the thing the player actually plays for, which is a piece of gear coming off a body. It also means the rate needs no tax of its own: the missing loot *is* the tax, and one to one is the honest rate for the hours. If it ever plays too rich, the dial to add is a share applied in `Camp.earned` -- there is deliberately none there now, a constant of 1.0 multiplying nothing being exactly the kind of knob that gets tuned by feel.

`MAX_SECONDS` (12 h) is the promise the feature makes: a night pays a night, and a fortnight away pays a night as well. It exists so nobody is tempted to leave the game shut for a week, which would make the map's whole difficulty curve a matter of patience rather than of gear; the screen says so in as many words once the cap is what is holding the numbers still, because a number that looks short with no explanation reads as a bug.

**The screen is the fight's screen with nobody on it.** Camping happens on the same ground as farming and the player *is* there, so the way in is the way into a fight -- the map put away behind it, the corner buttons gone, the tile's own backdrop, dimmed to dusk. What is missing is the point: no hero on the left, no enemies walking in from the right, nothing to click, just a panel saying the hero is resting here and fighting off the occasional monster. One look says both where the player is and that nothing needs them. It is also why Escape does nothing there, as on the transcension's black screen: a stray press should not be able to end a night's rest, and the X and **Break camp** are the two ways out.

Crit damage is what a crit *adds*: 50 is half again, and `arm` clamps crit chance to `CRIT_CAP` so a pile of gear -- or a piece from a save written before chances stopped being multiplied -- can never make every hit a crit. An automatic swing only earns while an enemy is standing there, so nothing banks through a walk-in. Health is `hp_modifier` over a base that grows **exponentially** with the distance from cell (0, 0) and **steps once per ice wall behind the cell** -- `BASE_HP * HP_GROWTH ^ steps * WALL_GROWTH ^ walls_inside(cell)` -- so the frontier pulls away from whatever the player is carrying and farming is how they catch up. `BASE_HP` is tuned so the first ring is what it always was, and `HP_GROWTH` is the dial for how fast the map gets hard. The second term is why the land a fallen wall opens is a frontier again rather than a walkover: felling a wall proves some forty times the damage a second the land inside it asks for, and a whole band of ten rings only grows by `HP_GROWTH ^ 10`, about five. `walls_inside` counts none of a wall's own ring, so the step lands on the first ring past a wall and `for_wall` carries no growth term of its own -- the wall on ring 21 comes out `WALL_GROWTH` times the one on 11 because its ring's bodies already are. Gold does **not** step with it: a body past a wall is tougher for the purse it always carried, and so is the level it drops at -- the reasoning is in `Scenes/Map/DESIGN.md` |

The corner counter (`_loot_button`) writes its count beside the sack in cream, and opens `_loot_panel`; it stands in the **bottom-right** corner, out of the way of the nameplate that has the middle of that edge. Its **face** is how full the bag is: `_tint_loot_button` ramps a tint over the brown button art from `FACE_BROWN` through `Palette.GOLD` to `FACE_DANGER` (the pack's own red, the key its danger button is played in) as `bag_room` runs out, a ramp rather than a step for the same reason `_clock_color` ramps. The tint goes on **copies of the theme's own styleboxes**, one per `UITheme.STATES`, rather than on the node — `modulate` on the Button would take the find's icon and the count with it, and what has to change colour is the frame behind them. A hit floats its number off the enemy at twice Pixellari's native size, and a crit at three times in the damage red with an exclamation mark, so which kind of blow landed is read without reading the number. `_show_find` throws each find and each orb out of the body instead of announcing it in the corner, and `_finds_shown` counts them, which is all anything downstream ever asks. **The one exception is a unique the collection log has never held**, which the main scene stands on a banner under the HUD column (`_announce_unique`, and `hud_bottom` is what keeps it off the clock): the arena's own language says *something* dropped and says it in a tenth of a second, which is right for the hundredth blue ring and wrong for a piece that happens twice in a world, fills a slot in the log and changes how a fight is fought. It is raised outside `CombatScene` on purpose -- the fight has no idea what the log already holds, and the one place that does is the main scene, in the moment before the ledger writes the find in. It is up for five seconds whatever the player does, because a banner that a mashed click can take away before it is read is worse than none; after those five seconds it is the player's own clicking that decides, and a player who stopped to read is given an X rather than having it snatched. `_place_corners` puts the counter and Terminate where they go every frame rather than anchoring them, because both are scaled by `ui_scale` and an anchor knows nothing about that. Endlessly the clock is hidden and the tally counts up. A `CanvasLayer` the main scene adds in front of the map rather than a scene change — the map holds a whole generated world in memory that a scene change would throw away, so it is hidden and its `process_mode` disabled instead. Drawn height comes from `SIZE_HEIGHT`, so a slime is knee-high and a boss looms. The backdrop is `Assets/Area/<env>_<variant>_<n>.png`, the environment from the encounter, the variant from `MapBuilder.area_variant`, and `n` from `layout_for(cell)` — every place is drawn `AREA_LAYOUTS` (4) ways, and the pick is seeded from the cell under its own `"backdrop"` tag, so a tile always fights on the same one however often it is attempted but which picture it is has nothing to do with who walks in. `backdrop_for` clamps a layout past the end and falls back to `grass_plain_1` rather than leaving a fight with no picture behind it. It is also where the player's autodiscard rule is applied: `_on_loot_dropped` is the only listener to `Encounter.loot_dropped` and forks into `loot_kept` and `loot_discarded`, which is what the rest of the game hears. A find at a ruled-out level is counted in `_auto_discarded` and nothing else — no square, no counter, nothing thrown — and the count is said once under the verdict. `gold_gained` is a body's purse passed on, for the reason `loot_kept` is. `_throw` is the one arc everything a body drops travels on -- across at a steady rate while the height goes up and comes back down, then lying where it fell before it fades and frees itself -- from `_drop_origin`, the enemy itself (a drop lands the frame it dies, so the body is still standing there). `_show_coins` throws a purse as `Coins.count_for` coins, so a richer body visibly pays more without the arena filling up; `_show_find` throws one find or orb, and above common stands a `LootBeam` over it in the rarity's **ring** colour (`BEAM_SCALE`, `BEAM_ALPHA`, `BEAM_LIFT`, which sets its foot on the piece rather than leaving it floating over one) -- the ring half of the ramp rather than the text half, because a find is thrown against a snowfield or a noon desert and that is what that half was picked for. It is the second hand-rolled tween in the file beside `_show_damage`, there being no effect helper, and it is what says something was found or earned while the fight is still on. `_gold_label` still says the total once under the verdict, behind a coin rather than the word and in the panel's own colour rather than `Palette.GOLD` -- that hex is the unique item step, and spending it here would put one colour on a currency and on an item's name in the same panel. `bag_room` is how much room the bag has left (`-1` when nobody has said), which is what the counter's face is tinted from and what puts `_warning` up inside `_loot_panel` — a line over the list rather than a panel of its own, flipped through `_show_warning` because it changes the popup's height and the popup is centred on what it holds; `drop_discarded` is a find the player threw away by hand from either list |

`EnemyRoster` lives in `Scenes/Enemies/`, documented there.

**Game feel** (`CombatScene`, through `Juice`): every landed blow flashes and squashes the enemy (`_jolt_enemy`, springing back to `_enemy_scale`), a crit shakes `_arena` (the backdrop is drawn `BACKDROP_BLEED` oversize so no edge shows), a death bursts in `CombatActor.tint()` and freezes the game by tier (`STOP_*`, boss and elite also shake), and an elite-or-better find slows the fight so its beam is watched. Damage numbers pop in about their middle and drift. Coins rest `COIN_REST` then fly into the loot counter, which brightens (a scale bump would walk it off the corner `_place_corners` pins). `HealthBar` leaves a pale ghost of each blow that holds and drains.

**Bleed is the mace's whole argument, and it is the deepest wound rather than a sum of them.** A share of the blow that landed goes on coming off the body every second it is standing there, so a heavy, slow weapon keeps working between swings where a fast one does not. Adding each blow's wound to the last was the obvious reading and is the wrong one: a weapon swinging twice as fast would bleed twice as hard *and* hit twice as often, so the stat would be worth most to exactly the kind it is not for. A new blow therefore replaces the wound only when it is deeper, which makes what a mace is worth the size of one blow and not how many of them land -- a crit deepens it, an equal blow does not, and a smaller one leaves it alone. It belongs to the body it was opened in: cleared with `_struck` as the next one walks on, so a body that gets back up under Restless dead gets up whole. It and the Sunscorched Cowl's Heatstroke are the same thing off different numbers -- damage a second with nobody touching the enemy -- so they go through one `_wear_down`, which is `WAITING`-only (a body walking in or already going down takes none of it, the way an automatic swing lands on neither) and sets `_domino = false`, a death nobody dealt being no one's first blow. No clock is involved either way, so a farm run needs no special case, and there are no floating numbers for a bleed tick: the health bar moving is the whole of what says it.

**Drop rate is the broad finder, and where it meets a narrow one the two are added.** It lifts gear, uniques, orbs *and* the purse; item rarity lifts only the weights a piece of gear rolls its rarity on and gold find only the purse, which is why the narrow ones carry the bigger numbers on a piece (20% Gold Find against 5% Drop Rate). The two extra lines are in `_kill` and **added** to the narrow stat -- `gold_find + drop_rate`, `orb_find + drop_rate` -- the way `Equipment.totals` adds its global percents, so 20 and 30 are half again as much rather than 56% more, and so which of the two stats a player happens to be wearing cannot change the answer. Neither `gold_of` nor `OrbTable.chance_for` learned about a second stat: those two are what a *body* is worth, with no player in them, and `TownPrices` quotes every price off `gold_at_steps`. Teaching them the player's finders would have moved every price in every town.

**Chests.** `Encounter.for_tile(..., chest = true)` (from `MapBuilder.has_chest`) fields `MIMIC` alone under the `CHEST` profile: a HUGE boss on the ordinary clock. Its loot is a coin toss (`MIMIC_UNIQUE`, 0.5): one guaranteed unique off the ground's pool and nothing else, or `MIMIC_ROLLS` (4) certain pieces of gear rolled the ordinary way. It used to roll ten times with the first certain, about seven pieces a chest, which made a chest worth more than several settlements; the toss keeps a chest exciting while capping it at four. The unique half ignores `FIRST_UNIQUE_KILLS`, and the mimic takes no ordinary unique roll on top. The mimic has no environments, so no other lineup can draw it.

**A find rolls again.** A gear roll that beats the chance rolls once more at the same chance, and that one again, to `MOST_DROPS` (4) pieces off one body. The point is the moment rather than the rate: at a common's 3% it is worth 3% more gear and is all but never seen, while a boss -- or anything on a deep drop rate -- turns a find into two often enough that a drop stops being one square. A **certain** roll never cascades, because it beat nothing: the promised Broken Sword is one helping hand, a mimic pays exactly `MIMIC_ROLLS`, and `always_drop` still means one piece a body, which is what kept every test and screenshot that uses it exactly where it was.

The cap is not a taste decision. `LootTable.chance_for` clamps at 1.0 and a late drop rate reaches that ceiling on a boss, so an uncapped chain would roll for ever; the follow-ups are also never `guaranteed`, or `always_drop` alone would do the same. It lives in `Encounter._kill` rather than in `LootTable.roll` because a drop is still one roll for every other caller -- a vendor's shelf, the tests, the smith -- and only a body killed in a fight is meant to be generous.

## Rules and gotchas
- **An endless run has no clock and no loss:** `Encounter.advance` does not touch `time_left` while `endless`, so the only two ways a run finishes are `stop()` and `give_up()`. Anything reading `time_left`, `remaining()` or `enemies` to decide something has to ask `endless` first -- the lineup grows, so `lineup.size()` is the only honest end of it. The one thing a run does keep off its tile is `elite_every`, which is the whole of its rhythm.
- **Loot belongs to the attempt, not the tile:** a tile's enemies are seeded from its cell, so the same tile always fields the same fight, but `Encounter.loot_rng` is left unseeded (a `RandomNumberGenerator` seeds itself, so there is no `randomize()` to add) and re-fighting a tile can turn up something else. Tests set the seed themselves.

**The nameplate speaks in tiers too.** The name used to be bone for everyone with "(elite)" tacked on, while the pips and the bar's frame already said green and gold. Now the name takes the tier's colour, stands between two marks (a skull, a crown), and carries a title seeded from the cell and the slot -- a word in front of an elite ("Ancient Werewolf"), a line under a boss ("Scourge of the Meadows", the ground from `Encounter.TITLE_GROUND`). A boss's name is 32 px and its plate pops in white; an elite's does not, so the pop stays the boss's. Tried and turned down: tinting the skull green -- the pack's brown outline times green sank into every meadow, so it stays the bone it is cut in -- and a third, gothic font, which would have been one more face that is only clean at one size.

## The enemies strike the clock (2026-09-21)
Half of every piece's numbers did nothing: armour, health, block, dodge, energy shield, regen, leech, life on hit and the resistances were rolled and shown, and the one thing that could hurt the player was the clock. **The user's answer was to let the enemies strike the clock itself** rather than to give the player a second pool to lose -- the clock stays the only way to lose, and a blow is seconds off it. Everything below is the user's decision unless it says otherwise.

- **Armour takes a share, block a flat amount, and armour goes first.** The user's own figure is the test: a 100-second blow against 90% armour and 10 s of block costs nothing (`taken`, `_test_enemy_strikes`). The two answer different enemies -- a share is strongest against a big blow, a flat amount against many small ones -- which is what makes a shield and a greatsword's empty hand a real choice.
- **Both grow for ever, and so do the blows.** A blow is sized off `base_hp`, so it grows with the walk and steps with every wall exactly as a body's health does, and gear answers it through `LootTable.scale` exactly as it answers health. So defence has the same shape as damage: the player is ahead through a band and falls behind past a wall, and "monsters outgrow gear on purpose" holds for both halves of a fight. Armour is a *rating* turned into a flat share of every blow, `rating / (rating + ARMOUR_K)`, so that it can grow for ever without ever reaching the whole; the user asked for no caps, and the formula is why none is needed. **The share ignores the size of the blow** (the user's correction, the same day): it was first `rating / (rating + K * hit)`, Path of Exile's way, which made a plain set's 90% inside the first band fall to 7% past the second wall and left the reading of armour to depend on who was hitting. Now 50 armour is half of any blow and 450 is 90% (K was 100 for a few hours, then halved), and it is block, the flat amount, that big blows outgrow.
- **Dodge works the same as armour** (the user's words): a rating on the gear, the same flat formula, but the share is a chance to take none of a blow rather than a cut of every one. It was a percentage with a flat step before; now it grows like armour and the material's power applies to it.
- **The rhythm is the user's:** a common once a second, an elite every two, a boss every four (first built at twice that pace from a slip in the user's message, and corrected the same day). `HIT_TIER` (1, 2, 4) is mine: it makes every tier cost the same seconds a second against bare skin, so what differs between them is the *shape* of the blow -- and armour and block read shape. The first blow waits a whole interval, so a body killed quickly never lands one, and damage stays the first defence.
- **The ice wall does not strike** (the user's): it stays the damage check it was, and `balance_wall.gd`'s wall figures did not move. **A farm run and a camp are not struck either** (mine, unchallenged): a run has no clock to strike and "a run cannot be lost" was a rule worth keeping.
- **Time on hit wins back only what the blows took** (`wounds`) -- the user chose it over "any time, up to the full clock", which would have let a fast dagger stop the clock for good.
- **Everything else went** (the user's): health, energy shield, regen, leech and the resistances, and the four kinds built on them -- the hat, robe, slippers and Sapphire Amulet -- rather than finding them clock jobs of their own. The helmet and body are now armour (helm, plate) or dodge (hood, jerkin), and each slot kept its share of drops. Heartwood Plate reads armour where it read health, and the Bulwark answers a blow its block stops whole where it rolled a block chance.
- **Block and time on hit are kept in tenths of a second** (mine). A blow near the start is a quarter of a second, and a modifier rolls whole numbers; counted in whole seconds, the smallest "+1 Block" was a wall against every blow in the first band, and the first probe had a plain shield making the first band untouchable. Tenths keep the numbers whole and the lines honest ("+0.3s Block").
- **Strikes are a switch the main scene turns on** (`Encounter.strikes`), the bargain `uniques_after` already strikes: a fight nobody tells anything is the fight it always was, so the tests that time a clock to the second did not all need rewriting. The one line that matters is in `_open_fight`.
- **Where the dials sit, and what they were chosen against** (`HIT_SECONDS` 0.25, `ARMOUR_K` = `DODGE_K` = 50): a bare-handed first ring still won at eight clicks a second, and the first ring past the wall won in the farmed set of rares at four clicks a second with some 6 s to spare and **lost** with the set's armour, dodge and block taken off. Measured with a throwaway probe on the day, at the first, doubled rhythm (so the blows cost twice what they do now), over rings 1 to 20 in one seeded set per rarity: inside the first band a worn set of commons is struck for nothing and a stripped one already loses on ring 10 at four clicks a second; past the wall commons lose and rares need the clicks; by ring 20 damage runs out before defence does. With the flat share the same frontier fight struck the farmed set for 11 s and **time on hit won every second of it back** -- while blows come slower than a hit heals, time on hit cancels them outright; without armour the blows were 27 s and it could not keep up. Those are measurements, not settled design, and `balance_wall.gd` reports the seconds its played-out player lost to blows.
