## Combat code (`Scenes/Combat/`, `Scenes/Enemies/`)
A tile has to be taken before it can be charted: a lineup of enemies against a clock, both of them the tile's own. What the world put there says which fight it is (`Encounter.PROFILES`, keyed by `MapBuilder.area_variant`): open land and roads field ten in thirty seconds ending on an elite, and a **settlement of any tier is a set piece** -- fifteen in a minute, an elite every fifth and a **boss** last, which is the only place a boss is ever rolled. A farm run on a settlement keeps that elite rhythm and never its boss. How hard they hit back grows with the walk -- see `level_of` and `HP_GROWTH` above. A click takes off what the player's gear is worth, crits at its crit chance for its crit damage on top, and the weapon swings itself `attack_speed` times a second beside that. Winning calls `MapBuilder.chart` exactly as pressing Chart used to; losing changes nothing and can be retried at once.

A tile already taken can be **farmed** instead (`MapBuilder.can_farm`, the Farm button beside Chart and Move here): the same arena and the same terrain's enemies, coming forever, with no clock and nothing riding on it. Nothing is won but what the bodies were carrying, and the only way out is the Terminate button, up in the top-right corner where a frantic click cannot reach it. A tile fight wears the same button: there it gives the fight up, which is a loss like running out of time (what already dropped is kept, Retry is offered) and says "Given up" instead; Escape does not do it, since a tile fight is something that can be lost and one stray key should not lose it. Every body also rolls for an **orb**, on its own generator and its own curve, **beside** the gear rather than against it -- so one body can hand over both, and the two rates are independent numbers rather than one number split in two. An orb is thrown out of the body exactly as a find is (`_show_find`, which both go through), and plain: it has no rarity, and a glow borrowed off that ramp would say it had one. It never enters `_drops`, so the loot counter, `DropsView`, the discard flow, `bag_room` and the autodiscard filter have nothing to say about it, and the verdict draws what was found as a row of `OrbSlot`s under the finds, on the same light panel the bag's tray stands on (`DropsView.fill(items, orbs)`; it was once a `+n orbs` line, which said how many and never which). Every body also hands over a **purse**, which is not rolled for: gold falls off every body where gear falls off about one, and the fight says once under the verdict what it earned. Both fights carry a **loot counter** in the bottom-right corner wearing a sack and the count, which opens the run's whole haul, and everything a body drops -- its purse, its gear and its orbs -- **falls out of it into the arena** and lies there before it fades, which is the one thing said while the fight is still on; a run has no verdict to wait for, so the counter and its list are where a find is then read properly. Opening a find in either list gives a Discard button, which is how a run that has turned up more than the bag will hold is sorted out before it banks. How much room is left is said by the **counter's own face**, which ramps from the pack's green to its red as the bag fills, and the sentence explaining it stands inside the counter's panel rather than out in the arena -- a colour is read out of the corner of an eye without taking any room, and the words belong where the player has already asked what the run is carrying, which is also the one place they can do something about it. A level the player has ruled out never appears at all -- not in the counter, never thrown into the arena, not in either list -- and the number thrown away is said once, under the verdict.

**Camping is that same run, played for the hours the game is shut, and it is deliberately not a second economy.** The obvious way to pay for hours the player is not there is to invent a rate -- gold an hour, experience an hour, a dial or two to tune against `TownPrices`. That is a whole economy to keep in step with the one the game already has, and it drifts the first time `HP_GROWTH` or a weapon's attack speed moves. So a camp is the tile's own `Encounter.farm`, armed off the player's gear and played out for a couple of minutes at `CLICK_RATE` (`Camp.rates`), and a share of what it earned a second is what the camp pays a second. Every dial that moves what a tile is worth moves a camp with it.

**It sets itself up (the user's ruling, 2026-09-23).** There was a Set up camp button on the tile panel, and a camp that had to be chosen, stood on and broken. Now there is none: the hour the save was last written (`Inventory.saved_at`, also written as the window closes) is when the player left, and at start-up the camp is made from then on `MapBuilder.best_farm()` -- the farmable tile farthest out, since a body's worth grows with the distance -- paid at once and shown. Paying before the screen is up means a game closed again on the report loses nothing.

**Its size is the user's ruling too: eight hours away pay about one hour of active play, and eight hours is the most.** The earlier camp played the run with nobody clicking, at one to one -- the missing loot was meant to be its tax -- and that was too strong. The share is `IDLE_SHARE`, `3600 / MAX_SECONDS`, of a run played at five clicks a second (the middle of the balance tables' 3/5/7), so the one figure in `Camp` is that share and it follows the cap. The weapon-alone condition went with the clicks: a hero whose weapon does not swing itself earns by the hand now, and only No Rest and Berserker's World, whose texts say so, forbid a camp.

Sampling rather than playing out the whole night is the one corner cut: the arrays an endless run grows never reach a night's worth of bodies, and `earned()` is rate times the seconds since. The price is that a night is an average night and never its own lucky roll of the dice, which is the right trade for hours nobody watched.

**What a camp does not pay:** no gear, no orbs, no bodies for a bounty board and nothing towards the kills that unlock the first orb and the first unique. Those are for going in with a sword in your hand. Gold and experience are what a camp is for -- the two things that pile up and are spent elsewhere.

`MAX_SECONDS` (8 h) is the promise the feature makes: a night pays a night, and a fortnight away pays a night as well, so the map's difficulty curve is never a matter of patience rather than of gear. The screen says so in as many words when the cap is what stopped it, because a number that looks short with no explanation reads as a bug.

**The screen is the fight's screen with nobody on it**: the map put away behind it, the corner buttons gone, the tile's own backdrop dimmed to dusk, and a panel saying what the hours came to. Escape does nothing there, as on the transcension's black screen, so a stray press cannot throw the report away unread; the X and **Break camp** are the ways out.

Crit damage is what a crit *adds*: 50 is half again, and `arm` clamps crit chance to `CRIT_CAP` so a pile of gear -- or a piece from a save written before chances stopped being multiplied -- can never make every hit a crit. An automatic swing only earns while an enemy is standing there, so nothing banks through a walk-in. Health is `hp_modifier` over a base that grows **exponentially** with the distance from cell (0, 0) and **steps once per ice wall behind the cell** -- `BASE_HP * HP_GROWTH ^ steps * WALL_GROWTH ^ walls_inside(cell)` -- so the frontier pulls away from whatever the player is carrying and farming is how they catch up. `BASE_HP` is tuned so the first ring is what it always was, and `HP_GROWTH` is the dial for how fast the map gets hard. The second term is why the land a fallen wall opens is a frontier again rather than a walkover: felling a wall proves some forty times the damage a second the land inside it asks for, and a whole band of ten rings only grows by `HP_GROWTH ^ 10`, about five. `walls_inside` counts none of a wall's own ring, so the step lands on the first ring past a wall and `for_wall` carries no growth term of its own -- the wall on ring 21 comes out `WALL_GROWTH` times the one on 11 because its ring's bodies already are. Gold does **not** step with it: a body past a wall is tougher for the purse it always carried, and so is the level it drops at -- the reasoning is in `Scenes/Map/DESIGN.md` |

Its **face** is how full the bag is: `_tint_loot_button` ramps a tint over the brown button art from `FACE_BROWN` through `Palette.GOLD` to `FACE_DANGER` (the pack's own red, the key its danger button is played in) as `bag_room` runs out, a ramp rather than a step for the same reason `_clock_color` ramps. The tint goes on **copies of the theme's own styleboxes**, one per `UITheme.STATES`, rather than on the node — `modulate` on the Button would take the find's icon and the count with it, and what has to change colour is the frame behind them. A hit floats its number off the enemy at twice Pixellari's native size, and a crit at three times in the damage red with an exclamation mark, so which kind of blow landed is read without reading the number. `_show_find` throws each find and each orb out of the body instead of announcing it in the corner, and `_finds_shown` counts them, which is all anything downstream ever asks. **The one exception is a unique the collection log has never held**, which the main scene stands on a banner under the HUD column (`_announce_unique`, and `hud_bottom` is what keeps it off the clock): the arena's own language says *something* dropped and says it in a tenth of a second, which is right for the hundredth blue ring and wrong for a piece that happens twice in a world, fills a slot in the log and changes how a fight is fought. It is raised outside `CombatScene` on purpose -- the fight has no idea what the log already holds, and the one place that does is the main scene, in the moment before the ledger writes the find in. It is up for five seconds whatever the player does, because a banner that a mashed click can take away before it is read is worse than none; after those five seconds it is the player's own clicking that decides, and a player who stopped to read is given an X rather than having it snatched. `_place_corners` puts the counter and Terminate where they go every frame rather than anchoring them, because both are scaled by `ui_scale` and an anchor knows nothing about that. Endlessly the clock is hidden and the tally counts up. A `CanvasLayer` the main scene adds in front of the map rather than a scene change — the map holds a whole generated world in memory that a scene change would throw away, so it is hidden and its `process_mode` disabled instead. Drawn height comes from `SIZE_HEIGHT`, so a slime is knee-high and a boss looms. The backdrop is `Assets/Area/<env>_<variant>_<n>.png`, the environment from the encounter, the variant from `MapBuilder.area_variant`, and `n` from `layout_for(cell)` — every place is drawn `AREA_LAYOUTS` (4) ways, and the pick is seeded from the cell under its own `"backdrop"` tag, so a tile always fights on the same one however often it is attempted but which picture it is has nothing to do with who walks in. `backdrop_for` clamps a layout past the end and falls back to `grass_plain_1` rather than leaving a fight with no picture behind it. It is also where the player's autodiscard rule is applied: `_on_loot_dropped` is the only listener to `Encounter.loot_dropped` and forks into `loot_kept` and `loot_discarded`, which is what the rest of the game hears. A find at a ruled-out level is counted in `_auto_discarded` and nothing else — no square, no counter, nothing thrown — and the count is said once under the verdict. `gold_gained` is a body's purse passed on, for the reason `loot_kept` is. `_throw` is the one arc everything a body drops travels on -- across at a steady rate while the height goes up and comes back down, then lying where it fell before it fades and frees itself -- from `_drop_origin`, the enemy itself (a drop lands the frame it dies, so the body is still standing there). `_show_coins` throws a purse as `Coins.count_for` coins, so a richer body visibly pays more without the arena filling up; `_show_find` throws one find or orb, and at rare and better, and over a good orb, stands a `LootBeam` over it in the rarity's **ring** colour, taller the rarer (its foot at the bottom of the icon, the ring round it, the find standing inside the light) -- the ring half of the ramp rather than the text half, because a find is thrown against a snowfield or a noon desert and that is what that half was picked for. It is the second hand-rolled tween in the file beside `_show_damage`, there being no effect helper, and it is what says something was found or earned while the fight is still on. `_gold_label` still says the total once under the verdict, behind a coin rather than the word and in the panel's own colour rather than `Palette.GOLD` -- that hex is the unique item step, and spending it here would put one colour on a currency and on an item's name in the same panel. `bag_room` is how much room the bag has left (`-1` when nobody has said), which is what the counter's face is tinted from and what puts `_warning` up inside `_loot_panel` — a line over the list rather than a panel of its own, flipped through `_show_warning` because it changes the popup's height and the popup is centred on what it holds; `drop_discarded` is a find the player threw away by hand from either list |

`EnemyRoster` lives in `Scenes/Enemies/`, documented there.

**Game feel** (`CombatScene`, through `Juice`): every landed blow flashes and squashes the enemy (`_jolt_enemy`, springing back to `_enemy_scale`), a crit shakes `_arena` (the backdrop is drawn `BACKDROP_BLEED` oversize so no edge shows), a death bursts in `CombatActor.tint()` and freezes the game by tier (`STOP_*`, boss and elite also shake), and an elite-or-better find slows the fight so its beam is watched. Damage numbers pop in about their middle and drift. Coins rest `COIN_REST` then fly into the loot counter, which brightens (a scale bump would walk it off the corner `_place_corners` pins). `HealthBar` leaves a pale ghost of each blow that holds and drains.

**Bleed is the mace's whole argument, and it is the deepest wound rather than a sum of them.** A share of the blow that landed goes on coming off the body every second it is standing there, so a heavy, slow weapon keeps working between swings where a fast one does not. Adding each blow's wound to the last was the obvious reading and is the wrong one: a weapon swinging twice as fast would bleed twice as hard *and* hit twice as often, so the stat would be worth most to exactly the kind it is not for. A new blow therefore replaces the wound only when it is deeper, which makes what a mace is worth the size of one blow and not how many of them land -- a crit deepens it, an equal blow does not, and a smaller one leaves it alone. It belongs to the body it was opened in: cleared with `_struck` as the next one walks on, so a body that gets back up under Restless dead gets up whole. It and the Sunscorched Cowl's Heatstroke are the same thing off different numbers -- damage a second with nobody touching the enemy -- so they go through one `_wear_down`, which is `WAITING`-only (a body walking in or already going down takes none of it, the way an automatic swing lands on neither) and sets `_domino = false`, a death nobody dealt being no one's first blow. No clock is involved either way, so a farm run needs no special case, and there are no floating numbers for a bleed tick: the health bar moving is the whole of what says it.

**Drop rate is the broad finder, and where it meets a narrow one the two are added.** It lifts gear, uniques, orbs *and* the purse; item rarity lifts only the weights a piece of gear rolls its rarity on and gold find only the purse, which is why the narrow ones carry the bigger numbers on a piece (20% Gold Find against 5% Drop Rate). The two extra lines are in `_kill` and **added** to the narrow stat -- `gold_find + drop_rate`, `orb_find + drop_rate` -- the way `Equipment.totals` adds its global percents, so 20 and 30 are half again as much rather than 56% more, and so which of the two stats a player happens to be wearing cannot change the answer. Neither `gold_of` nor `OrbTable.chance_for` learned about a second stat: those two are what a *body* is worth, with no player in them, and `TownPrices` quotes every price off `gold_at_steps`. Teaching them the player's finders would have moved every price in every town.

**Chests.** `Encounter.for_tile(..., chest = true)` (from `MapBuilder.has_chest`) fields `MIMIC` alone under the `CHEST` profile: a HUGE boss on the ordinary clock. Its loot is a coin toss (`MIMIC_UNIQUE`, 0.5): one guaranteed unique off the ground's pool and nothing else, or `MIMIC_ROLLS` (4) certain pieces of gear rolled the ordinary way. It used to roll ten times with the first certain, about seven pieces a chest, which made a chest worth more than several settlements; the toss keeps a chest exciting while capping it at four. The unique half ignores `FIRST_UNIQUE_KILLS`, and the mimic takes no ordinary unique roll on top. The mimic has no environments, so no other lineup can draw it.

**A find rolls again.** A gear roll that beats the chance rolls once more at the same chance, and that one again, to `MOST_DROPS` (4) pieces off one body. The point is the moment rather than the rate: at a common's 3% it is worth 3% more gear and is all but never seen, while a boss -- or anything on a deep drop rate -- turns a find into two often enough that a drop stops being one square. A **certain** roll never cascades, because it beat nothing: the promised Broken Sword is one helping hand, a mimic pays exactly `MIMIC_ROLLS`, and `always_drop` still means one piece a body, which is what kept every test and screenshot that uses it exactly where it was.

The cap is not a taste decision. `LootTable.chance_for` clamps at 1.0 and a late drop rate reaches that ceiling on a boss, so an uncapped chain would roll for ever; the follow-ups are also never `guaranteed`, or `always_drop` alone would do the same. It lives in `Encounter._kill` rather than in `LootTable.roll` because a drop is still one roll for every other caller -- a vendor's shelf, the tests, the smith -- and only a body killed in a fight is meant to be generous.

## Rules and gotchas
- **An endless run has no clock and no loss:** `Encounter.advance` does not touch `time_left` while `endless`, so the only two ways a run finishes are `stop()` and `give_up()`. Anything reading `time_left`, `remaining()` or `enemies` to decide something has to ask `endless` first -- the lineup grows, so `lineup.size()` is the only honest end of it. The one thing a run does keep off its tile is `elite_every`, which is the whole of its rhythm.
- **Loot belongs to the attempt, not the tile:** a tile's enemies are seeded from its cell, so the same tile always fields the same fight, but `Encounter.loot_rng` is left unseeded (a `RandomNumberGenerator` seeds itself, so there is no `randomize()` to add) and re-fighting a tile can turn up something else. Tests set the seed themselves.

**The nameplate speaks in tiers too.** The name used to be bone for everyone with "(elite)" tacked on, while the pips and the bar's frame already said green and gold. Now the name takes the tier's colour, stands between two marks (a skull, a crown), and carries a title seeded from the cell and the slot -- a word in front of an elite ("Ancient Werewolf"), a line under a boss ("Scourge of the Meadows", the ground from `Encounter.TITLE_GROUND`). A boss's name is 32 px and its plate pops in white; an elite's does not, so the pop stays the boss's. Tried and turned down: tinting the skull green -- the pack's brown outline times green sank into every meadow, so it stays the bone it is cut in -- and a third, gothic font, which would have been one more face that is only clean at one size.

## The enemies strike the clock (2026-09-21)
Half of every piece's numbers did nothing: armour, health, block, dodge, energy shield, regen, leech, life on hit and the resistances were rolled and shown, and the one thing that could hurt the player was the clock. **The user's answer was to let the enemies strike the clock itself** rather than to give the player a second pool to lose -- the clock stays the only way to lose, and a blow is seconds off it. Everything below is the user's decision unless it says otherwise.

- **Armour takes a share, block a flat amount, and armour goes first.** The user's own figure is the test: a 100-second blow against 90% armour and 10 s of block costs nothing (`taken`, `_test_enemy_strikes`). The two answer different enemies -- a share is strongest against a big blow, a flat amount against many small ones -- which is what makes a shield and a greatsword's empty hand a real choice.
- **Both grow for ever, and so do the blows.** A blow is sized off `base_hp`, so it grows with the walk and steps with every wall exactly as a body's health does, and gear answers it through `LootTable.scale` exactly as it answers health. So defence has the same shape as damage: the player is ahead through a band and falls behind past a wall, and "monsters outgrow gear on purpose" holds for both halves of a fight. Armour is a *rating* turned into a flat share of every blow, `rating / (rating + ARMOUR_K)`, so that it can grow for ever without ever reaching the whole; the user asked for no caps, and the formula is why none is needed. **The share ignores the size of the blow** (the user's correction, the same day): it was first `rating / (rating + K * hit)`, Path of Exile's way, which made a plain set's 90% inside the first band fall to 7% past the second wall and left the reading of armour to depend on who was hitting. Now 50 armour is half of any blow and 450 is 90% (K was 100 for a few hours, then halved), and it is block, the flat amount, that big blows outgrow.
- **Dodge works the same as armour** (the user's words): a rating on the gear, the same flat formula, but the share is a chance to take none of a blow rather than a cut of every one. It was a percentage with a flat step before; now it grows like armour and the material's power applies to it.
- **The rhythm is the user's:** a common once a second, an elite every two, a boss every four (first built at twice that pace from a slip in the user's message, and corrected the same day). `HIT_TIER` (1, 2, 4) is mine: it makes every tier cost the same seconds a second against bare skin, so what differs between them is the *shape* of the blow -- and armour and block read shape. The first blow waits a whole interval, so a body killed quickly never lands one, and damage stays the first defence.
- **The ice wall does not strike** (the user's): it stays the damage check it was, and `balance_wall.gd`'s wall figures did not move. **A farm run and a camp are not struck either** (mine, unchallenged): a run has no clock to strike and "a run cannot be lost" was a rule worth keeping.
- **Time on hit wins back only what the blows took** (`wounds`) -- the user chose it over "any time, up to the full clock", which would have let a fast dagger stop the clock for good.
- **Everything else went** (the user's): health, energy shield, regen, leech and the resistances, and the four kinds built on them -- the hat, robe, slippers and Sapphire Amulet -- rather than finding them clock jobs of their own. The helmet and body are now armour (helm, plate) or dodge (hood, jerkin), and each slot kept its share of drops. Heartwood Plate reads armour where it read health, and the Bulwark answers a blow its block stops whole where it rolled a block chance.
- **Block and time on hit are kept in tenths of a second** (mine). A blow near the start is a quarter of a second, and a modifier rolls whole numbers; counted in whole seconds, the smallest "+1 Block" was a wall against every blow in the first band, and the first probe had a plain shield making the first band untouchable. Tenths keep the numbers whole and the lines honest ("+0.3s Block").
- **Strikes are a switch the main scene turns on** (`Encounter.strikes`), the bargain `uniques_after` already strikes: a fight nobody tells anything is the fight it always was, so the tests that time a clock to the second did not all need rewriting. The one line that matters is in `_open_fight`.
- **Where the dials sit, and what they were chosen against** (`HIT_SECONDS` 0.25, `ARMOUR_K` = `DODGE_K` = 50): a bare-handed first ring still won at eight clicks a second, and the first ring past the wall won in the farmed set of rares at four clicks a second with some 6 s to spare and **lost** with the set's armour, dodge and block taken off. Measured with a throwaway probe on the day, at the first, doubled rhythm (so the blows cost twice what they do now), over rings 1 to 20 in one seeded set per rarity: inside the first band a worn set of commons is struck for nothing and a stripped one already loses on ring 10 at four clicks a second; past the wall commons lose and rares need the clicks; by ring 20 damage runs out before defence does. With the flat share the same frontier fight struck the farmed set for 11 s and **time on hit won every second of it back** -- while blows come slower than a hit heals, time on hit cancels them outright; without armour the blows were 27 s and it could not keep up. Those are measurements, not settled design, and `balance_wall.gd` reports the seconds its played-out player lost to blows.

## Tile modifiers and world curses (2026-09-21)
Two systems for length: the land past the **second** wall rolls modifiers on its own fight (`TileMods`), and a transcension may take curses into the new world up to a budget of skulls (`Curses`, and the section below). The first build is the ones that are **a number on a dial that already existed**; the rest wait until these have been played.

- **One rule for both: it takes on one side and pays on another,** so nothing nets to zero. "Prices doubled, +gold find" was turned down for that; a curse that costs power pays in loot, one that costs loot pays in experience.
- **A tile's modifiers are derived, never saved** -- `hash([map seed, "mods", cell])`, like its chest and its variant -- so a world always fields the same ones and the next world others. Set pieces (a settlement, a chest, the wall) carry none.
- **One more modifier every ring of land past the second wall, no dice** (the user's, 2026-09-24, over the first build's 1, 1-2, 2-3): counted in `MapBuilder.ring_of`, so under the Ring of Walls every five-ring loop adds one too and the modifiers begin past its closer second wall -- the curse's walls are real walls. **Draws are put back, and a repeat is a tier** (the user's, same day: "tougher and more rewarding"): Savage II hits 100% harder and pays +40%, a factor compounds (Piercing II leaves a quarter of the armour), Horde II is ten more with the elite still last, Elite Ground II fields one in three (never closer than one in two). Sparse, Barren and Gilded are `once`: a bigger boon is not tougher, and "no gold" has no second step. **Rings a wall stood on carry none**, fallen or not, though `ring_of` counts them with the land outside. Unplayed: a Short Day III is 24 s off the clock, the likeliest tier to make a tile unwinnable.
- **Shares add, they do not compound:** a tile's `hp` and Iron Foes, a tile's `hit` and the Bloodthirst, go into one sum each (`_hp_more`, `_hit_more`), the rule `_unique_more` and the global percents already follow.
- **A farm run and a camp carry only what can bite there** (`farm: true`: Thick-skinned, Elite Ground, Barren, Gilded), reward and all. A run has no clock and is never struck, so a clock or strike modifier there would be its reward for nothing. The user's ruling, over "charting fight only" (a modified tile would be a one-off) and "rewards always" (deep tiles would be the best farms by a mile).
- **Sparse is a boon on purpose** (the user's): six bodies, the tile charted sooner, nothing paid -- four fewer bodies is the price. Horde moves the elite to the end (`elite_every` 15) so the fight still ends on it; Horde, Sparse and Elite Ground never share a tile because all three set `elite_every`.
- **Timeless halves time on hit and does not take it away:** it is the one defence that wins clock back, and the rarest roll for it.
- **Lean Pickings is a "less" multiplier, not -50 points.** `drop_rate` is additive, so 50 points off would be nothing to a player with none; `Encounter._lifted` turns "half the finished chance" into the lift that gives it, and `LootTable.chance_for` floors at -100. Gear only: the purse, the orbs and the unique roll keep the full rate, and promised pieces stay certain. It pays in **ascended drops** (+1 one find in ten, +2 one in a hundred, `Item.ascend`, the Orb of Ascension's own act), the user's choice over +60% rarity -- fewer pieces, better ones. It is the only way a +n piece exists outside an heirloom.
- **Raising `MOST_DROPS` was turned down as a reward:** four chained finds is already a one-in-a-million body at base rates, so only a stacked drop-rate build would ever feel a fifth.
- **Meta rewards are allowed** (the user's ruling): the Long Winter pays two super orbs a wall.
- **Turned down:** Mimic Season (every chest already is one -- Hungry Mimics took its place), Heirless (a world with the heirloom doll off is a new game).
- **Deferred, each needing a mechanic of its own:** Warded, Thorned, Volatile, Regenerating, Enraged, Lair; Unmarked Roads, Closed Gates, Cold Forge (each turns off something a town is for), Lone Heir, One Breath, and Pauper's half-price selling (`TownPrices` has no player in it).
- **What `tests/balance_mods.gd` found on its first run, and it is about the strikes, not about these:** on the first ring past the second wall a common's blow is ~75 s of clock, ~24 s after a farmed rare set's armour and block. `hit_of` grows with `base_hp`, walls and all, and armour grows with the item's level, which is 7 out there -- so past the second wall one landed blow loses the fight and the tile is really "kill each body before it swings" (1 s a common, 2 s an elite). That is why Savage, Piercing, Keen-eyed, Sundering, Timeless and the Bloodthirst all read x1.00 in the table and Frenzied reads x1.33: they are free rewards until `HIT_SECONDS`' growth is looked at. Every number in both tables is an unplayed first guess.
- **Hard Lessons (the user's, added after the first ten): 75% less experience, every skill point worth double.** Both halves are multipliers on purpose. The experience is a "less" on the finished figure, so No Rest's and Pauper's +experience cannot buy it back point for point; the doubling is of what a point *adds* -- flat and percent sums alike, read through `Inventory.skill_worth` so the skill's card says what the fight gets -- and never of a capstone, which is not a number. **It is not "half the skill power", which is what was first written here:** a level costs only 3% more than the one before (`LEVEL_XP_GROWTH`), so a quarter of the experience is a quarter of the levels only at the start and a fixed ~47 levels behind later on. At the same experience earned it is 4 points' worth against 10 at level 11, 40 against 50 at level 51, level at about 78, and ahead from there -- and its real prize is the ceiling: the trees hold 69 ranks, so an uncursed player stops at 69 points' worth at level 70 and a cursed one goes on to 138. With No Rest and Pauper beside it (+65% experience, added before the "less") it is level by about 31. A curse for a long world, and one that turns the two experience curses from filler into its partners. Worked out by hand on 2026-09-21, not by a table in `tests/`; it ignores the overranks, which give an uncursed player somewhere to spend past 69 (and double what a Hard Lessons overrank adds too). The sentences in the table were cut to one line each when it arrived: eleven wrapped rows do not fit a 648 px window.
- **The second batch (2026-09-21, the user's pick of five ideas and the four that pay in meta currency).** *Pacifist Hands, Berserker's World, Glass World* are the Metronome's, the Berserker's Band's and the Glass Edge's rules made a whole world's, and **each adds to its unique where both are had** (the user's ruling) -- through the unique's own sum, never a second multiplier. Pacifist Hands had to give bare hands a swing of their own (+1.5 a second, then everything doubled) or a new world, which begins with no weapon, could not kill anything; it and Berserker's World cannot be taken together, because no clicks and no swings is no damage at all (`not_with`, which the picker greys). *Raw Finds* makes a world where every find is a crafting base and orbs fall three times as often. *Homeland*: only two lands leave gear -- the one the start stands on, so the first fights are never barren, and one other -- chosen once the new map exists, because nobody knows what the start stands on before that; chests and the wall still pay, being no land's. *Specialist*: one tree, every point worth half again, its share **added** to Hard Lessons' (2.5, not 3); it also means no skill goes past its most that world (overranks need every tree full), which is part of its price. *Restless*: a camp full in 2 hours that pays double an hour -- the one curse that changes how often the game is opened. **The four that pay in meta:** *Forgotten* (no log damage this world; its new finds count twice for good), *Ring of Walls* (a wall every 5 rings; only where the wall stands moves -- `walls_inside` still counts in tens, so the land and the tile modifiers are where they were and the extra walls are extra gates, each paying its orb), *Lone Heir* (the heirlooms' doll begins bare and takes one piece; the heirloom made at that world's end is +1), *No Second Chances* (a lost tile is a lost world, +1 orb a wall, added to the Long Winter's). A world lost that way makes **no heirloom**: otherwise losing a fight on purpose would be the cheapest way out there is, with no price and no wall asked. All unplayed.

## The skull budget (2026-09-21)
The skulls were a label until the user asked how a player should earn them: now a transcension may take any curses whose skulls add up to `Inventory.skull_budget`, and **nothing else caps them** -- `MOST` (three) is gone, the user's ruling, with no cap on the budget either. The table itself is the ceiling (41 skulls with every row taken, one of Pacifist Hands and Berserker's World), and every row added raises it.
- **The rule:** at each transcension the budget becomes `max(budget, depth + skulls carried)`, the skulls carried being only those the budget paid for (the dungeon's depth is added on top of the budget and never carried, see The dungeon). Put the other way, every wall broken is a skull and every skull of the budget left unspent cancels one: playing safe with 2 of 6 and breaking 4 walls earns nothing, and only a world that uses its budget climbs. It never falls, so a bad world costs only itself.
- **Depth, not walls:** `walls_credited` counted in `WALL_STEP` rings. Counting walls would let the Ring of Walls (a wall every five rings) double the climb for three skulls; it still pays its extra super orbs.
- **A world lost to No Second Chances raises nothing** -- its depth and its skulls both go, the user's ruling ("an all or nothing gamble"). The raise stays whole for a world left through the fortuneteller however it went.
- **Starts at 0:** the fortuneteller's way out waits for a wall, so a first transcension always has at least one skull to spend; only a world lost under No Second Chances can leave the curse card greyed.
- **Turned down:** buying skulls with gold or super orbs (difficulty should be earned, not bought, and the orbs are spoken for), a Transcend tree node (would lose to every power node), a budget of transcensions made (grows with time played, not with what the player can take), Hades-style free curses with the pay gated (these pay row by row already).
- **What it asks of the table:** now that skulls cost, players will read reward per skull. First looks, unplayed: Restless (1) pays a lot for one skull; Hard Lessons (2) is cheap for a curse that dominates a world.

## The dungeon (2026-09-21)
The user asked for an infinite dungeon out of what the packs already held, "solely to test the damage of the player", with a global leaderboard to come. Built as a third kind of `Encounter` rather than a mode of its own: the wall's bargain (one clock, never struck, a check on damage) on a run's growing lineup.
- **Depth is won by killing Gollux, and by nothing else** (the user's ruling, after the first build scored the floor reached). A depth is fifteen floors with him on the last; the count of depths won is `Inventory.dungeon_depth`, it only rises, **it persists across every transcension** (the user's ruling), and the next descent begins under it with a whole clock. What this replaced: a score in floors, and `dungeon_start`, which walked a descent past the floors a plain blow felled -- beginning under the depths already won does that job with no arithmetic, and a player new to it climbs the easy depths several to a descent, since a descent that kills Gollux goes on.
- **Clicks land** (the user's ruling). The first build stilled them, so a leaderboard could not be won by an autoclicker and a score could be checked by playing the build again; the user turned that down. So a future ladder ranks hands as well as gear, and its scores cannot be re-derived: that is the trade, knowingly made. The generators are unseeded again, as a run's are.
- **The clock stops outside `WAITING`.** Every floor costs `walk_in + DEATH` (1.1 s) whoever fights it; charged for, fifteen floors would be 16 s of a 60 s clock before a blow was struck.
- **Health is the floor and the tier, never the roster's size or `TIER_HP`.** A land boss is 24 bodies; `DUNGEON_TIER_HP` (1, 2, 5) keeps Gollux a boss without making him the whole of his depth, and means who stands on a floor cannot move what it asks.
- **Dials, unplayed:** `DUNGEON_GROWTH` 1.2 -- the user asked for steeper than the first 1.12 -- so a depth is 1.2^15, about fifteen times the one over it, a little steeper than an ice wall's eleven and a half since 2026-09-27; `DUNGEON["seconds"]` 60; `DUNGEON_TIER_HP`.
- **What still reaches down:** everything worn and learned, since that is what is measured -- Heartwood's and Second Wind's seconds, Last Gasp, the Glass Edge. Of the curses only the ones that are the player's own numbers or the clock's (Short Days, the Glass World); none sizes a body.
- **It pays nothing in the fight, and a skull a depth on the black screen** (the user's, 2026-09-26): `Inventory.skull_allowance` adds the dungeon's skulls to the budget a transcension may spend on curses: depth n is n skulls and every depth won adds up, so depth 4 is 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 (the user's correction of a first build that paid one a depth). Won once, never again a descent. It is a flat bonus handed out whole every time, so it must not compound: `skulls_earned` carries only the curses the world's own budget paid for (`min(skulls, budget)`), and whatever the dungeon paid for is given back whole next time rather than climbing into the budget. So the skull climb is still walls, and the dungeon is how far past it a player may reach.
- **Nothing else is paid,** the camp's rule taken further: a second economy with no ceiling is the one thing an endless mode must not be. The depth is the pay. Milestone rewards were left for later.
- **Its creatures are one artist's** (Admurin: `Enemy_Galore_I`, Gollux, the cave parallax), cut by `tools/cave_dungeon.py`. The game's own skeletons and gargoyles suit a cave but are drawn three times the size by another hand, and looked pasted on. The cave creatures are 15-32 px tall, so they draw at 5-7 screen pixels a pixel against the hero's 3.4: shown to the user in `tools/qa/cave_dungeon.png`.
- **The nearest rock is in front of the fighters and they stand lower than on land** (the user, on seeing the first build: "it looks like they are floating"). That first build also drew the pack's `0.png` as the nearest layer; it is the pack's preview of all seven, opaque, and hid them.
- **Deferred:** a weekly seed with `TileMods` on it, skull brackets, Gollux's `healing` strip as a rule, the leaderboard itself (Steam's if it ships there, else one small endpoint), an entrance on the map instead of the corner's skull (built 2026-09-26: see *The Gollux cave* below).

## The bounty kill toast and the filled banner (2026-09-22)
The user asked for a small "+1" toast with the monster's portrait when a bounty monster is killed, a more prominent one -- the portrait "with a banner around it" and bigger text -- for an elite or a boss, and a popup when the bounty is filled. Until then a counted kill was invisible in the fight: the ledger wrote the board and the journal was the only place to read it.
- **The ledger says it, the main scene draws it.** Only the ledger counts a kill (`Scenes/CLAUDE.md`), so it is the ledger that reports one: `bounty_counted(enemy, have, need)` out of `add_kill`. The fight itself never hears of a bounty -- a board is the main scene's state, as the collection log is, which is why the unique banner is raised there too. The toast stands under the fight's top column beside where that banner stands rather than floating off the body with the damage numbers: `_float_text` is the arena's own language, and a toast with a picture in it is not a number.
- **A run's toast is a preview.** A run pouches its bodies and the board is written at `bank`, but the user wanted the toast live, so the ledger works out what the pouched body will count as (the board's count plus the pouch, through `BountyBoard.takes`) and says that. It cannot be wrong: both ways out of a run bank, so what it said the body would count as is what it counts. A body past the job is said by neither kind of fight.
- **The filling kill is the banner and nothing else.** Boards post commons at five kills and elites at one (`NEED_ELITE`), so an elite bounty is filled by its first kill: the prominent elite toast and the filled popup would always have come up together for one body. The user chose the popup alone for that kill, wearing the elite's frame and the large count -- so the banner carries the toast's weight, and the toast is for the kills short of the job. The elite and boss toast styling is still there for a posting that ever needs several. A boss is never posted at all.
- **The popup is the unique banner.** The user chose the non-blocking banner over a tip-style popup that would freeze the fight: five seconds, then an X, swings passing through. `_announce_unique` was split into what is the unique's (the `ItemSlot`, the rule lines, the slow-motion beat, which stays the rarer thing's) and `_raise_banner`, which the bounty shares, so the hold, the X, the click-through and the close on the verdict are written once.
- **What sets an elite apart is what the game already uses for one:** the nameplate's skull (a gilded crown at 2x for a boss), Pixellari's 32, and a frame round the picture -- `ItemRarity.frame` of the elite step (the unique's gold for a boss), which is the one framed square the packs' style already has. On cream the tier's colour is the ramp's darker half (`LEAF`, `SLOT_TAN_DK`), never the backdrop's `LEAF_LT` or `GOLD`.
- **The latest toast wins.** A run with spawn speed drops bodies faster than a toast fades; stacking them was more code for a column of "+1"s nobody reads. The banner puts any toast down for the same reason.

## The Gollux cave and the Seeing Stone (2026-09-26)

Everything below is the user's unless it says otherwise.
- **One cave a world, and none inside the first wall, and none past the land the last wall ever broken opened** (`MapBuilder.place_cave`, `CAVE_FIRST_RING`, `Inventory.farthest_land`). Read as: the ring one past the first wall's, out to the furthest land radius any world has reached. So there is no dungeon until a wall has fallen in some world, and every world after puts it behind its own first wall -- the way down is re-earned each world. Never on a ring a wall stands or stood on (the ice would cover it) or a settlement (mine). Chosen by where it lies, off the map seed, before the land out there is generated; saved in the map, since generation is never repeated.
