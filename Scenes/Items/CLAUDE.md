## Items (`Scenes/Items/`)
Every kill rolls for gear (about one body in thirty), always hands over gold, and rolls separately for an orb. Every drop is its own `Item` with a rarity, a level and modifiers; nothing stacks. **Read `DESIGN.md` here before changing what stats, modifiers, rarities or orbs mean** -- it holds the why.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `loot_table.gd` (`LootTable`) | `ITEMS`: the eight pieces (sword, shield, armour, helmet, boot, torch, ring, amulet) with icon, weight, `slot`, and three lists: `stats` (what it shows; all a PERCENT mod can scale), `affixes` (stats it may roll a FLAT mod for), `globals` (stats it may roll a GLOBAL percent for; jewellery only). `chance_for` = `TIER_CHANCE * SIZE_CHANCE`, lifted by `drop_rate`, capped at 1. `roll(enemy, rng, guaranteed, tile_level, drop_rate)` gives `null` or an `Item`. `scale(stat, value, level, flat)` with `LEVEL_GROWTH` / `LEVEL_FLAT` is the one place a level touches a number. `STAT_LABELS`, `stat_line`, `stat_value` (the number alone, for a table), `stat_delta`, `delta_shows` are the one place a stat is spelled |
| `item_rarity.gd` (`ItemRarity`) | The five steps (common, uncommon, rare, elite, unique), per-tier weights out of 1000, `MOD_COUNT`, both colour ramps, the slot `StyleBoxFlat` (the socket only -- no border), `frame(rarity)` (the drawn frame from `tools/item_frames.py`, null for common), `LEVEL_FLOOR` / `roll_level`, `weights_for(rarity%)` |
| `modifier_table.gd` (`ModifierTable`) | The pool and the four kinds: `PERCENT` (needs `has_stat`), `FLAT` (needs `can_roll`), `GLOBAL` (needs `can_globalize`), `PLAYER` (fits anything, applied by nothing yet). `pool_for` builds only legal candidates; `roll` draws without replacement. `DORMANT` names the resistance mods nothing can roll. `band_line(id, level)` writes a modifier with its band in the number's place (the fortuneteller's appraisal), through the same `_written` as `line`. `level_flat` on an entry overrides the stat's own level step |
| `equipment.gd` (`Equipment`) | What is worn. Eight `Socket`s; `TAKES` joins a socket to an item `slot` (rings fit either hand, shield and torch share the offhand). `sockets_for(item)` puts the emptiest first. `totals(flat, percent)` adds every piece's `effective_stats`, then applies the set's GLOBAL percents once |
| `item.gd` (`Item`) | type, rarity, level, rolled `stats`, `mods`, and `broken` (a smith's upgrade that went wrong). `effective_stats()` = flat mods added, then percent mods scaling. `global_percents()` feeds `Equipment.totals`. `locked_mod()` is the modifier the smith has pinned, or `{}`; `locked_line()` is its line among `mod_lines()`, which `ItemDetails` writes in a base stat's ink -- there is no word for it on the line. **Every line an item writes leads with its number** (`stat_line`, `stat_delta`, `ModifierTable.line`): `ItemDetails` splits on the first space into a table row's value and its name. `to_dict` / `from_dict` are the save, with `broken` absent reading as whole |
| `orb_table.gd` (`OrbTable`) | The six orbs in tray order (`ORBS`), and `can_apply` / `apply` / `why_not` as three branches of one `match`. `_reroll_at` is where rarity and modifier count are settled, and where a locked modifier is put back and the draw fills around it. A **broken** piece fails all six in one line at the top of `can_apply`. Drop chance has `LootTable.chance_for`'s shape; weights are flat with no depth gating. `roll_favoured(rng)` is a vendor's shelf: two weighted draws, the rarer kept, so the shops lean on the drop rates rather than on a table of their own |
| `unique_table.gd` (`UniqueTable`) | The unique items. `UNIQUES`: id -> `name`, `base` (the `LootTable` piece it is), `mods` (the modifier ids it always carries, at most `MOST_MODS`), `effect` + `effect_text` (what `Encounter` reads and what the card says; a home piece adds `home` and `clause`, and reaches the fight as `"home:<env>"` through `effect_of` and `"<clause>:<env>"` through `clause_of`; `effect_text(id)` appends `PILGRIM_TEXT` to a home card), `envs` (where it is found; none = anywhere). `pool_for(env)`, `chance_for(enemy, drop_rate)` = its own `TIER_CHANCE` x `LootTable.SIZE_CHANCE`, `roll(enemy, env, rng, tile_level, drop_rate, guaranteed)`, `icon(id)` (its own under `Assets/Gear/Unique/`, else its base's), `FIRST_UNIQUE_KILLS` |
| `inventory.gd` (`Inventory`) | `uniques_found` (the collection log, `note_unique(id)`), `effects()` (capstones learned + uniques worn), `items`, `equipment`, `gold`, `orbs` (name -> count), `kills`, `level` / `xp`, `skills`, `towns` (`TownState`), `fortunes` (what the fortuneteller sold the player: a plain Dictionary read through `FortuneTeller`'s statics, which this file never names), `tips`, `first_elite_taken`, `autodiscard`. `CAPACITY` 40 loose items; `add` appends then `trim`s and returns what was destroyed. `equip` / `unequip` move a piece as one step. `order()` reads the bag, `worst_first()` empties it. `stats()` is what a fight is armed with. `save(path)` goes through `SafeFile`; `load_from(path, problem)` migrates old versions |

## Rules and gotchas
- **An item's level and base stats are frozen when it is rolled** and saved with it; an orb changes only its rarity and modifiers. **The one exception is the fortress smith** (`Scenes/Town/blacksmith.gd`): an upgrade raises the level by one and rewrites `stats` to `Item.scaled_stats` at the new level, leaving the modifiers at the values they rolled. Nothing else may move either. An `Item` built by hand (tests do this) must be given `stats` or it is worth nothing.
- **Crafting happens only from the bag,** where no fight is holding a second reference to the same `Item`. The smith obeys the same rule, and works on nothing that is worn.
- **A broken piece is out of the game as far as changing it goes** -- no orb, no upgrade, no lock -- and a **locked** modifier (`{"id", "value", "locked": true}`) survives every orb: `_reroll_at` keeps it and fills the rest around it, and Divine skips its value. No orb lowers a rarity, so a locked piece is never a common (a contradiction in `ItemRarity.MOD_COUNT`). A lock is one of the rarity's handful and never an extra on top of it.
- **`LootTable.scale` is the only place that knows what a level does.** PERCENT and GLOBAL mods take the multiplier only, PLAYER mods take neither, `CHANCE_STATS` (crit, block, dodge, drop rate) take the flat step only, and a FLAT mod may name its own step (`level_flat`).
- **A tile's level is a ceiling, not a payout:** a drop rolls evenly up to `level_of(cell) + TIER_LEVEL[tier]`, with rarity lifting the floor. Balance numbers describe a piece at the ceiling.
- **`stats`, `affixes` and `globals` must not overlap,** and every affix needs a FLAT mod naming it and every global a GLOBAL one, or it is unreachable. `test_inventory._test_items` fails on all of it. `_test_slot_locks` pins which stats are locked to which piece.
- **A GLOBAL mod is applied by `Equipment.totals`, never by `Item.effective_stats`,** and two of them add rather than compound.
- **Increased item rarity lifts weights, it does not add steps;** UNIQUE has no weight in `TIER_WEIGHTS` and no `RARITY_STEP`, so it never comes out of `ItemRarity.roll`. A test holds that. **Uniques are rolled by `UniqueTable.roll` instead,** beside the gear, the way orbs are.
- **A unique is an `Item` with `unique` set:** `type` is still its base piece (slot, base stats, the smith's upgrade), its rarity is UNIQUE, and its modifiers are its row's -- always those, in that order, only the values rolled (`Item.rolled_unique`). It may carry a line its base could never roll; `effective_stats` applies by kind and never asks the pool. **Only Orb of Divine applies to one** (`OrbTable.can_apply`, beside the `broken` line). A retired id loads as null, like a retired piece.
- **`ModifierTable.UNIQUE_ONLY` names the modifiers only a unique's row may carry** (`added_gold_find`): no piece lists the stat as an affix, so no pool holds them. `test_inventory` tells one from a dead modifier by that list, as it does `DORMANT`. No row may carry a PERCENT and a GLOBAL on one stat: they write the same sentence.
- **`gold_find` is in `CHANCE_STATS`** for `drop_rate`'s reason: a level adds to it and never multiplies it.
- **`Equipment.effects()` is where a Pilgrim's set is made:** two or more home pieces emit each ground once and every worn piece's clause for every worn ground. The damage never stacks; the shape of the fight does. No two home pieces may share a socket (a test holds it), so all six can be worn and everywhere is home.
- **A set piece is a unique that wears green:** its rarity stays `UNIQUE` (drops, floors, the log), and only `Item.border_color()` / `text_color()` / `frame()` know it is one (`is_set`, a row with a `clause`), so **ask the item for a colour or a frame, never `ItemRarity`'s tables by rarity**. The ramp runs cold to hot -- grey, blue, lilac, brick, gold, one hue a step -- which is what left green free. What the set does is `Item.set_text()`, a green line of its own on the card under the piece's `effect_text`.
- **`Inventory.stats()` carries what a unique asks of the player:** the Spiked Helm's hundredth of the set's armour goes in with the skills' flat damage (so the percents scale it), and `bare_sockets` / `bag_pieces` ride along for the Ascetic's Cord and the Packmule's Harness. `Inventory.salvage(item)` is `TownPrices.salvage_price` while the Rag and Bone Sack is worn, else 0.
- **Found is found:** `uniques_found` only grows, and `FightLedger._put_in_bag` is the one place that writes it, so it follows the bank-or-pouch rule. **Each one found is `UniqueTable.COLLECTION_DAMAGE` (1) percent more damage, worn or not** (`Inventory.collection_bonus`), applied in `Inventory.stats()` as its own multiplier after gear and skills -- never in `Equipment.totals`, which knows nothing of the log.
- **The resistances are off every piece on purpose** (nothing can hurt the player). Their labels, steps and mods stay written down; `DORMANT` is how a dormant mod is told from a dead one.
- **`order()` is level-major and `worst_first()` is rarity-major; they are not each other reversed.** `_test_bag_order` pins both.
- **A piece is in the bag or a socket, never both:** always go through `Inventory.equip` / `unequip`. `unequip` refuses on a full bag. Worn gear is outside `CAPACITY`.
- **Gold and orbs are not in `items`:** never capped, trimmed, sorted or autodiscarded. A spent-out orb is erased, not left at zero.
- **Every modifier value is a whole number,** so saves hold `14` not `14.0` and round-tripped items compare with `==`. Stats are stored as the number they display.
- **Names, not enum positions, go on disk** (rarities, sockets, orbs). A retired item, mod, orb or ill-fitting socket is dropped on load rather than guessed at.
- **`Inventory` never saves itself** and the path is always a parameter, so tests never touch the player's save. A file that is there and cannot be honoured comes back empty **with a reason in `problem`**, and the caller must then never save over it.
- **`ItemRarity` must never mention `Item`,** and a `const` must not name another class's enum: that is where Godot's cycle checker bites.
