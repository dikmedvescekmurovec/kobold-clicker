## Items (`Scenes/Items/`)
Every enemy killed rolls for gear and hands over a purse, and both are the player's whatever the fight does next — a lost fight still leaves the kills that were made. Gold is the half that always lands: gear falls off about one body in thirty and gold off all of them, and nothing spends it yet. A drop is its own thing: it rolls a rarity off the enemy that carried it and modifiers off that rarity's band, so two Wooden Swords are two different swords and nothing stacks. **The offensive stats are live, and so are the three finders, the mace's Bleed and the torch's Sight.** What the player wears decides what a click takes off, how often it crits and how hard, how fast the weapon swings on its own, how much of a blow goes on coming off a body that is still standing, how far a tile shows when it is charted, and how often a body leaves anything at all — the jewellery is where the finding lands, and where offence lands that is not the weapon's: flat damage and crit among its affixes, and **global** percents that scale what the whole set is worth rather than what the ring is. Everything else — armour, health, leech, the attributes — is still rolled, saved and shown and waits on the systems that would give it something to do (nothing can hurt the player yet, so there is nothing for a defence to defend against), and the resistances are off every piece until there is.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `loot_table.gd` (`LootTable`) | `KINDS`, the twenty-three kinds of gear a slot can hand over and the materials each is found in, and `ITEMS` built out of it: sixty-nine bases under `Assets/Gear`, each with the `slot` it goes in (`slot_of`, which `Equipment` turns into sockets), its icon, its kind's weight, and three stat lists apiece. `stats` is what the piece *is*, the few numbers it shows and the only ones a PERCENT modifier can scale; `affixes` is what it can *carry*, stats it has none of and still rolls a flat modifier for, which is how a ring with no health rolls "+8 Health"; `globals` is what it can carry a **global** percent for, a percentage of what the whole set is worth rather than of anything the piece has, which is the only way "+14% increased Damage" means anything on a ring (`has_stat` / `affixes_of` / `can_roll` / `globals_of` / `can_globalize`). Only the **jewellery** has a `globals` list, so "the jewellery carries the global offence" is a property of this table rather than a rule about slots written somewhere else. Stats span pieces on Path of Exile's rule: base `damage` on the weapons, health and armour nearly everywhere, and `move_speed` (the boots), `block_chance` (a thing you hold), `bleed` (the mace) and `sight` (the torch) locked to one kind apiece — `test_inventory._test_slot_locks` pins those by kind, and pins the difference between showing a stat and merely being allowed to carry it: base damage is a weapon's while the jewellery carries damage as an affix, and a torch rolls flat block chance while showing none. The **resistances are off every piece** for now: nothing can hurt the player, so a resistance defends against nothing, and their labels, level steps and modifiers all stay written down so putting them back is a word per item. The Gold Ring's own base stat is `drop_rate` and the Gold Amulet's is `gold_find`, and item rarity is an affix on all seven jewels: the finders are what the jewellery is worn for. And how often anything drops at all. As with health, the chance is not written per enemy: `TIER_CHANCE` and `SIZE_CHANCE` multiply into `chance_for`, lifted by whatever `drop_rate` the player is wearing and capped at 1. `roll(enemy, rng, guaranteed, tile_level, drop_rate, item_rarity)` gives `null` or a whole `Item`, drawing the chance first so a kill that leaves nothing still costs exactly one draw, and settling rarity and level **before** the type so a material can be gated by what the piece itself is worth. A fight of nine commons and an elite is worth about a third of an item, which `test_inventory.gd` holds to. `LEVEL_GROWTH`, the per-stat `LEVEL_FLAT` and `scale(stat, value, level, flat)` are the one place that knows what a level does to a number -- and `CHANCE_STATS` (crit, block, dodge, the three finders, bleed, sight) take the flat step alone, because a probability has a ceiling a multiplier walks straight through, and `scale`'s trailing `flat` is how a modifier band sized for another piece passes a step of its own (`ModifierTable`'s `level_flat`), and `TIER_LEVEL` is what a body's tier adds to the ceiling on what it drops. `STAT_LABELS`, `stat_line` and `stat_delta` are the one place a stat is spelled, and `delta_shows` is what keeps a difference too small to print off the screen rather than showing it as "+0 Armour" |
| `item_rarity.gd` (`ItemRarity`) | The five steps (common, uncommon, rare, elite, unique), what each enemy tier is worth as integer weights out of a thousand, how many modifiers each step carries, the two colour ramps and the `StyleBoxFlat` an item square is drawn with (`BORDER` is two panel pixels — exactly four on screen at `ui_scale` 2; one disappeared into the tan socket the ramp sits on). `LEVEL_FLOOR` and `roll_level` decide what level a drop comes out at: evenly anywhere up to the tile's ceiling, with rarity lifting the floor so a rare piece is never worth nothing. Kept apart from `Item` because a `const` in one class naming another class's enum is where Godot's cycle checker bites: `Item` reads these tables, so these tables never mention `Item`. UNIQUE is hand-crafted and has no weight in any row, so `roll` never gives one — `UniqueTable.roll` does, beside the gear, and a test holds that the rows stay without it |
| `modifier_table.gd` (`ModifierTable`) | The pool, and four ways onto a piece: a `PERCENT` modifier scales a stat the item has (`has_stat`), a `FLAT` one adds a stat the item is merely allowed to carry (`can_roll`, so base stats *or* affixes), a `GLOBAL` one scales what the **whole set** is worth and needs only that the piece be allowed to (`can_globalize`, which today is the jewellery), and a `PLAYER` one buffs the player and fits anything. Where each is *applied* follows from that: percent and flat are folded into the piece by `Item.effective_stats`, a global once by `Equipment.totals` after every piece is added up, and a player one by nothing yet. The attributes, leech and life-on-hit are flat-only — each is a quantity you add up across the set, so "+35% Fire Resistance" is the whole idea and a percentage of it would mean nothing; that is also what lets them sit in `affixes` on pieces showing none of them. `DORMANT` names the three resistance modifiers nothing can roll while the resistances are off the items -- written down because a dormant modifier and a dead one are told apart by name and not by inspection, and `test_inventory` fails on either mistake. `level_flat` on an entry is what one level adds to *that modifier's* band, in place of the stat's own step: `added_damage` takes a quarter point rather than a weapon's whole one, because the step was sized for the slot that has damage as a base stat and four sockets now roll the modifier. `added_gold_find` uses it the other way, naming **zero**: its band was sized for The Tithe, a unique, and a percentage of a purse that is already exponential in the walk has no business growing with the level as well. `pool_for` builds the candidates first, so a modifier an item cannot carry is never drawn rather than rolled and rejected, and `roll` draws without replacement so nothing repeats on one piece |
| `equipment.gd` (`Equipment`) | What the player is wearing, and `totals()` — what the whole set is worth, every worn piece's `effective_stats` added up stat by stat and then scaled by whatever the set's `GLOBAL` modifiers ask of the total, which is the one answer everything else asks for. The globals are gathered in the same pass and applied at the end, which is the only point at which the answer does not depend on the order the sockets were read in; a global on a stat nothing carries scales zero, so increased damage is worth nothing bare-handed. Eight `Socket`s over seven places, because two are not one-to-one: `TAKES` is the join from a socket to an item's `slot`, and it is the only place that knows a ring fits either hand and that a shield and a torch compete for the one offhand. Sockets are saved by name, for the reason rarities are. A piece saved into a socket it no longer fits is dropped rather than worn wrongly |
| `item.gd` (`Item`) | One dropped thing: type, rarity, **level**, the base stats it rolled at that level, its modifiers, and the lines the stat block shows. A piece is **frozen when it is rolled** -- `scaled_stats` works its numbers out once and they are stored on it and saved with it, so it is the same piece on every load. `effective_stats()` is what the piece is actually worth — flat modifiers added first, then percent ones scaling the result, Path of Exile's order and the only one that makes both shapes worth having; a flat modifier on an affix puts a stat here that `base_stats` never had. A `GLOBAL` modifier is deliberately **not** folded in -- it is a percentage of the set rather than of the piece, and `global_percents()` is what hands it to `Equipment.totals`. Only the level, the rolled stats and the modifiers are stored — everything else is looked up from the type. `to_dict`/`from_dict` are the save, with the rarity written by name because an enum value is only a position |
| `orb_table.gd` (`OrbTable`) | The six orbs -- Transmutation, Alteration, Alchemy, Chaos, Exalted, Divine -- and the one place that knows what each does. `ORBS` is the table (icon, weight, and the sentence the card reads out) in the fixed order the tray draws them in; `can_apply`, `apply` and `why_not` are three faces of one piece of knowledge and so are three branches of one `match` in one file, because the rule that lights a square, the rule that carries it out and the sentence explaining a refusal must never disagree. **No orb ever touches a piece's level or its base stats** -- rarity and modifiers are the whole of what an orb changes, and `_reroll_at` is where four of the six end up, because "what rarity is it" and "how many modifiers does it carry" are one question that only `ItemRarity.MOD_COUNT` may answer. Alchemy steps one rarity at a time and stops at ELITE, so UNIQUE stays unreachable; Exalted needs room under the rarity's own ceiling, so a common (band `[0, 0]`) has none. **Augmentation and Scouring were removed**: Exalted already adds a modifier at any rarity, and nothing takes a piece back down. The drop rate is `LootTable.chance_for`'s shape -- `TIER_CHANCE` times `SIZE_CHANCE` -- and the weights are flat, with no depth gating: fighting deeper buys better **modifiers**, and an orb rate that climbed with it would make the frontier the only place worth farming. The two marks a blacksmith leaves are handled in one place each: a **broken** piece fails `can_apply` for all six at the top of it, and a **locked** modifier is put back by `_reroll_at` before the draw fills the rest around it, and stepped over by Divine -- no orb lowers a rarity, so a locked common, a contradiction in `MOD_COUNT`, cannot come about, and a lock that counted on top of the handful would be a back door to a rarity's ceiling |
| `inventory.gd` (`Inventory`) | `items: Array[Item]` and the `equipment` worn, plus `first_sword_taken`, `kills` (lifetime, gates the first orb), `tips` (ids of the first-time pop-ups shown and corner buttons pressed, kept for the main scene) and `gold`, and the JSON under `user://inventory.json`. `equip`/`unequip` move a piece between bag and socket as one step, so a piece is never in both places or neither. It never saves itself: the main scene owns it and decides when, and the path is a parameter so the tests and the screenshot scripts never touch the player's own save. A missing file is a first run. A file that is there and cannot be honoured (unreadable, not JSON, the wrong shape, from a newer build) comes back empty **with a reason in `problem`**, and the main scene then refuses to start rather than play an empty bag over it -- the map's two-nulls rule. `save` writes through `SafeFile`, whole or not at all. Loading migrates the version 1 tally of names into plain common items. Version 3 added `equipped`; a version 2 save simply has none, and its owner starts bare-handed. Version 4 gives every item its level and the stats it rolled at that level; a version 3 save's pieces come back at level 1 carrying the tables unscaled, which is exactly what they were worth when it was written. Version 5 adds `autodiscard`, the levels the player has told the game to stop bringing; a version 4 save has none, and an absent key reads as an empty list. Version 6 adds `gold`, the purse, which a version 5 save comes back empty-handed from; it is **not in the bag and not against its cap** -- a number cannot be the worst thing in a full bag and can never be trimmed. The bag holds `CAPACITY` (40) loose items — worn gear is outside it — and that invariant lives here rather than at the callers: `add` appends and then `trim`s, returning whatever had to be destroyed, `load_from` trims a file written before the cap or edited by hand, and `unequip` refuses outright when the bag is full. Version 7 adds `orbs`, the currency, as counts rather than objects -- two Orbs of Chaos are the same orb, which is exactly what gear stopped being when it started rolling modifiers. They stand where gold does: outside `CAPACITY`, never trimmed, never filtered by an autodiscard rule and never sorted into a level section, which is true by construction rather than by a guard because they are not in `items` at all. `add_orb` / `orb_count` / `spend_orb` / `total_orbs` are the whole of it, an emptied orb is erased rather than left at zero so the save holds what is carried rather than a list of everything ever spent, and a version 6 save comes back with none. Version 9 adds `skills`, what `Skills.to_dict` writes; a version 8 save comes back with nothing learned and every level's point free. `stats()` is `equipment.totals(skills.flat(), skills.percent())` -- what a fight is armed with -- and `respec(tree)` pays `respec_cost` out of `gold` and refuses when there is nothing to give back or not enough to pay. `order()` is how the bag is read and `worst_first()` is how it is emptied; `levels()`, `count_at`, `discard_level`, `autodiscards` and `set_autodiscard` are the sections and their rules |

## Rules and gotchas
- **A generated item's level and base stats never change again, and the blacksmith is the one exception; its rarity and modifiers are what an orb is for.** An item's level and its scaled base stats are worked out once, when it is rolled, and stored on it and in the save. Crafting is the first verb that changes a held piece at all -- `item.gd`'s own docstring anticipated it -- and it changes only the two things `OrbTable` is allowed to touch, and only from the bag, where no fight is holding a second reference to the same object. The fortress smith's **upgrade** is the second verb and the only one that moves the frozen pair: level + 1, and `stats` rewritten to exactly what `scaled_stats` would have rolled at the new level, so an upgraded piece is indistinguishable from one found there. Its modifiers keep the numbers they rolled, which is what leaves a Divine orb something to do and what makes an upgraded piece a project rather than a finished article. It is priced, capped to the ground's own ceiling and carries a chance to **break** the piece, and all of that reasoning lives in `Scenes/Town/DESIGN.md` because it is a town's business; what belongs here is that the exception exists, that `Blacksmith` is the only place it may happen, and that a piece which `broken` is true of is never changed again by anything, orb or hammer. Before this, base stats were looked up from `LootTable` on every call so that retuning a sword retuned every sword already held; that is what made a held piece drift, and it is gone. `LootTable` is still where a *new* piece is rolled from and still owns `slot_of`, `icon`, `affixes` and the labels. Stats are stored as the number they display -- whole, or one decimal for a rate -- so the save round-trips exactly and the stat block can never disagree with the fight. An `Item` built by hand (tests do this) has to be given `stats`, or it is worth nothing.
- **A level is a multiplier *and* a per-stat flat step.** `LootTable.scale` is the only place that knows what a level does to a number. `LEVEL_FLAT` carries one entry per stat because an absolute step has to suit the size of the number it is added to: a point a level is everything to damage, which starts at 1, and a rounding error to crit damage, which starts at 50. A multiplier alone would leave a sword reading "Damage 1" for four levels. A `PERCENT` or `GLOBAL` modifier takes the multiplier only -- it is already a percentage of a stat that grew -- and a `PLAYER` modifier takes neither, because `+4s on the fight clock` scaled would delete the only way to lose a fight. A **flat modifier may name its own step** (`level_flat`) where the stat's was sized for a different piece: base damage keeps its whole point a level, which is what makes a sword gain a visible point each time, while `added_damage` takes a quarter, because four sockets roll it and only one of them is the weapon.
- **A slot is several kinds, and a kind is several materials.** With one base to a slot a drop differed only by level, rarity and modifiers, and every sword the game ever handed over was the same sword. Path of Exile's answer is two axes: **kinds** that play differently (the dagger weak and fast, the mace bleeding, the greatsword slow and heavy and using both hands) and **materials** gated by the piece's own item level. Which kind drops is the single weighted draw it always was, and the material is a second draw, evenly between the best the level has unlocked and the one under it — two of them rather than all four, so deep ground stops dealing the plainest material without the best arriving alone, and **no draw at all while only one is unlocked**, which is what keeps the shallow game costing exactly the rolls it always did. The kind weights are chosen to sum to 252 (21 x 12), so each **slot's** share of all drops is what it was before any of this, and each base has a name of its own rather than a number after a shared one, which is Path of Exile's convention and reads as a found thing rather than as a grade. The eight pieces the game shipped with are tier 0 of their kind, keep their numbers, and are written first for their slot, so nothing that already existed moved: old saves, the uniques' bases and every test asking for "a plain piece" see what they always saw.
- **Power is applied after the level, and is never written into the table.** A kind's factor and a material's are one number (`power_of`) that `Item.scaled_stats` multiplies by **after** `scale` has done its work. Writing the dagger as `damage: 0.6` in `stats` was the obvious way and is wrong: `LEVEL_FLAT` adds a whole point of damage a level to every weapon alike, so the flat step swamps the written difference and a dagger is within a tenth of a sword by level 10 — the kinds would level themselves out exactly where the game gets interesting. Applied after, a dagger is 60% of a sword at every level. One place, so the blacksmith's upgrade — which re-runs `scaled_stats` at the new level — follows it for nothing. The **material's** fifth skips `CHANCE_STATS` and `RATE_STATS`, because a steel shield holds more armour than a wooden one and blocks exactly as often, which is the ceiling `scale` already refuses to walk through; the kind's own factor does not skip them, since no kind names a factor for a chance. `TIER_POWER` (0.20) and `TIER_MIN_LEVEL` are the dials, and what they are worth at the frontier is measured in `Scenes/DESIGN.md`.
- **The torch is the one kind that states each material outright.** Sight is the whole of the piece and it has only two values, so the torch has two tiers rather than four and names its own unlock levels (`tier_levels`, `tier_stats`): 1 Sight from item level 1, 2 from 10, and a level never moves either. Everything else came off it — the energy shield, the regen and the crit damage it used to show are affixes at most now, and all its affixes are flat because with no base number but Sight there is nothing for a percent modifier to scale. That is also why the Hunter's Lantern and the Overflowing Chalice carry `added_crit_damage` in place of the percent line they had: a unique's PERCENT line needs its base to have the stat. Torches already held keep the numbers they rolled and have no Sight, pieces being frozen.
- **A tile's level is a ceiling, not a payout.** A drop rolls its level evenly up to `level_of(cell) + LootTable.TIER_LEVEL[tier]`, with rarity lifting the floor (`ItemRarity.LEVEL_FLOOR`), so fighting deeper improves the odds rather than the prize and farming is how the top of a tile's range is eventually rolled. A single drop is therefore a poor measure of a tile: the balance numbers describe a piece rolled at the ceiling.
- **Increased item rarity lifts weights, it does not add steps.** `ItemRarity.weights_for` multiplies each step by `1 + rarity% * RARITY_STEP` (common 0 ... elite 3), in whole numbers blown up by `RARITY_PRECISION`. At zero it returns the table untouched and `roll` draws exactly as it always did; unique is in neither table, so no bonus reaches it.
- **The bag is read one way and emptied another, and they are not one reversed.** `Inventory.order()` is level-major, because that is how a player looks for a piece; `worst_first()` is rarity-major, because that is what "worst" means when something has to go — a level-2 elite is worth keeping over a level-9 common. `test_inventory._test_bag_order` pins both, and that they differ.
- **A piece is in the bag or in a socket, never both:** `Inventory.equip`/`unequip` move it, which is why they live on `Inventory` rather than on `Equipment` — the bag and the worn set have to change together, and every caller getting that right separately is the same bug waiting in as many places as there are callers.
- **A two-hander is the one swap that is not one piece for one piece,** and that is what `Equipment.displaced_by` exists for. Putting a greatsword on takes the weapon *and* the offhand off; putting anything in the offhand takes the whole greatsword off. So `equip` hands back an `Array[Item]`, `Inventory.equip` has to check the bag has room for all of it (`can_equip`) where a swap never needed a guard before — one leaving the bag paid for the one coming back — and the page's Equip greys rather than destroying something to make room for a hand the player had two of. It is one function rather than a rule in each place because the button's tooltip, the comparison's columns and what `equip` actually does have to be the same answer; asking `equip` afterwards is too late for a button that must already be grey. A save holding an offhand beside a two-hander loses the offhand, by the same pruning rule as a socket a piece no longer fits: the weapon is what the player chose. The cost is deliberately more than a socket of numbers — it is the torch, so a greatsword sees one ring of the map less, and it is the only thing in the table that trades a whole slot for damage.
- **Item rarity and gold find became gear stats, and the dormant `PLAYER` modifier now sits beside a live `FLAT` one.** Both were skill-only, with `LEVEL_FLAT` 0 and a comment saying no piece shows or rolls them; `added_item_rarity` is on all seven jewels' affix lists now and `gold_find` is the Gold Amulet's base stat, and both take drop rate's point a level. The old player-wide `item_rarity` modifier is still in the table and still applied by nothing, which is not a contradiction but is worth knowing when reading the pool: one is a percentage the piece carries and the other was to have been a percentage of the player.
- **What an item shows, what it can roll and what it can scale are three different lists:** `stats` is the piece's own, `affixes` is a permission to roll a flat modifier for a stat it has none of, and `globals` is a permission to scale the whole set's. Neither permission reaches a stat block, so adding a stat to one widens the modifier pool without lengthening what the player reads. Neither may overlap `stats`, and an affix with no `FLAT` modifier naming it -- or a global with no `GLOBAL` one -- is unreachable; `test_inventory._test_items` fails on all of it.
- **A `GLOBAL` modifier belongs to the player, so it is applied by `Equipment.totals` and never by `Item.effective_stats`.** A ring has no damage of its own for "+14% increased Damage" to scale; the sword's is exactly what it means, and the one place both are in hand is the set. Two of them add rather than compound, which is the arithmetic a player does in their head. It writes the same line a `PERCENT` one does, and honestly so: with one weapon between them, a sword's increased damage and a ring's are the same claim about the same number.
- **Every modifier value is a whole number:** it means one `randi_range` a modifier, it means the save holds `14` rather than `14.0`, and it means a round-tripped item can be compared with `==` instead of an epsilon. Base stats may hold a float (`attack_speed`) precisely because they live in a const table and never cross the file.

## Uniques (`unique_table.gd`)
**What they are for.** Everything else the game hands over is a better number. A unique is a rule: the Metronome turns the clicking game into an idle one, the Knucklebone Ring pays for clicking without a pause, The Tithe trades every ordinary drop for gold. They are the thing to hunt once a set of rares is farmed, and the collection log is the list of what is still out there.

- **A unique takes an ordinary socket and loses on raw numbers.** Two or three modifiers against an elite's five or six (`MOST_MODS`). If a unique were simply better, every socket would end up holding one and the orbs would have nothing left to do; as it is, wearing one is giving up a crafted piece for a rule, and sometimes the rule is not worth it.
- **Its modifiers are written, not rolled -- and chosen to serve the effect.** Every Metronome carries the same three lines; only the values roll, in the band any modifier rolls in at the piece's level, which leaves Divine its job and makes a well-rolled one a second thing to hunt. The lines are stats that do something today (damage, crit, attack speed, drop rate, gold find), plus at most one that says what the base piece is. A PERCENT line only where the base has the stat; elsewhere FLAT or GLOBAL. A row may carry a line its base could never roll -- flat damage on armour -- which is part of what makes it unique. Never a PERCENT and a GLOBAL on one stat: they write the same sentence, and on a hand-written piece two identical lines read as a bug (the first Metronome did exactly that).
- **Only Orb of Divine touches one.** Every other orb changes which modifiers a piece has or how many, and a unique's are its identity.
- **Any monster can carry one, off the pool of the ground it stands on.** `TIER_CHANCE` is one common in two thousand, one elite in a hundred, one boss in ten, times the body's size and lifted by drop rate. A pool per environment rather than one global roll, because "Rimeplate is found on ice" is a plan the player can act on and "0.01% anywhere" is not -- it is what the log's hint exists to say. The tile's level is still only a ceiling, so the same unique is worth finding again deeper in.
- **Nothing before the 100th kill, and pure chance after it.** The wait keeps a unique from falling before the player knows what an ordinary drop is. The first boss used to be promised one; the user took that out (2026-09-19) -- a unique is found by luck or not at all.
- **Duplicates are allowed; loops and immortality are not.** Only rings can be doubled (two sockets). Same-kind effects add inside their own term -- two Tithes are x5, not x9; two Knucklebones cap at +100% -- and different kinds were first multiplied once each -- since replaced by one sum for everything a unique adds to a blow, see the second batch below. The clock is the only way to lose, so anything touching it is fenced: the Hourglass never pushes the clock past where it started, gives nothing for a boss, and does nothing on a run, which has no clock at all.
- **The Knucklebone streak is the hand's alone.** It first multiplied every blow, the weapon's own swings included; with a home piece on top that made the reference edge fight idle. It now multiplies clicks only, which is also what the ring is *about*. A click that lands nothing still counts towards the streak, or every walk-in (0.6 s) and death (0.5 s) would break it.
- **What "not infinite power" means on a map with no edge.** The first draft of the ceiling test held the best unique set to "still needs clicking at hex 20". It cannot: a farmed set of rares already needs only 1.5 clicks a second there, so any flat doubling makes that one tile idle. The honest measure is what the set *buys*: monsters grow by `HP_GROWTH` a step for ever, so a set that multiplies a blow a fixed number of times is caught up a fixed number of steps further out. `test_combat._test_uniques_keep_the_edge` walks outward until the fight wants as many clicks as it did without the uniques, and holds the best clicker set (Headsman, the ground's home piece, two Knucklebones at a full streak, every line at the top of its band) to a dozen steps. It measures ten.
- **The collection log shows what a thing is only once it is found or shown.** A unique neither found nor shown by a fortuneteller is its outline in black with no ring (hiding those squares altogether was tried and taken back the same day: the outlines are what say how much there is to find). One she has shown is the piece itself, frame and all, darkened (`ItemSlot.SHADOW`), and its card says what it is and names its ground -- the tile panel's own swatches and the nearest seen tile, never land under the fog. The found square is a level-1 specimen at the bottom of every band, because the log says what the thing is and the one in the bag says what the player's rolled. Found is found: selling one takes nothing off the list.
- **Art.** Seventeen icons are RPG-pack pieces, cut by `tools/ui_kit.py` and finished the way every base is: the pack's white border painted into a dark outline (`_outlined`). Four of them are now pixel for pixel a base's picture -- Spiked Helm is the Iron Helmet, Sunscorched Cowl the Steel Helm, Rimeplate the Iron Armor, Meadowstriders the Iron Greaves -- and two more are a base recoloured (Heartwood Plate the Wooden Armor, Glass Edge the Iron Sword), which is Path of Exile's own habit and is why a unique's *frame* and its name carry the weight rather than its outline. The other ten -- four rings, three amulets, Dominoes, Snowball, Bulwark -- were 12 px `Icons.png` art doubled, which is the doubled pixel the base jewels were redrawn for. They are drawn now, by `AI-sprites-generator/gear.py` (`UNIQUES`) in the pack's style, each keeping what it was: a band of bone, a ruby in milled gold, an hourglass of glass and sand. `tools/qa/ui_kit_uniques_drawn.png` shows each one over what it replaced.

## The second batch of uniques (sixteen more, and the home pieces' second rules)
- **One sum, not a product.** The first batch said "different kinds multiply once each", which with six effects was x4 at most. With twenty-seven, a clicker could wear Glass Edge x2, Berserker's x3, a home piece x2, Knucklebone x1.5 and the Packmule x1.4 -- x25 before crits, some twenty hex steps of frontier, and every one of them better in its socket than any crafted elite, which is the opposite of what a unique is for. So everything a unique adds to a blow is **added up and applied once** (`Encounter._unique_more`), the way `Equipment.totals` adds its global percents. The same stack is x5.9, and `test_combat._test_uniques_keep_the_edge` measures it at **five** hex steps once the set has paid its own prices (a faster clock, two more bodies, a weapon that never swings). The Gambler's Die stands outside the sum because it is not more damage but a different blow every time; Giantsbane is Giant Slayer made bigger, never a second one on top.
- **Dead stats got jobs through uniques rather than by making enemies hit back.** Armour, health and block are rolled on half the gear and do nothing, because nothing can hurt the player and the clock is the only way to lose. Making enemies dangerous is a second game on top of this one. A unique that reads a dead stat -- Spiked Helm, Heartwood Plate, Bulwark -- makes the stat worth rolling *for the player who wears it* and costs three lines each; the modifiers already on every shield are suddenly a build.
- **Momentum and Heartwood are capped, and the brief's uncapped versions were turned down.** A farm run never ends, so 2% a kill for ever is unbounded damage; health grows with every level, so a second per hundred health eventually deletes the clock, which is the one way to lose. Both break the rule the user set for duplicates: no infinite power, no immortality. "Up to double" and "up to ten" are on the cards.
- **Every clock unique is worth nothing on a run** (Glass Edge, Last Gasp, Heartwood, Hourglass). A run has no clock, so a price paid in clock is no price there, and Glass Edge free would simply be the best farming sword. The user chose this over a second, run-only drawback.
- **Salvage pays wherever the player throws gear away,** because the card says "gear you throw away" and a discard that paid in one panel and not the next would read as a bug. It is a quarter of the *sell* price (`TownPrices.salvage_price`), so a trader is still worth four times the walk. A full bag destroying a piece pays nothing: the player did not throw it away.
- **The home pieces' second rules each make a differently shaped fight:** more bodies (Grazing), the elite on a full clock (Flush out), damage with nobody swinging (Heatstroke), free walk-ins (Frozen clock), a ground for killing bosses (Giantsbane), kills that pay twice (Restless dead). Two of them reshape the lineup, which is why `Encounter.wear` exists and runs before the view is built. **Restless dead rises in the slot it fell in** rather than joining the lineup: `KillPips` is built once from `enemies`, and a bar that grew mid-fight would be a second layout to get right for one ring of text.
- **The Pilgrim's set stacks shape, never damage.** Two home pieces work on each other's ground; read as "both bonuses apply" that is x3 then x7, and the six together are the best thing in the game on every tile. Read as "every worn piece's *rule* holds on every worn piece's ground", it is a collection goal that turns into a build -- Grazing with a Frozen clock, Heatstroke on a herd -- and home damage stays +100%, once. The mountain piece was first a shield, the Stoneward, and the Hunter's Lantern is held in the off hand too, so five was the most that could be worn and 'everywhere is home' could not happen. It is now the Stonebreaker, a hammer in the weapon slot -- Giantsbane is a weapon's rule anyway -- and `test_inventory` holds that no two home pieces share a socket. The whole set costs the weapon, so a Pilgrim gives up Metronome, Headsman and the Glass Edge for it.

## Heirlooms, and what is left when the world is (2026-09-19)
The ice wall is a cliff on purpose (`Scenes/Map/DESIGN.md`), so a world ends at a wall it cannot pass. Transcending is the way round it, and an heirloom is what makes the next world faster than the last: **every wall broken, in any world, is one piece the player may keep for good.** Everything below is the user's decision unless it says otherwise.

**A second inventory, not a flag.** Heirlooms have their own stash and their own doll -- "their own everything" -- and the heirlooms' doll is worn *as well as* the ordinary one, sixteen pieces. So an heirloom never competes with what a new world drops: it is pure addition, and a run's finds are still worth finding. In code it is literally a second `Inventory` inside the first (`stash()`), which is why `BagPage` could be built a second time rather than written a second time. It is saved inside the one file, because making an heirloom moves a piece from one list to the other and one save is one write. It shares `CAPACITY`; a pick is a wall and nobody breaks forty.

**Flats add, percents multiply.** The user chose between three sums for two dolls -- the heirlooms' stat as a percentage of the ordinary one's, a straight product, and this -- and took this: every piece's numbers on both dolls go into one sum, and each doll's GLOBAL percents are a multiplier of their own over it. 100 damage with +20% and 40 damage with +20% is 140 x 1.2 x 1.2 = 201.6, where one doll of sixteen would make 196. It keeps an heirloom worth its face on a bare-handed first tile (a product would not: 0 x anything) and makes a second doll's jewellery worth more than a ninth and tenth ring would be.

**Global or per side.** A stat is the player's, so whatever reads one reads all sixteen: the Spiked Helm's armour, Heartwood's health, the Bulwark's block. A unique that shapes the fight has only one fight to shape. **A count and a set are per side:** the Ascetic's Cord counts the bare places on the doll it hangs from, the Packmule's Harness the loose pieces beside its own doll, and a Pilgrim's set is made of home pieces on one doll -- one on each are two lone pieces. The Rag and Bone Sack, asked about on its own, pays from either doll for everything thrown away, heirlooms too. I guessed the helm as per-doll first and was wrong, and the standing instruction since is: **when it could be either, ask.**

**What the end of a world does to one.** Level 1, the base stats of a level 1, and **every modifier rescaled to level 1's band at the place it held in its own** (`ModifierTable.rescaled`) -- the user turned down keeping them as rolled, which would have made a level 1 sword carrying level 30 lines the whole of the early game. Rarity, the lines themselves, a lock and `broken` do not move. What it *was* is kept as `safe_level`, and the smith walks it back up to there with no chance of breaking it: that road was paid for once. Past it the hammer is the hammer. `safe_level` is the best the piece has ever been rather than the last (mine, unchallenged): a short second run should not cost a piece what a long first one earned. The modifiers do not come back with the levels -- a smith's upgrade never moved a roll -- so a Divine is how they catch up, which is what a Divine was already for.

**Broken stays final** (mine, unchallenged), heirloom or not: a player who pushes past `safe_level` is taking the same one-in-twenty everybody takes. A broken piece cannot be *made* an heirloom, since no world could ever mend it. **An heirloom can be discarded** (the user's correction to my first plan), one at a time, and the question is asked every time with no "Don't show this again": a misclick there costs runs of work. The pick does not come back. It can never be sold or filtered, and never returns to the bag. **A worn piece can be made one as well as a bag piece** (also the user's correction): it comes straight off the doll into the stash without passing through the bag, so a full bag is no obstacle.

**What survives:** the stash and its doll, picks not yet spent, the collection log, the kills and the tips. The user's phrase for the last two was that "helpers, such as broken sword and other guaranteed loot" are not dealt again: kills kept means the orb and unique gates are not waited out twice, and `first_sword_taken` is forced true, which also ends the promised elite. Everything else -- bag, doll, gold, orbs, level, skills, towns, fortunes, autodiscard, the map -- is a fresh start.
