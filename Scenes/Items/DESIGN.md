## Items (`Scenes/Items/`)
Every enemy killed rolls for gear and hands over a purse, and both are the player's whatever the fight does next — a lost fight still leaves the kills that were made. Gold is the half that always lands: gear falls off about one body in thirty and gold off all of them, and nothing spends it yet. A drop is its own thing: it rolls a rarity off the enemy that carried it and modifiers off that rarity's band, so two Wooden Swords are two different swords and nothing stacks. **The offensive stats are live, and so is drop rate.** What the player wears decides what a click takes off, how often it crits and how hard, how fast the weapon swings on its own, and how often a body leaves anything at all — the Gold Ring is the piece that finds things, and the jewellery is where offence lands that is not the weapon's: flat damage and crit among its affixes, and **global** percents that scale what the whole set is worth rather than what the ring is. Everything else — armour, health, leech, the attributes — is still rolled, saved and shown and waits on the systems that would give it something to do (nothing can hurt the player yet, so there is nothing for a defence to defend against), and the resistances are off every piece until there is.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `loot_table.gd` (`LootTable`) | The eight pieces of gear under `Assets/Gear` — sword, shield, armour, helmet, boot, torch, ring, amulet — each with the `slot` it goes in (`slot_of`, which `Equipment` turns into sockets): their icons, their relative weights, and three stat lists apiece. `stats` is what the piece *is*, the few numbers it shows and the only ones a PERCENT modifier can scale; `affixes` is what it can *carry*, stats it has none of and still rolls a flat modifier for, which is how a ring with no health rolls "+8 Health"; `globals` is what it can carry a **global** percent for, a percentage of what the whole set is worth rather than of anything the piece has, which is the only way "+14% increased Damage" means anything on a ring (`has_stat` / `affixes_of` / `can_roll` / `globals_of` / `can_globalize`). Only the **jewellery** has a `globals` list, so "the jewellery carries the global offence" is a property of this table rather than a rule about slots written somewhere else. Stats span pieces on Path of Exile's rule: base `damage` on the weapon, health and armour nearly everywhere, and `move_speed` (boot) and `block_chance` (shield, torch) locked to one place — `test_inventory._test_slot_locks` pins those, and pins that base damage is the sword's while the jewellery carries damage as an affix. The **resistances are off every piece** for now: nothing can hurt the player, so a resistance defends against nothing, and their labels, level steps and modifiers all stay written down so putting them back is a word per item. The Gold Ring's own base stat is `drop_rate`, which is the one stat outside the fight's four that anything reads. And how often anything drops at all. As with health, the chance is not written per enemy: `TIER_CHANCE` and `SIZE_CHANCE` multiply into `chance_for`, lifted by whatever `drop_rate` the player is wearing and capped at 1. `roll(enemy, rng, guaranteed, tile_level, drop_rate)` gives `null` or a whole `Item`, drawing the chance first so a kill that leaves nothing still costs exactly one draw. A fight of nine commons and an elite is worth about a third of an item, which `test_inventory.gd` holds to. `LEVEL_GROWTH`, the per-stat `LEVEL_FLAT` and `scale(stat, value, level, flat)` are the one place that knows what a level does to a number -- and `CHANCE_STATS` (crit, block, dodge, drop rate) take the flat step alone, because a probability has a ceiling a multiplier walks straight through, and `scale`'s trailing `flat` is how a modifier band sized for another piece passes a step of its own (`ModifierTable`'s `level_flat`), and `TIER_LEVEL` is what a body's tier adds to the ceiling on what it drops. `STAT_LABELS`, `stat_line` and `stat_delta` are the one place a stat is spelled, and `delta_shows` is what keeps a difference too small to print off the screen rather than showing it as "+0 Armour" |
| `item_rarity.gd` (`ItemRarity`) | The five steps (common, uncommon, rare, elite, unique), what each enemy tier is worth as integer weights out of a thousand, how many modifiers each step carries, the two colour ramps and the `StyleBoxFlat` an item square is drawn with (`BORDER` is two panel pixels — exactly four on screen at `ui_scale` 2; one disappeared into the tan socket the ramp sits on). `LEVEL_FLOOR` and `roll_level` decide what level a drop comes out at: evenly anywhere up to the tile's ceiling, with rarity lifting the floor so a rare piece is never worth nothing. Kept apart from `Item` because a `const` in one class naming another class's enum is where Godot's cycle checker bites: `Item` reads these tables, so these tables never mention `Item`. UNIQUE is hand-crafted, weighted zero everywhere and cannot drop — a test holds that until the chunk that writes them |
| `modifier_table.gd` (`ModifierTable`) | The pool, and four ways onto a piece: a `PERCENT` modifier scales a stat the item has (`has_stat`), a `FLAT` one adds a stat the item is merely allowed to carry (`can_roll`, so base stats *or* affixes), a `GLOBAL` one scales what the **whole set** is worth and needs only that the piece be allowed to (`can_globalize`, which today is the jewellery), and a `PLAYER` one buffs the player and fits anything. Where each is *applied* follows from that: percent and flat are folded into the piece by `Item.effective_stats`, a global once by `Equipment.totals` after every piece is added up, and a player one by nothing yet. The attributes, leech and life-on-hit are flat-only — each is a quantity you add up across the set, so "+35% Fire Resistance" is the whole idea and a percentage of it would mean nothing; that is also what lets them sit in `affixes` on pieces showing none of them. `DORMANT` names the three resistance modifiers nothing can roll while the resistances are off the items -- written down because a dormant modifier and a dead one are told apart by name and not by inspection, and `test_inventory` fails on either mistake. `level_flat` on an entry is what one level adds to *that modifier's* band, in place of the stat's own step: `added_damage` takes a quarter point rather than the sword's whole one, because the step was sized for the one piece that has damage as a base stat and four sockets now roll the modifier. `pool_for` builds the candidates first, so a modifier an item cannot carry is never drawn rather than rolled and rejected, and `roll` draws without replacement so nothing repeats on one piece |
| `equipment.gd` (`Equipment`) | What the player is wearing, and `totals()` — what the whole set is worth, every worn piece's `effective_stats` added up stat by stat and then scaled by whatever the set's `GLOBAL` modifiers ask of the total, which is the one answer everything else asks for. The globals are gathered in the same pass and applied at the end, which is the only point at which the answer does not depend on the order the sockets were read in; a global on a stat nothing carries scales zero, so increased damage is worth nothing bare-handed. Eight `Socket`s over seven places, because two are not one-to-one: `TAKES` is the join from a socket to an item's `slot`, and it is the only place that knows a ring fits either hand and that a shield and a torch compete for the one offhand. Sockets are saved by name, for the reason rarities are. A piece saved into a socket it no longer fits is dropped rather than worn wrongly |
| `item.gd` (`Item`) | One dropped thing: type, rarity, **level**, the base stats it rolled at that level, its modifiers, and the lines the stat block shows. A piece is **frozen when it is rolled** -- `scaled_stats` works its numbers out once and they are stored on it and saved with it, so it is the same piece on every load. `effective_stats()` is what the piece is actually worth — flat modifiers added first, then percent ones scaling the result, Path of Exile's order and the only one that makes both shapes worth having; a flat modifier on an affix puts a stat here that `base_stats` never had. A `GLOBAL` modifier is deliberately **not** folded in -- it is a percentage of the set rather than of the piece, and `global_percents()` is what hands it to `Equipment.totals`. Only the level, the rolled stats and the modifiers are stored — everything else is looked up from the type. `to_dict`/`from_dict` are the save, with the rarity written by name because an enum value is only a position |
| `orb_table.gd` (`OrbTable`) | The eight orbs -- Transmutation, Augmentation, Alteration, Alchemy, Chaos, Exalted, Divine, Scouring -- and the one place that knows what each does. `ORBS` is the table (icon, weight, and the sentence the card reads out) in the fixed order the tray draws them in; `can_apply`, `apply` and `why_not` are three faces of one piece of knowledge and so are three branches of one `match` in one file, because the rule that lights a square, the rule that carries it out and the sentence explaining a refusal must never disagree. **No orb ever touches a piece's level or its base stats** -- rarity and modifiers are the whole of what an orb changes, and `_reroll_at` is where four of the eight end up, because "what rarity is it" and "how many modifiers does it carry" are one question that only `ItemRarity.MOD_COUNT` may answer. Alchemy steps one rarity at a time and stops at ELITE, so UNIQUE stays unreachable; Exalted needs room under the rarity's own ceiling, so a common (band `[0, 0]`) has none, which is what separates it from the uncommon-only Augmentation. The drop rate is `LootTable.chance_for`'s shape -- `TIER_CHANCE` times `SIZE_CHANCE` -- and the weights are flat, with no depth gating: fighting deeper buys better **modifiers**, and an orb rate that climbed with it would make the frontier the only place worth farming |
| `inventory.gd` (`Inventory`) | `items: Array[Item]` and the `equipment` worn, plus `first_elite_taken`, `kills` (lifetime, gates the first orb), `tips` (ids of the first-time pop-ups shown and corner buttons pressed, kept for the main scene) and `gold`, and the JSON under `user://inventory.json`. `equip`/`unequip` move a piece between bag and socket as one step, so a piece is never in both places or neither. It never saves itself: the main scene owns it and decides when, and the path is a parameter so the tests and the screenshot scripts never touch the player's own save. A missing file is a first run. A file that is there and cannot be honoured (unreadable, not JSON, the wrong shape, from a newer build) comes back empty **with a reason in `problem`**, and the main scene then refuses to start rather than play an empty bag over it -- the map's two-nulls rule. `save` writes through `SafeFile`, whole or not at all. Loading migrates the version 1 tally of names into plain common items. Version 3 added `equipped`; a version 2 save simply has none, and its owner starts bare-handed. Version 4 gives every item its level and the stats it rolled at that level; a version 3 save's pieces come back at level 1 carrying the tables unscaled, which is exactly what they were worth when it was written. Version 5 adds `autodiscard`, the levels the player has told the game to stop bringing; a version 4 save has none, and an absent key reads as an empty list. Version 6 adds `gold`, the purse, which a version 5 save comes back empty-handed from; it is **not in the bag and not against its cap** -- a number cannot be the worst thing in a full bag and can never be trimmed. The bag holds `CAPACITY` (40) loose items — worn gear is outside it — and that invariant lives here rather than at the callers: `add` appends and then `trim`s, returning whatever had to be destroyed, `load_from` trims a file written before the cap or edited by hand, and `unequip` refuses outright when the bag is full. Version 7 adds `orbs`, the currency, as counts rather than objects -- two Orbs of Chaos are the same orb, which is exactly what gear stopped being when it started rolling modifiers. They stand where gold does: outside `CAPACITY`, never trimmed, never filtered by an autodiscard rule and never sorted into a level section, which is true by construction rather than by a guard because they are not in `items` at all. `add_orb` / `orb_count` / `spend_orb` / `total_orbs` are the whole of it, an emptied orb is erased rather than left at zero so the save holds what is carried rather than a list of everything ever spent, and a version 6 save comes back with none. Version 9 adds `skills`, what `Skills.to_dict` writes; a version 8 save comes back with nothing learned and every level's point free. `stats()` is `equipment.totals(skills.flat(), skills.percent())` -- what a fight is armed with -- and `respec(tree)` pays `respec_cost` out of `gold` and refuses when there is nothing to give back or not enough to pay. `order()` is how the bag is read and `worst_first()` is how it is emptied; `levels()`, `count_at`, `discard_level`, `autodiscards` and `set_autodiscard` are the sections and their rules |

## Rules and gotchas
- **A generated item's level and base stats never change again; its rarity and modifiers are what an orb is for.** An item's level and its scaled base stats are worked out once, when it is rolled, and stored on it and in the save. Crafting is the first verb that changes a held piece at all -- `item.gd`'s own docstring anticipated it -- and it changes only the two things `OrbTable` is allowed to touch, and only from the bag, where no fight is holding a second reference to the same object. Before this, base stats were looked up from `LootTable` on every call so that retuning a sword retuned every sword already held; that is what made a held piece drift, and it is gone. `LootTable` is still where a *new* piece is rolled from and still owns `slot_of`, `icon`, `affixes` and the labels. Stats are stored as the number they display -- whole, or one decimal for a rate -- so the save round-trips exactly and the stat block can never disagree with the fight. An `Item` built by hand (tests do this) has to be given `stats`, or it is worth nothing.
- **A level is a multiplier *and* a per-stat flat step.** `LootTable.scale` is the only place that knows what a level does to a number. `LEVEL_FLAT` carries one entry per stat because an absolute step has to suit the size of the number it is added to: a point a level is everything to damage, which starts at 1, and a rounding error to crit damage, which starts at 50. A multiplier alone would leave a sword reading "Damage 1" for four levels. A `PERCENT` or `GLOBAL` modifier takes the multiplier only -- it is already a percentage of a stat that grew -- and a `PLAYER` modifier takes neither, because `+4s on the fight clock` scaled would delete the only way to lose a fight. A **flat modifier may name its own step** (`level_flat`) where the stat's was sized for a different piece: base damage keeps its whole point a level, which is what makes a sword gain a visible point each time, while `added_damage` takes a quarter, because four sockets roll it and only one of them is the weapon.
- **A tile's level is a ceiling, not a payout.** A drop rolls its level evenly up to `level_of(cell) + LootTable.TIER_LEVEL[tier]`, with rarity lifting the floor (`ItemRarity.LEVEL_FLOOR`), so fighting deeper improves the odds rather than the prize and farming is how the top of a tile's range is eventually rolled. A single drop is therefore a poor measure of a tile: the balance numbers describe a piece rolled at the ceiling.
- **Increased item rarity lifts weights, it does not add steps.** `ItemRarity.weights_for` multiplies each step by `1 + rarity% * RARITY_STEP` (common 0 ... elite 3, unique 0), in whole numbers blown up by `RARITY_PRECISION`. At zero it returns the table untouched and `roll` draws exactly as it always did; a zero weight stays zero, so uniques remain unreachable.
- **The bag is read one way and emptied another, and they are not one reversed.** `Inventory.order()` is level-major, because that is how a player looks for a piece; `worst_first()` is rarity-major, because that is what "worst" means when something has to go — a level-2 elite is worth keeping over a level-9 common. `test_inventory._test_bag_order` pins both, and that they differ.
- **A piece is in the bag or in a socket, never both:** `Inventory.equip`/`unequip` move it, which is why they live on `Inventory` rather than on `Equipment` — the bag and the worn set have to change together, and every caller getting that right separately is the same bug waiting in as many places as there are callers.
- **What an item shows, what it can roll and what it can scale are three different lists:** `stats` is the piece's own, `affixes` is a permission to roll a flat modifier for a stat it has none of, and `globals` is a permission to scale the whole set's. Neither permission reaches a stat block, so adding a stat to one widens the modifier pool without lengthening what the player reads. Neither may overlap `stats`, and an affix with no `FLAT` modifier naming it -- or a global with no `GLOBAL` one -- is unreachable; `test_inventory._test_items` fails on all of it.
- **A `GLOBAL` modifier belongs to the player, so it is applied by `Equipment.totals` and never by `Item.effective_stats`.** A ring has no damage of its own for "+14% increased Damage" to scale; the sword's is exactly what it means, and the one place both are in hand is the set. Two of them add rather than compound, which is the arithmetic a player does in their head. It writes the same line a `PERCENT` one does, and honestly so: with one weapon between them, a sword's increased damage and a ring's are the same claim about the same number.
- **Every modifier value is a whole number:** it means one `randi_range` a modifier, it means the save holds `14` rather than `14.0`, and it means a round-tripped item can be compared with `==` instead of an epsilon. Base stats may hold a float (`attack_speed`) precisely because they live in a const table and never cross the file.
