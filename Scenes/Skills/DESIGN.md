# The skill tree: design notes

The rules are in `CLAUDE.md`; this is why they are the way they are.

## What it replaced
Until 2026-10-08 there were three fixed trees -- Power, Fortune and Guard, ten skills each in one sketched shape -- and, once all three were full, bursting: the trees popped black, kept their stats and started again a point dearer. The user replaced the lot with a tree the player builds out of loot, from their own sketches: a root in the middle, three branches (red strength up-left, blue intelligence up-right, green dexterity down), and stones of any base hanging off any branch.

## The user's rulings (2026-10-08)
- **Stones are items.** They drop, take orbs anywhere like gear (they are not inert; they just cannot be worn), sell and bin like gear, and are placed only on the black screen of a transcension.
- **Base**: strength, dexterity or intelligence, each with its own modifier pool.
- **Lines unlock by tier** (2026-10-09): every modifier in the game sorted onto the stone it belongs to, the good ones saved for the deep tiers, and the ranks line common on a shallow stone and rare on a deep one.
- **Connectors** 0 to 3, counting children only; 0 is a leaf.
- **Tier** is the deepest a stone may sit (a tier-3 stone fits at depths 1, 2 and 3), it comes from how deep the land is, and a higher tier rolls better -- capstones included.
- **One modifier a rarity step** (common none, uncommon one, rare two, epic three), and **how many ranks a stone takes is one of them** (`added_stone_ranks`): a rank is bought with a line the stone could have carried instead.
- **Levels still pay points**, spent as ranks in placed stones during a world; every line counts once a rank.
- **Capstones** are unique stones, not craftable, a rare share of the stones that drop, on a list of their own (not the collection log, no achievements). Fixed lines, values rolled by tier.
- **Lost with the bag**: a stone left unplaced, or pushed out by a swap, goes when the new world starts.
- **Frozen**: a placed stone keeps its numbers forever; it does not climb like an heirloom.
- **The starter tree**: the root; under it 5 strength and +1 damage, 5 intelligence and +1% crit chance, 5 dexterity and +1% attack speed. Until 2026-10-09 the root gave +1 of each attribute, always.
- **The root has one connector from the start** (2026-10-09; three until then), so the starter tree is the dexterity stone alone, in slot "0" straight down (my pick of the three: the slot that already was "0"). Nothing grants the root more yet.
- **No starter stone** (2026-10-10): the root's one slot stands empty for a new hero, so the first point has one place to go, the root, whose light breathes on the skills page until that point is spent -- the tree's tutorial. A save that already holds the dexterity stone keeps it (it may hold points and stones under it); only a new game or a Reset starts empty.
- **The root has no most** (2026-10-09): it takes every point the player has, each +1 damage and +1% increased damage, so a point always has somewhere to go while the tree is small.
- The art is the user's: carved discs with the tier in Roman numerals (`tools/skill_stones.py`, `Assets/Skills/Stones/`), so a stone's tier reads off its face in the bag; the nine capstones keep the old skills' badges.
- **The tree draws small** (2026-10-09, the user's pick A of `tools/qa/skill_nodes_m1.png`): a stone is its base's disc at 16 px with no numeral (the bag keeps the carved 32 px disc, tier and all), with no square or rarity frame, round the user's grey pixellab stone as the root at its whole 32 px; an open slot is the grey stone small. The root was the gem sheet's agate slice until then.
- The skills page shows the slots nobody has filled yet (faint, not pressable), and the root's points on its corner with no most, and the black screen's builder stands the stones beside the tree, the tree in the doll's place.

## Why it is built this way
- **A slot is a path, not a stone.** "1.2.0" names a place: the third connector's first, under the root's second branch. A swap replaces what stands at a path and renames nothing, so ranks, the save and the view all key on the same string, and "what falls off a swap" is every key under the path whose next step is past the new stone's connectors.
- **The root is not an item.** It never moves, never drops, never takes an orb, and would need an icon in `Assets/Gear`, a pool deep enough for the item tests and a branch in every rule that asks "is this a stone". As consts (`ROOT_DAMAGE`, `ROOT_PERCENT`) it is one line in the flat and percent sums, its points under the path `""` like any stone's, so the spending, the reset and the save need nothing of their own -- only the loops over the stones step past it (`Skills._stones_held`).
- **The tree's attributes are never multiplied by `skill_worth`.** The Sage's Abacus makes the worth a function of intelligence; if the worth multiplied the tree's intelligence, the Abacus would read itself. Flat and percent lines are multiplied; attributes are added with the gear's.
- **Stones are `LootTable.KINDS` rows of weight 0**, so the gear's draw, the shelves and the bounties pass them by the way they pass the Broken Sword by. They drop on a roll of their own in `Encounter._kill`, off by default (`stone_drops`) so every test that counts drops counts what it always did, and on from the first sword's fall -- a stone before it would spend that promise -- and since 2026-10-10 (the user's) none at all until the promised first has fallen: a common Strength Node with every connector in kills 151-170 (`SkillTree.first`, `Encounter.PROMISED`; `Scenes/DESIGN.md`, *A new player's first kills are scripted*).
- **A node's lines read their bands at its item level, exactly as gear's do** (the user's, 2026-10-09). Until then its tier lifted them three levels a tier, the way a plus does, for "higher tier, better rolls" -- and since a line's tier is drawn from T1 up to that level, weighted to the top, a level-3 tier-III node rolled mostly T8-T12 lines (and up to 24 ranks) while its card said level 3. A higher tier is better now by the lines it unlocks (`LINES`) and by the deeper land it comes from.
- **The view is a radial**, as the user drew it: each slot's children split its wedge of the circle, the root's a third each at the most (`SkillTreeView.BRANCH_WEDGE`), so the one branch hangs below the root as it did when there were three and none of its lines cross the root. A slot's children split its wedge by how many slot ends hang off each, not evenly: split evenly, every ring had a third of the angle the one before had, so the radius tripled a ring, and past the 4th wall (tier 7-9 stones, 7-9 deep) the tree ran to tens of thousands of pixels. A ring stands a gap past the last, and further only where two slots side by side on it would come within a gap; that radius is worked out, not searched for. Pushed out two pixels at a time with every pair checked, as it first was, a tree of 100-200 stones took 0.5-2 s to lay out, on every point spent (2026-10-09). A tree too big for its room is drawn a whole window pixel a pixel smaller (`fit`), never a fraction, so the stones stay as sharp as the rest of the game; past one window pixel it scrolls.
- **The stones are `ItemSlot`s**, so the card under the cursor, the frames, the glint, an orb's light on the card and the cursor's hand are the bag's, written once.
- **The builder is `BagPage` in a stones mode**, the way the smith's counter puts something else in the doll's place: the bag's grid, selection, orb tray and crafting are the page's own, and the tree stands where the doll does. The black screen writes nothing until the world is left, as it always has.

## Dials and calls the user has not ruled on
- The dials: `STONE_SHARE` 0.25, `CAPSTONE_SHARE` 0.05, the tiers by land level (the user's, 2026-10-09: tier N lasts N levels -- 1, 2-3, 4-6, 7-10, which I carried on as 11-15, 16-21, 22-28, 29-36 and IX from 37), `CONNECTOR_WEIGHTS` 1/5/3/1 (the user's, 2026-10-09), a stone's base attribute 5, `added_stone_ranks` at `RANKS_WEIGHTS` 12 down to 2, its band tiered like any line's but written by the tier -- 1 to 2t at Tt (`per_tier`, the user's, 2026-10-09), Mastery 6/10/15/21 stones. All first guesses.
- **The pools are a list, not a gate** (`SkillTree.LINES`). Gear's pools are worked out from what a piece has or may carry (`affixes`, `globals`); a stone has nothing but its attribute, and "which tier unlocks this" is a number a line needs anyway, so each base names its lines by id with that number. A line is on one base only, so a base means something: strength is damage, armour, block, bleed, crit damage and the big targets; dexterity is attack speed, dodge, time, tempo and the fight clock; intelligence is crit chance, the finders, experience, burn and the wards against the land. Left off every stone: the PERCENT lines (a stone has nothing of its own to scale; the GLOBAL line is its form), all attributes (`Skills.attributes` reads the three, so it would count for nothing), a helmet's ranks on a base (`Inventory._tree` reads gear) and a count of enemies (signed and whole: three ranks of "-1 enemy" is a fight of none).
- Which tier unlocks which line is my first draft (2026-10-09), all dials: tier I is each base's plain lines and its attribute (dexterity's global attack speed among them, attack speed having no flat form and the starter carrying it); the base's global damage, double strike and less health come last, at IX. With `TIER_EVERY` 5 and a tile level of about 3 a circle, a run that stalls at the third wall sees tier II at most.
- The capstones' lines are the old capstone skills' stats, and are not tier-gated.
- Unruled since the root's one connector: the Specialist curse (one branch) refuses nothing while there is only one branch, and still pays its half again.
- My calls: capstones stay their 32 px badge in the tree (at 16 px they are mush); the root's card writes its totals (a tooltip until 2026-10-10); a capstone carries its base's attribute; two of one capstone give its effect once; stones fall from the first sword on; a swap is the only way to move a stone; hold-to-learn and the old skill card's status line are gone; the smith works on stones as on gear; with a small tree most of a level's points sit unspent until the tree grows.
