# Main scene (`Scenes/main_scene.gd`) and rules that span several folders

`Scenes/main_scene.gd` has the exported `world_seed` and `map_seed` (0 = random; the used seeds are printed), `map_origin`, `zoom` (3 by default, whole numbers only so sprite pixels stay square) and `ui_scale` (2). It prints the clicked tile's environment weights and town connections, and builds the UI in code, so the scene file stays untouched while the editor has it open: a side panel flush against the right edge, running the full window height and hidden until a tile is selected — a green title bar with the title and an X at its right end, over a cream body holding one row per environment on the tile (a 16 px swatch cut from that environment's own hex-sheet tile, plus its percentage), a "Chart", a "Move here" and a "Farm" button pinned to the bottom, each shown only while it can be pressed — charting works on any tile beside a charted one (a player not next to it walks to the nearest such tile first, `_chart_target`, and the fight opens on arrival) and walks you onto it, and "Move here" goes back to any tile already charted (the camera follows until the player arrives), and "Farm" opens an endless run on a tile already taken, and an X top-right that closes the panel and calls `HexMap.deselect()`. Opposite it, **two square brown icon buttons** in a row under the character panel in the top-left corner (`_icon_button`, `CORNER_GAP`): a chest and a star, each a mark on the pack's brown face with no words on it. They come and go together (`_show_corner`) because what takes them away is never about one of them. The star opens the **Skills** page: the free points over the two trees side by side, each a `SkillTreeView` with a Reset button under it wearing a coin and the price (disabled when nothing is spent or the purse is short). Clicking a skill spends a point and saves; hovering one puts a `SkillCard` **beside the page** rather than over the tree it describes. `_refresh_skills` redraws it from the inventory, and opening the page calls it, because levels and gold both move while it is shut. The page is height-bound: five rows, the title, the points and both resets have to fit a 648 px window at `ui_scale` 2, which is what `SkillTreeView.GAP_Y` is set by. Both pages stand against the left edge, so they share it: `_close_left_pages` and `_open_left_page` are the one place that knows which pages those are, and opening either puts the other away. The chest opens the bag: the same titled panel against the left edge, holding every item the world has handed over as tan squares straight on the cream body — the pack puts nothing between the two, so neither does this — each ringed in its rarity's colour above common. How full it is and what is in the purse share **one line at the top** -- the two things true of the whole bag rather than of anything in it, read in one glance, which also leaves the foot of the panel to the orb tray, which needs the width. The bag is **sectioned by level, highest first**, and inside a level sorted by rarity and then newest first; how full it is stands over the lot as `n / 40`, in `Palette.RUST` once it is, and the **orb tray is a footer at the very bottom**, after both of the two things that expand to fill the panel's height -- so it stands under whichever of them is up, the grid or an open stat block, and never scrolls out of sight, least of all while a piece is open, which is exactly when it is being used. It is a footer rather than a section because orbs are not sorted, filtered or chosen between, so it wants no heading and no buttons; `_refresh_orbs` is its own call beside `_refresh_gold`, because a kill moves one count and nothing else and there are ten or fifteen kills to a fight. **The tray has two states, and the bag decides which** -- the same resting-state-and-context pairing the character sheet beside it uses for its doll and its comparison. At rest it is what the player holds, every owned orb lit. With a piece open it answers a different question: the orbs that can do something to *that* piece stay lit and the rest go grey, and clicking a lit one spends it there and then. So there is no crafting row in the stat block and no arming step -- the thing already on screen becomes the thing you press. `_on_orb_pressed` applies first and spends second, so an orb that turns out to have nothing to do is never consumed, and because crafting adds nothing to the bag and takes nothing out of it the selection survives: `_refresh_bag` reopens the same index and the block simply says something new. `_orb_card` hovers **above** the square rather than beside it, which is the one direction there is room in -- one side of the tray is the screen edge and the other is the character sheet, which is also why it is added to the layer **after** the sheet: it overhangs that page for five of the eight orbs, and tree order is the whole of what decides which of the two the player sees -- and it carries `UITheme.theme()` itself, because a type variation means nothing to a Control with no themed ancestor. It is a **coin and a number**, not the word: `Coins.icon()` in front of the figure, which reads at the glance a footer is given rather than at the reading it is not. Each section's heading carries **Auto** and **Clear**: Clear throws away what is held at that level, Auto stops any more of it arriving. They are two buttons rather than one because a player who is finished with level 3 from now on has not necessarily finished with the three they are holding. A level with a rule on it and nothing in it keeps its heading, which is the only place the rule can be turned off again, and the rule is said in the heading in a word and a colour rather than left to the toggle's pressed face — the pack presses a button by drawing it a pixel lower, which is right for a press you are watching and far too quiet for a state you are reading off four headings at once. The character sheet is a **separate panel standing outside it**, against its right edge: the pack's own wood page next to its cream one, with the sides swapped so what you carry is on the left and what you wear is on the right, and no title bar of its own because the pack gives the spread one over the other page. It opens, closes and hides with the bag, and it has **two states**, decided by what the bag is showing rather than by anything it remembers itself. With nothing selected it is the doll: the pack's silhouette with the eight sockets laid **over the body they belong to** — helmet on the head, offhand on the shield arm, weapon on the sword arm, body on the chest, boots on the feet, and the jewellery in a row beneath, where a body says nothing about where a ring goes. The sockets are translucent (`ItemRarity.SOCKET_ALPHA`), which is the only thing that makes an overlay work: an opaque square would hide the very part it points at, and the pack washes its own sockets over its figure for the same reason. An empty ring or amulet socket carries the pack's faint mark for it. `DOLL_SOCKETS` is measured off the sprite and placed by hand — no container expresses "over the shield hand" — and `test_inventory` checks no two sockets overlap and none runs off the page. `DOLL_SCALE` is set by that layout rather than by taste: head, chest and feet are 17 and 15 sprite pixels apart, so a 40 px square needs at least 2.7 to clear its neighbours, and whole numbers only, for the reason `zoom` is. With a bag item selected the **comparison takes the doll's place**: the piece that item would replace, standing beside that item's own block, at `WORN_WIDTH` (the bag's own width, so the two columns wrap alike). The socket is `Equipment.sockets_for(item)[0]`, exactly the one the Equip button targets, so the page shows precisely what pressing Equip would take off, and rings and the shield-or-torch offhand are settled without a second rule. The panel's own face changes with the state — the pack's wood page under the doll, its cream one under a stat block, because the darker half of the rarity ramp was picked to be read on white and vanishes into wood — and `_layout_ui` measures and re-centres it each time, twice, for the reason the tile panel is measured twice. Unequip from the comparison keeps the bag's selection open, finding the piece again by identity because what comes back into the bag moves every index after it; Unequip on an opened socket goes back to the doll as it always did. Clicking a square, or a filled socket, puts that item's stat block in the grid's place — name and rarity in that rarity's colour, then what the piece is worth and what it rolled — with Equip and **Discard** over a Back button, or Unequip over one. The block says what the piece **is** and nothing about what it would replace: the comparison is the worn piece standing whole beside it, and a second one written out as signed numbers under the stats said the same thing twice in two different languages, so a selected piece and an equipped one now read exactly alike wherever either is shown. The lines scroll and the buttons under them do not, so a long piece can never push Equip off the bottom of the window. A worn piece has no Discard of its own — the cap is on the bag alone, so nothing presses the player to destroy what they are wearing — and a full bag greys Unequip rather than destroying something to make room for a piece being looked at. Only ever one of the two is open: they share the space. The squares on the panel at the end of a fight open the same way, in the verdict's place. The grid scrolls by wheel and by dragging it, and never shows a scrollbar; a press that travels is a drag and one that does not is a click, the same rule `HexMap` uses to tell a pan from a tile click. Both buttons and every left-hand page go while a fight is on — a `Control` left in that corner would take the mouse before the fight saw it and quietly eat the player's swings. `inventory_path` and `map_path` are exported beside the seeds so tests can point them away from the real saves. The map is written at the end of `_ready` (the world is decided the moment it is generated), on every arrival (the one place the map's own state changes) and in `_exit_tree`; a refused save blocks all three. A save that cannot be honoured builds no map at all and puts up a panel saying so, naming the file.

## Rules and gotchas
- **Orbs follow gold, with one difference: gold is one number and orbs are eight.** A charting fight banks each orb as it lands and a run pouches its own in `_fight_orbs` until it ends, exactly as the gold beside it -- and `_bank_farm_loot` banks them **above** its empty-pouch early return for the same reason the gold is banked there, because a run that turned up currency and no gear has still earned its way. Nothing can refuse an orb either: the bag's cap has nothing to say about a count, no autodiscard rule filters it, and there is no Discard for it.
- **Gold follows loot, with one difference: nothing can refuse it.** A charting fight banks each purse as it lands and a run pouches its own in `_fight_gold` until it ends, exactly as their finds are handled and for exactly the same reasons. But a purse is a number rather than a square, so the bag's cap has nothing to say about it, an autodiscard rule never filters it, and there is no Discard for it -- a run that threw every find away still earned its way. It is also the half that is never written as a word: the coin stands for it in both places it is said, and `_show_coins` throws it out of the body so the one thing that always drops is the one thing that was never drawn. Which is also why `_bank_farm_loot` banks the gold **before** its empty-pouch early return.
- **Farm loot belongs to the run, not the kill:** a charting fight writes each drop to disk as it lands (`main_scene._on_loot_dropped`), because it is over in half a minute and closing the game mid-fight must not cost a find. A run could go an hour and has no end of its own, so its finds wait in `_fight_drops` and go into the bag in one write when it ends. Both ways out bank -- `_on_combat_finished` and `_exit_tree` -- and `_banked` is what keeps the second from repeating the first.
- **Monsters outgrow gear on purpose.** Health climbs `HP_GROWTH` per *hex step* while gear climbs `LEVEL_GROWTH` per *level*, and levels arrive in bands that widen -- so the gear exponent grows with the square root of the distance and the health exponent with the distance itself. The player is ahead through the early map and falls behind past the middle distance. Since the clock came down to 30 seconds the frontier is past what a **plain** set of commons can beat, and that is the design rather than a regression: reaching it means farming for better rolls. Farming cannot raise the level a tile drops at — that ceiling is the tile's own — so what a long run buys is modifiers. `test_combat._test_a_won_fight` pins all three ends of that: the first ring stays beatable bare-handed, a plain set at the far edge must need **8 clicks a second or more** (it is out of reach), and a farmed set of rares must bring it under 8 and stay over 1 (not a walkover). `HP_GROWTH`, `LEVEL_GROWTH` and `LEVEL_FLAT` are the dials, and `Encounter.SECONDS` is now one too — over a third of a 30-second fight is `WALK_IN` and `DEATH` animation, so shortening those is the other way to give a fight back its time.
- **Only the offensive stats and drop rate are wired up:** `damage`, `crit_chance`, `crit_damage` and `attack_speed` are read by `Encounter` and decide what a blow is worth; `drop_rate` is read by the same `arm()` and handed to `LootTable.roll`, where it lifts the chance a body leaves anything, capped where that chance has always been capped. The skill-only stats are read there too: `item_rarity` goes to `ItemRarity.roll`, `orb_find` to `OrbTable.roll` (capped like drop rate), and `gold_find` lifts a purse at the death site rather than inside `gold_of`, which stays what the body is worth. The capstone skills' effects are not stats: `main_scene` sets `Encounter.effects` from `Skills.effects()` beside `guarantee_elite`, and `_strike` / `_advance_phase` read them; Jackpot and Transmute draw from `loot_rng` / `orb_rng` only while learned, so a player without them rolls exactly as before. Every other stat is rolled, saved and shown and does nothing. Nothing can hurt the player — the clock is still the only way to lose — so armour, health, block, dodge and the resistances have nothing to defend against, and giving them a job means giving the player a health pool first.
- **An autodiscarded find is filtered in exactly one place, `CombatScene._on_loot_dropped`, and the fight is what everything downstream believes.** The main scene no longer listens to `Encounter.loot_dropped` at all; a second listener applying the rule a second way is how the counter, the pouch and the bag would come to disagree about what a run found.
- **A comparison is against one socket, and it is the socket Equip targets.** `_show_item` and the character sheet both ask `Equipment.sockets_for(item)[0]`, so what Equip would take off is exactly the piece shown beside the one being judged. A ring compares against the emptiest finger, which is the one it would actually go on. The comparison is that piece and only that piece — the stat block is the same block for a bag item and a worn one. `ItemDetails.fill` still takes an `against` and `ItemDetails.deltas` still works it out, tested on its own; nothing in the bag passes one.
- **The character sheet only changes while something is selected.** The doll is the page's resting state and is untouched — same silhouette, same hand-placed sockets, same wood panel, same centring. The comparison is what is shown *instead of* it for as long as a bag square is open, and closing the square brings the doll straight back.
- **A square in the bag takes no mouse input at all** (`MOUSE_FILTER_IGNORE`): the grid sits in a `ScrollContainer` that has to see every press to tell a drag from a click, and a square that swallowed the press would break every drag that started on one — which is most of them. Which square was clicked is worked out from where the cursor is.
- **A seed written into the scene wins over a save of another world.** 0 means "carry on"; non-zero means "that world, now", and so does a changed `map_origin`. It is a developer knob, which is why every test and screenshot script redirects `map_path` -- they all pin a seed, so without it they would replace the player's own map.
