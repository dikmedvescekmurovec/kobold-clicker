## Map code (`Scenes/Map/`)
| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `hex_grid.gd` (`HexGrid`) | Pointy-top offset grid with odd rows shifted right (same as the TileMapLayers). `Edge` enum (E, SE, SW, W, NW, NE) and `EDGES`, `neighbor`, `neighbors`, `opposite`, `distance`, `corners`, and the edge bitmask pair `edge_mask` / `mask_edges` that roads are described with |
| `sheet_meta.gd` (`SheetMeta`) | Reads `AI-sprites/spritesheet/hex_tileset.json` once per run and hands out its `meta` block, so nothing restates the rules the sprites were drawn to. `env_adjacency()` is the adjacency table, with each environment's self-entry stripped |
| `hex_tileset.gd` (`HexTileset`) | Builds the TileSet at runtime from `AI-sprites/spritesheet/hex_tileset.json` (56×64 tiles). `atlas_coords`, `road_name`, `blend_name`, `env_rank`, `can_border`, `opaque_mask` |
| `hex_map.tscn` / `hex_map.gd` (`HexMap`) | GroundLayer, one `Blend_<env>` layer per spreading environment (created in `_ready`), RoadLayer, Highlight. `set_ground` redraws the blends of the cell and its neighbors, reading the environment of cells that aren't drawn yet through the `hidden_env` Callable, so a tile looks the same however late it is charted. `get_tile_info` includes `blends` and `environments` (visible pixel share per environment). Owns the `fog` overlay and the `player` token. Signals `tile_hovered`, `tile_clicked`, and `dragged` (mouse movement while dragging, for the owner of the camera). A click selects a tile; a press that travels more than `DRAG_THRESHOLD` pans instead. `set_player_cell` moves the player token |
| `player_token.gd` (`PlayerToken`) | The player's character, an `AnimatedSprite2D` with an "idle" and a "run" animation from `Assets/Player`. The sheets' frames are wide (room for the attack swing), so one `BOUNDS` crop, the union over both sheets, cuts every frame down to the character; sharing it keeps the character still when the animation changes. Drawn at `SCALE` (half size). `walk(path)` runs it from tile to tile at `SECONDS_PER_TILE`, `advance(delta)` steps that on (tests drive it directly), `finish_walk()` jumps to the end, and `arrived` fires on the last tile. Kicks up dust every `DUST_EVERY` while walking, from `_process` so tests driving `advance` make none. `HexMap` creates it in code, after the highlight, so it draws on top |
| `fog_overlay.gd` (`FogOverlay`) | The translucent grey hex drawn over every tile that is seen but not charted. A removed cell lifts over `LIFT_TIME` (`_lifting`, drawn only -- `has_cell` is false at once). `HexMap` creates it in code, between the roads and the outlines |
| `ambient.gd` (`Ambient`) | Weather and time of day over the map, added under `HexMap` by the main scene so it hides with it during a fight. One sparse world-space `CPUParticles2D` following the camera, retuned per environment from `WEATHER` on arrival (`set_env`) -- kept to a handful of particles on purpose -- and a `CanvasModulate` cycling to `NIGHT` over `DAY_LENGTH` -- it tints only the map's canvas, never a `CanvasLayer` |
| `hex_highlight.gd` | Hover and selection outlines: a bright band with a dark ink border, readable on every terrain |
| `environment_generator.gd` (`EnvironmentGenerator`) | Random-frontier growth under `allowed()`, the sheet's `env_adjacency`: prefers neighbors, boosts regions under `MIN_REGION_SIZE`, damps regions over `MAX_REGION_SIZE`, then merges small regions, every pick and merge leaning towards the environments the new land has least of (see **Even environments**). `extend()` grows an existing map into new cells without touching the old ones (they are frozen against merging), which is how the map enlarges. Tunables are at the top |
| `town_world.gd` (`TownWorld`) | 256×256 world of towns (small 1%, medium 0.5%, fortress 0.1%, never on neighboring spots). Each town links to its 1/2/4 nearest towns (small/medium/fortress) within 20 steps, in both directions. `to_dict`/`from_dict` are the map save's copy of the whole world -- towns as flat `[x, y, tier]` against a legend of tier **names**, links once per pair with the lower spot first, and `_buckets` rebuilt rather than stored. It is written down rather than regenerated because "a pure function of its seed" is a property of this code today and not a promise to the player |
| `road_network.gd` (`RoadNetwork`) | Routes roads along the town links with A*, using only shapes the sprites have (straight, 120° curve, 3/4/6-way). Roads stop at town edges and never cross a town tile. Terrain-independent, so a world always gives the same roads. `extend()` adds the links a window brings into reach, routing each inside its own box (around its two towns), so a road never moves once laid — growing the map only ever adds edges, sometimes turning a drawn road into a junction |
| `map_builder.gd` (`MapBuilder`) | Generates the map (the hexagon of `land_radius` round cell (0, 0), which the camera centers on screen, its ice wall and `WASTE_DEPTH` rings of wasteland -- see **The ice wall** below) and draws it as the player uncovers it. `rect` is what the map has generated: it grows only when the wall falls (`_break_wall` -> `_cover`), generating the new land, its tiles and the roads it brings into reach, and redrawing the blends and roads of drawn tiles the new land touches. Everything already generated stays exactly as it is. Every cell is in one `State`: `HIDDEN` (the fog of war, nothing drawn), `UNCHARTED` (drawn under the grey fog, can't be walked to) or `CHARTED`. `create()` generates everything and draws `start_cells()`: the charted center, where the player starts, ringed by six uncharted tiles. `chart(cell, sight := 1)` takes the grey off a tile next to any charted one (`can_chart`; `chart_from` is the charted neighbour the player is fewest steps from), draws the land within `sight` steps of it as uncharted, and -- unless they already stand on it, having walked onto it to fight for it (`walk_onto`) -- sends the player walking onto it. One ring is what a player carrying nothing sees; a torch is the only thing that widens it, and it is read once, by the caller, at the moment the tile is taken — so what a tile showed when it was charted is what it showed, whatever is worn afterwards. Both it and `scour` go through `_reveal_around`, there being one answer to "take the fog off everything within n steps that the map has generated and the player has not seen". `name_of(cell)` is what the tile is called: `_show` names it the first time the player lays eyes on it -- uncharted land is land they can see, so seeing it is the encounter -- and the name is kept in `_names` and written into the save. It is stored rather than worked out again even though `TileNames.generate` is a pure function of the seed and the cell, for the reason the town world is written down: a place somebody has fought over must not be renamed by a later build widening a word list. `level_of(cell)` is the tile's level, in even bands of `LEVEL_TILES` rings (level `n` starts at `first_step(n)`; see **Walls on level edges**) -- what the side panel shows and the ceiling on what can drop there. `area_variant(cell)` says which battle backdrop the tile fights on -- its town tier if it has one, else `road` where a road runs, else `plain`. `can_farm(cell)` is any charted tile that is not a settlement, with no walk under way -- farming asks nothing about where the player stands, because a run is a thing you choose to do rather than a step you take; and a town is fought for once and then visited, never farmed or camped on, or its counters would stand beside an endless hunt. `move_to(cell)` sends the player walking to a charted tile over `route_to(cell)`, a shortest path of charted tiles (`can_move_to` refuses anything else, and anything at all while `walking`); arriving emits `arrived(cell)` and charts nothing by itself. `reveal_all()` charts the lot. `origin` is the world spot at cell (0, 0): generated environments, plus that environment's town sprite where the world has a town. Before drawing, it clears any town within `START_TOWN_DISTANCE` (5) of the center and guarantees a small town exactly that far out, which a road links to cell (0, 0); that town's world spot is `start_town`. `_spot(cell)` is the crossing from map cells to world spots. `to_save()` and `restore()` are the map save's two ends: `restore` is the mirror of `create` and shorter than it, because the saved town world already has the clearing and the start town in it and the start road is among the saved roads, so it generates nothing and touches no town. It draws through `_draw_saved`, which lays every ground and road down first and refreshes each cell's blends once, rather than through `_show` -- `set_ground` refreshes a cell **and its six neighbours**, which is seven times the work when a whole map arrives at once |
| `tile_names.gd` (`TileNames`) | What a tile is called: a made-up proper word (a head syllable and a tail one, five hundred pairs) and a feature word taken from the environment, or from the settlement's tier where there is one -- "Bralmere Keep" says more about a fortress than "Bralmere Meadow" does. `generate` is a pure function of the map seed and the cell, under its own `"name"` tag, so what a place is called has nothing to do with which sprite it is drawn with or which backdrop it fights on. It names neither `MapBuilder` nor `TownWorld` -- the caller passes the environment and the tier's name -- so it is a table and a dice roll and nothing else |
| `map_save.gd` (`MapSave`) | The explored map at `user://map.json`: the seeds, the origin, the window, the whole town world, the land, the roads, the names of the tiles the player has seen, and what they have uncovered and where they stand. Version 2 added the names, written out as a plain `"x,y"` object rather than as rows against a legend -- a name is a whole word per cell and only the cells that have been looked at have one, so there is nothing for the alphabet to index; a version 1 save simply has none and its tiles are named again as they are asked about. `_tiles` and the fog are left out, being derived. The environments and the states are **one string per map row, one character per cell**, each indexing a legend written beside it -- which keeps the save small, keeps enum positions off the disk, and makes the env rows read as a picture of the map. `fingerprint(tileset)` is the SHA-256 of the three spritesheet tables a saved map is *read through* (`env_adjacency`, `blend_priority`, `legal_road_masks`) and nothing else, so a rebuilt sheet that only adds a variant costs nobody their map. `load_from` returns **two different nulls**: an empty `problem` is a first run, and a reason in it is a refusal. Never names `MapBuilder` -- that is the cycle Godot's checker bites on, so the states are raw ints here and `STATE_NAMES` is the legend |

**The ice wall.** The land is a hexagon of radius 10 round cell (0, 0), and ring 11 is a wall of ice the player has to fight through: one body (`Encounter.for_wall`), far tougher than anything its ring would field, so it is a wall in both senses -- the edge of the map and the edge of what the player can do yet. Beating any one tile of it brings the **whole ring** down: the land reaches 20, and a new wall stands at 21 (then 31, ...). One gate per tier, like a stage boss, rather than a gap through one tile, because a hole would leave the rest of the ring as scenery and make the next stretch a corridor. Past the wall is a frozen wasteland: the player can see it (sight, torches and scours reveal it like any land), but it is snow with nothing under it, so the land beyond cannot be read, farmed, posted as a bounty or hold a chest before it is earned. The land out there is generated all the same (`WASTE_DEPTH` rings), for two reasons: the wall's own tiles blend against what is really beyond, so nothing on screen changes when it thaws, and sight cannot outrun generated land. The wall replaced edge-growth (`expand_if_needed`, the 20x11 `START_RECT` growing by 10x5 near an edge): the map now grows only when the wall falls. Saves from before the wall (version 2 and older, no `land_radius`) are **migrated rather than refused**: the wall goes on the first ring, counted the way walls fall (11, 21, 31...), past the farthest tile the player has seen, so nothing they had found is iced over. The ice and the snow are generated art (`AI-sprites-generator/ice_wall.py`, 2026-09-25, see **The wall's art** below). The wall's body in the fight is its own still picture (`Assets/Enemies/The Wall`). **How hard it is** is `Encounter.WALL_HP` -- 33 now; the history follows. It was first set to 50: the first wall is meant to fall on the first day, but only after a lot of farming. At the 6 it shipped with it fell in about 350 kills and 13 minutes, in common gear, the moment the Power tree reached Giant Slayer -- the wall is a boss -- so it checked a level and no gear. `tests/balance_wall.gd` plays a fresh player out to it: at 50 about 2100 kills and 50 minutes of fighting at five clicks a second, as a ceiling (that player ignores Fortune, orbs, vendors, the smith and uniques). The dial is a cliff past that (60 is 7000 kills, 100 is 30000), because the Power tree is full at level 24 and drops top out at the farmed tile's level, leaving rarity as the only thing still growing. Turned down: a wall Giant Slayer does nothing to, which plays out exactly as twice the figure and costs a special case; and damage that stays on the wall between attempts, which would flatten the cliff but make it a check on time rather than power -- the thing to reach for if 50 plays as a brick. **Played, 50 felt twice too strong** (2026-09-19), so it is 33: about 850 kills and 25 minutes at five clicks a second, half the time. There is a cliff right behind it -- 34 is already 1600 kills and 36 minutes, 25 to 32 all sit near 550 -- so move it a point at a time and rerun the table. **Every wall fallen multiplies the next by `WALL_GROWTH`** -- first 10, now 2.8 (see the next paragraph): the second wall is meant to be near impossible without transcending (not built yet), and on the dial alone it was not -- the played-out player held ring 20 inside two hours and had it down to 5 clicks a second after 60000 kills, or about 5000 with a Berserker's Band, a home piece and the Glass Edge on, their damage summing to five times. At 10 that same stack still wants about 15 clicks a second after twenty hours, and a transcend need only be worth five to ten times the player to open it. Turned down: 30, which asks a first transcend for thirty times or the wall never falls.

**The land past a wall steps by ten, the wall by 2.2** (2026-09-27, the user's ruling: "wall HP is good, but monster HP should grow 10x on the first tile after the wall is felled"). `Encounter.LAND_GROWTH` (10) is the bodies' step and `WALL_GROWTH` (2.2) is kept for the walls and for the size of a blow, so a body past a wall is tougher without hitting harder. Ring 12's common is 510 health where it was 112. Measured on the day: in the farmed rares the first ring past the wall wants 21 clicks a second on gear alone and 3.5 with the whole Power tree; played with the enemies striking and the whole tree, it is won at six clicks a second and lost without the set's defence up to ten. The wall stayed exactly as it was (44.6 clicks a second in farmed rares), so a felled wall now opens land that has to be grown into rather than walked across.

**Both wall dials came down with the weapons' damage step** (2026-09-27). `LootTable.LEVEL_FLAT`'s damage went from a whole point a level to 0.4, because the first weapon off an elite one-shot the first rings (`Scenes/Items/DESIGN.md`). That took about two fifths off a farmed set's damage at item level 5-6, so the dials were moved until `balance_wall.gd` read as before, not re-decided: `WALL_HP` 33 -> 28 (a fresh player at 5 clicks a second through the first wall in 994 kills and 26.5 minutes, against 1060 and 27.3; the sweep's cliff is still there, 30 is already 1200) and `WALL_GROWTH` 2.8 -> 2.2 (the first ring past the wall wants 3.2 clicks a second of the farmed rares, against 3.3, and the second wall asks the played-out player 22.8 / 15.6 / 9.8 clicks a second at 5k / 20k / 60k kills, against 22.0 / 15.8 / 10.4). So the second wall is about 335K now, a wall eleven and a half times the last; the "half a million" below is what it was when that paragraph was written.

**The second wall is half a million** (2026-09-20). Asked for after the first was played: `WALL_GROWTH` 10 put the wall on ring 21 at 1.79e6, and the played-out farmer still wanted 30 clicks a second at it after 400,000 kills -- a first transcend would have had to be worth six times the player. At **2.8** the second wall is 502,128 (the first untouched at 34,056, the third 7.4e6, each wall about fifteen times the last once the band's own `HP_GROWTH ^ 10` is counted), and that same farmer wants 6.9 -- so a transcend worth a few times the player opens it, which is the window `WALL_GROWTH` was always aimed at. `WALL_HP` did not move: the first wall is the day's gate that was tuned by playing it, and scaling the ladder there would have given that back. What 2.8 costs is the band a wall opens. The step is still what keeps it from dying to one click, but it is the gentler half now: a wall is crossed with about fourteen times what the new band's first ring asks rather than four, so the ring past a fallen wall wants 2.0 clicks a second in the farmed rares that broke the wall (it wanted 8+) and none at all with the whole Power tree. The band's own ten rings grow it back into a frontier; the wall, not the land behind it, is the check -- which is what `test_combat` pins now (`rate > 1.0` rather than `> 8.0`, and the cliff test reads the wall's own body against a band's walk, since a 2.8 step is smaller than the 4.4 a band walks).

**The land past a wall steps with it** (2026-09-20). Played, everything behind a fallen wall died to one click: the wall had been the only thing `WALL_GROWTH` touched. The numbers say why. A wall is `WALL_HP` over a boss body -- HUGE and BOSS, an `EnemyRoster.hp_modifier` of 24 -- so about 930 commons of the ring inside it, in one minute; clearing a tile on that ring is some twelve commons in thirty seconds. So felling a wall proves about **forty times** the damage a second the land inside it asks for, while a whole band of ten rings only grows by `HP_GROWTH ^ 10`, about five. The band a wall opened could never be a fight again, in that world or any world after a transcension. So `WALL_GROWTH` moved out of `Encounter.for_wall` and into `Encounter.base_hp`, where **every wall behind a cell multiplies its bodies**: `walls_inside(cell)` counts none of a wall's own ring, so the step lands on the first ring past a wall and every wall's health is exactly what it was (ring 11 34,056; ring 21 1.79e6 -- `for_wall` needs no growth term now, because its ring's bodies carry it). The shape that buys: a wall is crossed with roughly four times what the new band's first ring wants, the band's own curve eats that over ten rings, and the next wall is again the same forty-times check -- every band the same shape as the first, which is what makes one wall a tier gate rather than the last one. Measured with `tests/balance_wall.gd`: everything up to the first wall is untouched (33 is still 1076 kills and 30 minutes at five clicks a second, the cliff still right behind it at 35), and **the second wall asks exactly what it always asked**, which is the thing to check when this dial moves. The same seeded player walked all the way out wants 99 clicks a second after 5,000 kills where they wanted 253, 80 after 20,000 where they wanted 97, and 39 after 60,000 where they wanted 41 -- the far end unchanged and the early stops easier, because a band worth ten times as much pays its levels and its drops sooner. So a transcend worth five to ten times the player still opens it, the window this was always aimed at. What the step does cost is the clock: 2,092 minutes to 60,000 kills where it was 1,284, a 10x body taking longer to put down. (An earlier note here compared that 39 against a 15 recorded further up this paragraph; the 15 is a player with a Berserker's Band, a home piece and the Glass Edge stacked, not the played-out one, and the two are not the same measurement.)

**Gold and the drop ceiling deliberately do not step with it** (user, 2026-09-20). A body past a wall is ten times the health for the purse it always carried and the level it always dropped at, so the land past a wall is not where gold an hour is won -- a tile behind you pays better per minute, and the frontier is for taking ground. The alternative for the ceiling was a bump to `MapBuilder.level_of` per wall, and matching a tenfold jump in health would take twenty levels of `LootTable.LEVEL_GROWTH` a wall (level 5 to 25 to 45), which would undo the wall as a check on power and drag the smith's cap, vendor shelves, experience and every price along with it. Gear falling behind the walk is the design (`Scenes/CLAUDE.md`, "Monsters outgrow gear on purpose") and transcension is the answer to it.

**Even environments** (2026-10-03). The user asked whether a random map shows every environment equally; it did not. Over 150 first maps the land was 27% mountains, 21% dirt, 20% grass, 14% forest, 10% desert and 9% ice, and ice was missing from the first land of 28% of worlds and desert from 17% -- which keeps their uniques and bounties off the first circle. Nothing in the weights favoured anyone; the adjacency table did. Mountains could border all five others, desert and ice only two each, so wherever two environments that may not meet came near each other the cell between had to be mountains (or dirt or grass), and the merge of small regions handed them to the neighbour allowed next to everything. Tried in the generator alone and turned down: pulling the picks towards the rarest environment (mountains stuck at 19-20% however hard it pulled, because those seams are forced picks it cannot touch), counting only regions big enough to survive the merge (it broke regions into pockets the merge then folded back), and recolouring whole regions after growth (it could only recolour regions with few neighbours, so desert and ice went to the far corners of the generated square). With every pair allowed the generator came out even by itself; the user kept desert away from ice, which leaves those two short (13% and 15%), so `SHARE_PULL` closes the rest: each weight is scaled towards the environment with the fewest cells so far, and the merge prefers the least-used neighbour. The pull moves weight among the neighbours' environments and among the new ones but never from one group to the other, so it changes which environment a cell takes and not how often a region starts -- pulling every weight alike made regions a further 10% smaller. Now each environment covers 16-17% of the generated land and 15-18% of a first map (the rest is the handful of regions one map holds). Regions are about a fifth smaller than under the old table (42 cells against 53 on a first map), which is the table's doing: more borders are allowed, so more cells start one. The new borders needed no art, since a blend is drawn per environment, not per pair; the user approved them from `qa.py blends m_alladj`. Land already generated keeps its old mix; worlds made from now on, and the land a fallen wall uncovers, are even. The sheet's fingerprint changed with the table, so `MapSave.WIDENED_SHEETS` lets maps saved under the old one load.

**Towns are laid out ring by ring** (2026-09-22). `TownWorld.generate` used to roll every spot of the world against `TIER_CHANCES`, knowing nothing of where the map's origin was, so how many settlements a ring of land held was a Poisson draw: ring 0 (331 cells) got 5 give or take 2, one ring in a world had 2 and its neighbour 9, and a fortress -- the only smith -- stood inside the first wall in about one world in three. The user asked for the same average density in every ring and a fortress in each. So the world is laid out round the origin now: the spots of each ring (counted the way `MapBuilder.ring_of` counts, in `WALL_STEP`s from `START_LAND_RADIUS`) are shuffled by the seed and each tier takes `round(spots * chance)` of them, the fortress first and floored at one, never next to another town. `TIER_CHANCES` did not move -- the fortress is floored rather than made likelier, because a smith a band is what the deep map is built on (`Scenes/Town/DESIGN.md`) and three a band would make the walk to one nothing. "Independent of the rendered map" was true only nominally: one origin is ever used and it is saved with the world, so the world may as well know it. No town is generated within `KEEP_OUT` (`START_TOWN_DISTANCE`) of the origin, which is what keeps `create`'s clearing from taking ring 0's fortress away; the guaranteed start village is still added on top. Under the Ring of Walls the five-wide rings deliberately share one fortress per ten, since the land under the extra walls is the same land. A sliver of a ring in the world's corner holds its one fortress and little else, which the test lets pass.

**Treasure chests.** `MapBuilder.has_chest(cell)` is a per-cell roll (`CHEST_CHANCE`, open land only, `CHEST_MIN_DISTANCE` from the start) that holds until the tile is charted -- derived, not saved, since a won tile is charted. `_draw_chest` puts a sprite (`CHEST_TEXTURE`/`CHEST_REGION`) on a seen chest tile under `HexMap.chests`, over the fog. `nearest_chest()` scans every generated cell, so the main scene calls it on arrival and keeps the answer; hands it to `MapPointer.target` (`map_pointer.gd`), a badge that pins itself to the window's edge in that direction. **It stands for as long as the chest does and never ducks out of the way**: it used to hide behind a `covered` Callable while any interface was up, and since the tile panel is up whenever a tile is selected -- which is nearly always -- a bought star was invisible until the next restart. Now it sits at `z_index` -1 on the interface layer, so a panel that happens to lie over it covers that corner of it and nothing more.

**The fog behind the map** (2026-09-25). Hidden cells draw nothing, and what showed there was Godot's grey clear colour, which read as unfinished rather than unknown. It is now thick fog (`backdrop.gdshader`): slow banks of grey-lavender mist, stretched wide the way fog lies, two layers drifting against each other. It is quantised to world pixels and Bayer-dithered so it is as coarse as the tiles at every zoom, and it lives on the map's canvas, so the `Ambient` night dims it with the land. A dark cloud in `Palette.INK`'s purples came first and the user turned it down: it read as a night sky, not fog. Value noise was turned down too: smoothstep flattens its slope along every grid line, so the dithered bands ran in boxes. Other directions turned down: an unfinished parchment chart (desert and wasteland wash out on tan), a whiteout (seen wasteland is already snow, so the wall would stop reading as an edge), stars (the map floats in space). The fog then laps onto the land (`EdgeFog`): every shown tile's edge that faces the unknown gets a band of the same shader, full fog at the edge thinning to none twelve pixels in, the dither and a fine drifting wobble making the edge ragged and alive. Without it the land stopped at a hard hex line and the fog read as a backdrop behind a board rather than something the land sits in -- and the grey mountains round the start sit so close to the fog in value that the hex line was all that separated them. The band starts four pixels outside the hex, because the tile sprites carry light rim pixels right on the line and they showed through as a dotted outline. The fog knows what is shown by being told cell by cell, like the ice and the veil, rather than asking the builder, which keeps it out of Godot's cycle checker.

**The wall's art** (2026-09-25). The wall is a ridge a little wider than a road, laid through wasteland snow along the ring the way a road runs through its tile, rather than a hex of ice: a full hex a cell read as a ring of blocks, however the ice was drawn, and meeting the land on a straight hex line on both sides. The ridge is a row of faceted ice peaks down its centre line, capped by the band's edge, so it rises from the snow on both sides to a crest -- an even roof-shaped profile was tried first and drew as flat stripes, a double-lined road. The peak nearest each tile edge is shared with the tile across it, so the range carries across seams and corners. The snow is not a tile with a shared edge band like the land's: at any size worth seeing, a drift in that shared band came round on every cell and drew a grid across the whole wasteland. It is one field over the map, a sum of cosines whose wavelengths fit a block of 6x4 cells, cut into 24 tiles a cell picks by position. Things lying on it (tracks, rocks, a dead shrub, ice, pebbles, dry grass) are overlays on a quarter of the cells, and the snow drifts onto every land tile beside the ice the way one environment's blend runs onto the next, so the land has no hex line against it either. The user chose the band, fewer drifts and more accents while looking at mockups; `qa.py icewall` redraws them.

**What a fallen wall leaves** (2026-10-07). Once the ring thawed nothing told it from the land either side, so a world's walls left no history on its map. The user picked **rubble** out of six mockups (`qa.py fallen`; the others were sheared-off stumps, a strip of leftover snow, meltwater pools, a dotted line of ice studs and one lone spike a tile): the band's peaks toppled into a broken line of chunks and chips, lying round the peaks' own spots so the seams match the way the peaks did. It is land now, so it sits with the land: on its own layer (`HexMap.rubble_layer`) over the blends and **under the roads and the fog** -- a road runs through it as if cleared, and uncharted rubble is veiled like the ground under it -- where the ice proper sits over the fog, being the edge of what is the player's. A settlement on the ring has none (the village stands in the breach). Its shadows are translucent ink (`hexlib.SHADE`), the one overlay on the map that casts any, because it is laid over every ground and cannot darken the one under it the way a tile's own props do. The snow, white on white, vanished on the ice ground, which is a sixth of the land; the rubble's blue and darker rims read on all six. Under the Ring of Walls the walls stood every `RING_OF_WALLS_STEP`, and a saved map draws its rubble while it loads, so `restore` is told the step rather than having it set afterwards.

## Rules and gotchas
- **Rules come from the JSON meta:** `blend_rule`, `blend_priority` and `env_adjacency`, all read through `SheetMeta` — `HexTileset.can_border` and `EnvironmentGenerator.allowed()` are the same table, so the generator cannot produce a border the sprites can't draw.
- **Map origins must be on even rows**, otherwise world neighbors don't match on-screen neighbors (`MapBuilder` asserts this).
- **Nothing on the map changes, so nothing on it is regenerated.** Both map generators are order-dependent, not seed-dependent: `EnvironmentGenerator.extend` seeds its frontier from the order cells were inserted, and `RoadNetwork._route` routes into the roads already laid and abandons a route that would merge illegally. So the same seed and the same final rect do *not* give the same land -- only replaying the identical sequence of windows would, and that would weld every save to one build of the generator. The town world is written down too, though it is a pure function of its seed today, because that is a property of the current code rather than a promise to the player.
- **A tile is named once, and the name is written down.** `MapBuilder._show` names every tile the moment it comes out of the fog and never again, and `MapSave` carries the names rather than regenerating them on load. `TileNames.generate` being a pure function of the map seed and the cell is a property of today's tables, not a promise to the player -- widening a word list or adding a seventh environment would otherwise rename places somebody has already fought over. The fight's HUD is the one place a name is read, so a rename would be read as a different tile.
- **A save that cannot be honoured stops, and is never written over.** Refusing and then generating a fresh world would destroy the old save on the first arrival, which is the one outcome worse than an error message -- the same reason `Inventory` leaves a newer-version file alone. Only a *missing* file means a new world, which is why `MapSave.load_from` has two different nulls.

## Charting blind (2026-09-21)
The Thick Fog curse takes the player's own ring of sight, so `main_scene._sight()` can be 0. Two things follow, both the user's rule: **a chart with no sight uncovers the tile taken and nothing round it** (`chart` floors at 0, no longer at 1), and **the first tile into the fog can be charted unseen** -- `can_chart` asks for generated land or wall beside a charted tile, seen or not, and never one tile further. It is not a special case for the curse: with a ring of sight or more, every tile beside a charted one has been seen already, so for everyone else the rule is what it was.

`HexMap.can_pick` (set to `can_chart` by the builder) is what lets the mouse land on a cell nothing is drawn on; the tile panel calls it "Unknown land", gives its level (which is only the walk out) and says nothing of what is there, and asking must not name it -- a tile is named as it comes out of the fog. The fight's terrain comes from `env_at`, not from what is drawn. A player can walk into the wall, a settlement's set piece or a mimic blind; that is the cost, and it is what makes the Broken Torch in the offhand a choice between seeing and blocking. The fortuneteller's scour and chest star are then the only ways to see ahead.

## Walls on level edges (2026-09-28)
The user moved the first wall to ring 12 and set the walls 12 apart (`START_LAND_RADIUS` 11, `WALL_STEP` 12; the Ring of Walls halves that to 6), and replaced the widening level bands with even ones of `LEVEL_TILES` (4) rings. The point: beating a wall should put the very next monster at a higher item level. Under the old triangular bands the walls fell mid-band, and a whole circle past the first wall was only two levels deep. Now every wall stands on a band edge (but every other one of the Ring of Walls', which is on ring 18, 30, ...) -- rings 11 and 13 are levels 3 and 4, 23 and 25 are 6 and 7 -- and a circle is three levels: 1-3, 4-6, 7-9, which is what `LootTable.CIRCLE_MATERIAL_LEVEL` gated materials on until the same change replaced it with a material every two tile levels (`TIER_MIN_LEVEL`, `Scenes/Items/CLAUDE.md`). Any ring numbers quoted above this section are the old layout's.

### Rules in full
One paragraph per bullet of the old "Rules and gotchas", under the title of its short rule in `CLAUDE.md`; the other short rules cut from the same bullet point at the same heading.

#### Widen `env_adjacency` only with `WIDENED_SHEETS`
**Widening `env_adjacency` (`terrain.ADJACENT`) changes `MapSave.fingerprint`, and every saved map would be refused:** add the old fingerprint to `WIDENED_SHEETS` against the new one. Narrowing it may not be done that way -- an old map could hold a border the new table forbids.

#### Take generation rules from the sheet's meta
**Generation rules come from the sheet's JSON meta** (`blend_rule`, `blend_priority`, `env_adjacency`) through `SheetMeta`: `HexTileset.can_border` and `EnvironmentGenerator.allowed()` are one table, so the generator cannot make a border the sprites cannot draw.

#### Mirror `MapBuilder`'s rings in `TownWorld`
**`TownWorld.RING`, `FIRST_RING` and `KEEP_OUT` are `MapBuilder.WALL_STEP`, `START_LAND_RADIUS` and `START_TOWN_DISTANCE`, held together by `test_generation`:** TownWorld may not name MapBuilder, which names it. Rings are counted in `WALL_STEP`s whatever the Ring of Walls says, like `Encounter.walls_inside`, so under that curse two of its six-wide rings share one fortress.

#### Put map origins on even rows
**Map origins must be on even rows** (`MapBuilder` asserts it), or world neighbours stop matching on-screen ones.

#### Never regenerate
**Nothing generated is ever regenerated.** Both generators are order-dependent, not seed-dependent, so the same seed and rect do *not* give the same land. Land, roads, the town world and tile names are all written into the save; growing the map only ever adds.

#### Never move a `GOLDEN` digest
**What a seed generates is pinned** (`test_generation`'s `GOLDEN`: SHA-256 digests of two seeds' environments, town world and created map, beside the engine's `hash([...])`, `%s` of a `Vector2i` and a seeded stream, which derived facts and saved keys lean on).
Existing saves hold that land and derive the rest from the same seeds, so a digest that moves means the order or number of rolls, an iteration order, a sort or a `hash` seed moved: put the generator back, never the digest.

#### Name a tile once
**A tile is named once, when it first comes out of the fog (`_show`), and the name is saved.** Do not recompute names on load.

#### Stop on a refused save
**`MapSave.load_from` has two nulls:** empty `problem` is a first run; a reason in `problem` is a refusal, and the caller must stop and never write over the file. **Every value is checked for its shape before it is read** (`TownWorld.is_numbers`, `typeof`): a typed read of the wrong thing stops the function short of its reason, which leaves by the first null.

#### Never name `MapBuilder` in `MapSave`
**`MapSave` never names `MapBuilder`** (Godot's cycle checker): states are raw ints there, with `STATE_NAMES` as the legend.

#### Draw a whole map through `_draw_saved`
**Drawing a whole map goes through `_draw_saved`, not `_show`:** `set_ground` refreshes seven cells per call.

#### Plant the start town in `create` only
**`MapBuilder.create` clears towns within `START_TOWN_DISTANCE` (5) of the centre and guarantees a small one exactly that far out,** linked by road to cell (0, 0). `restore` does none of that: the saved world already has it.

#### Draw a settlement as two pictures
**A settlement is two pictures:** its ground (clearing, plate, shadows) is the `town_<env>_<tier>` tile on GroundLayer, and its buildings are an 84x96 sprite on `HexMap.towns`, bigger than the hex and over the fog, so a town reads across the map and before the land round it is charted.
`MapBuilder._draw_town` puts it down wherever a cell is drawn (`_show`, `_draw_saved`, a wall falling) and only on seen land -- none on the wall or past it.
`TOWN_ANCHOR` is the generator's `towns.ANCHOR`.

#### Derive chests
**Chests are derived, not saved:** `has_chest` is a per-cell roll (open land, `CHEST_MIN_DISTANCE` out) that holds until the tile is charted. A chest is drawn once seen, or anywhere under the dev's `Settings.show_all_chests()` (`redraw_chests` re-asks every cell). `nearest_chest()` scans every cell, so call it on arrival only, never per frame.

#### Never scan per frame
**`envs_within` scans like `nearest_chest`:** ask it when a town is walked into, never per frame.

#### Let fog give way to gold in two places only
**The fog gives way to gold in two places and nowhere else:** `scour` and the chest star, both the fortuneteller's.

#### Keep the generator's constants in step
**`IceOverlay`'s sheet constants are the generator's** (`ice_wall.WASTE_COLS` / `WASTE_ROWS`, `VARIANTS`, `ACCENT_KINDS`, `ACCENT_CHANCE`): change one, change both and rerun `build_ice.py`.

#### Give nothing past the wall until it falls
**Nothing past the wall is the player's until it falls.**
Wasteland is seen as snow (no ground, no road, no fog veil, `name_of` is `WASTE_NAME`), never charted or walked to.
A wall tile draws its real ground under the ice but reads as `WALL_NAME`, `area_variant` "plain" and `town_tier` -1.
`has_chest`, `envs_within` and `town_tier` only answer for `is_land`.
Wall and wasteland names are never saved, so the land under them is named once it thaws.

#### Use `wall_step` for walls only
**`wall_step` is where the next wall stands and where the fallen ones lie, and nothing else** (`WALL_STEP`, or `RING_OF_WALLS_STEP` under the curse, said by the main scene as the world is built and never saved -- to `restore` itself, which draws the rubble as it loads): `_break_wall` and `walls_fallen` read it.
**`Encounter.walls_inside` goes on counting in `WALL_STEP`s**, so the land is as hard as it ever was, tile modifiers begin where they always did, and the extra walls are extra gates at the health their own ring gives them.

#### Grow the map only when the wall falls
**The map only grows when the wall falls** (`_break_wall` -> `_cover`).
Arrival grows nothing.
**Its environments are generated ahead, on a `WorkerThreadPool` task** (`_generate_ahead`, set off by `walk_onto` a wall tile), into a copy of `_envs` that `_generate` adopts when the area matches -- they are most of the fall's time (0.3 s for the first wall, 1.5 s for the sixth; the rest, on the main thread, under 0.2 s).
**`_merge_small_regions` finds the regions once and joins them as they merge (`_join`)**: finding them all again after every merge was quadratic in the whole map, 17 s of the third wall's and 80 s of the sixth's.
The task touches nothing but its copy and `EnvironmentGenerator`'s statics -- the adjacency table among them, built the first time it is asked for (`allowed()`), which after a load may be on the task's own thread; a lost fight leaves it for the next try, which is sound because `_envs` changes nowhere but `_generate`.
The dev's skip and the tests' `chart` of a wall without a walk generate in place, as before.

#### Chart the first tile blind
**The first tile into the fog can be charted blind.**
`can_chart` wants generated land or wall beside a charted tile, **seen or not**, and `chart` floors its sight at 0: none at all (the Thick Fog's, no torch held) uncovers the tile taken and nothing round it.
With a ring of sight every such tile is already seen, so this is the old rule for everyone else.
**Under the Nightwalkers the reach is further** (`dark_reach`, a Callable the main scene points at `Inventory.dark_reach`; `reach()` is 1 without it): any generated land or wall up to that many steps out, across land (the wall only ever ends a way).
`can_chart` reads `_chartable`, one walk out from all charted land at once, cleared by `_show`, `_break_wall`, `place_cave` and a changed reach, because the hover asks on every mouse move.
**Distant charting** (`distant`, the fourth wall's unlock, which the main scene's `_credit_walls` sets off `WallUnlocks.DISTANT`; its setter clears `_chartable`) adds every **seen** tile that seen land leads to from the charted, however far -- never a hidden one, never across the dark.
`dark_path(cell)` is the way in (fewest tiles, then the start nearest the player;
`_way_in` within `reach()` over any land first, then under `distant` over seen land with no count), **every tile of which is fought for in turn** -- the main scene's `_dark_way` -- so charting still only ever takes a tile beside the charted land: `walk_onto` refuses a tile further out, and `chart` takes the whole way at once only for the dev's skip.
**A lost fight retreats to `chart_from(cell)`.**
`HexMap.can_pick` (the builder sets it to `can_chart`) is what lets the hover and a click land on an undrawn cell; its `get_tile_info` is `{}`, so whoever handles the click asks the builder (`env_at`, `level_of`) and **must not ask `name_of`, which would name it**.

#### Derive `mods_of`
**`mods_of(cell, wild)` is derived, like `has_chest`:** `TileMods.for_cell` off `env_seed` and `ring_of` (not `Encounter.walls_inside`: the count follows the walls that actually stand, Ring of Walls and all), and `[]` for anything but open land -- a settlement, a chest, the wall, the wasteland, and any ring a wall stood on (`on_wall_ring`), fallen or not. `wild` is the Wild Tiles curse, which the builder cannot know.

#### Keep one Gollux cave a world, saved
**The Gollux cave (`cave`, `place_cave(near)`, `can_enter_cave`, `_draw_cave`) is one a world and saved** (`MapSave` version 4, `cave` absent for none): **placed beside the wall tile just broken** (the user's, 2026-10-09): `chart` keeps that tile as `breach` (not saved), and `place_cave(breach)` -- the main scene's, asked only once Gollux is unlocked: `WallUnlocks.GOLLUX`,
the third wall ever -- takes a cell within `CAVE_NEAR` steps of it and further out, never on `on_wall_ring` or a settlement, by `hash([env_seed, "cave"])`.
So the first world's goes down past its third wall, every later world's past its first; none while `breach` is `NO_CELL`.
It is a set piece: `road_at`, `has_chest`, `mods_of`, `can_farm` / `can_farm_ground` (and so `best_farm`) all refuse it, and `name_of` names it from `TileNames.CAVE_FEATURES` (`place_cave` drops the land's name a tile already seen had been given).
Drawn by `HexMap.set_cave` (`CAVE_ART`, one `CAVE_NODE` on the settlements' layer and anchor) once seen and on land; a missing picture draws nothing.
`can_enter_cave` is `can_visit`'s rule (charted, stood on, no walk under way): going down is being there.

#### Read a torch once, as a tile is charted
**A torch is read at the moment a tile is charted and never again.**
`main_scene._sight()` (`BASE_SIGHT` (2) + `inventory.stats().sight`, the base gone under the Thick Fog) is passed to `chart` there only, so putting a torch on uncovers nothing, taking one off hides nothing, and a torch that dropped in that same fight is in the bag and does not count.
`_reveal_around` cannot reach past generated land either: `rect` reaches `WASTE_DEPTH` rings past the wall, and no chart is further out than the wall.

#### Keep the walls on level edges
**`level_of(cell)` comes in even bands of `LEVEL_TILES` (4) rings** (level `n` starts at `first_step(n)`), and `START_LAND_RADIUS + 1` and `WALL_STEP` are multiples of it (`test_generation` holds that), so the land past a wall is a level above the land inside it -- but for every other wall of the Ring of Walls, whose `RING_OF_WALLS_STEP` is no multiple; it is the tile panel's number and the ceiling on drops.
Enemy health uses the smooth distance instead.

#### Keep `can_farm` blind to where the player stands
**`can_farm` asks nothing about where the player stands, and refuses every settlement** (Farm hangs off it); `best_farm()` is the farmable tile farthest out, where the camp stands; `can_move_to` refuses everything while `walking`.
