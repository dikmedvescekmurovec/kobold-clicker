## Towns (`Scenes/Town/`)
What a settlement is for: which counters it has, what everything over them costs, what work it has posted, and what the player has left behind in each one. All rules classes, static or node-free like `OrbTable`, so the tests need no interface. **Read `DESIGN.md` here before changing what a town offers, what a price means or how the page and the bag share the window** -- it holds the why. The page itself is `Scenes/UI/town_page.gd`.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `town_services.gd` (`TownServices`) | Which counters a settlement has: `services_for(tier, spot, world_seed)` gives `BOUNTIES` / `GEAR` / `ORBS` / `SMITH` / `FORTUNE` in `ORDER`. A village is the board and one vendor picked by `hash([seed, spot, "vendor"])`, a town is the board and both, a fortress adds the smith; **every settlement has the fortuneteller**. `label(service)` is the one place a counter is named |
| `town_prices.gd` (`TownPrices`) | Every price, quoted in bodies. `gold_at_level(level)` = `Encounter.gold_at_steps` at the first step of that level band. `sell_price(item)` = that at the **item's** level times `RARITY_MULT[rarity]` times `SELL_BODIES`, **halved for a broken piece**; `buy_price(item)` is that divided by `SELL_SHARE`; `sell_total(items)` sums a handful. `upgrade_price(item)` / `lock_price(item)` are the smith's, off the piece's own level. `orb_value(orb, town_cell)` is inverse to `OrbTable.ORBS[orb].weight` against `commonest()`, pegged to the **town's** level, and is what a vendor asks. **Orbs are bought, never sold back.** `reroll_price(town_cell, rerolls)` is `REROLL_BODIES` at the town's level times `REROLL_GROWTH ^ rerolls`. `fortune_price(reading, town_cell, cast)` is `FORTUNE_BODIES[reading]` at the town's level times `FORTUNE_GROWTH ^ cast` -- **but `transcend`, which is bodies at `transcend_steps()`** (the second ring of land behind the first wall), the same in every town and world, and never cast twice. Dials: `SELL_BODIES`, `SELL_SHARE`, `ORB_BODIES`, `RARITY_MULT`, `UPGRADE_BODIES`, `LOCK_BODIES`, `REROLL_BODIES`, `REROLL_GROWTH`, `FORTUNE_BODIES`, `FORTUNE_GROWTH` |
| `blacksmith.gd` (`Blacksmith`) | What the fortress smith does to a piece, as `OrbTable`'s three faces: `can_upgrade(item, cap)` / `upgrade(item, cap, rng)` / `why_not_upgrade`, and `can_lock(item)` / `lock(item, rng)` / `why_not_lock`. An upgrade is level + 1, `Item.scaled_stats` at the new level and `OrbTable.reroll_values` (the Divine orb's own act) on top of it, so the piece comes off the anvil wholly at its new level; what a lock or a Binding holds fast, and a perfected line, are the only numbers that do not move; `BREAK_CHANCE` is how often it ruins the piece instead (`item.broken`, gold spent anyway); `break_chance(item)` is 0 while an heirloom is under its `safe_level`, and then no `randf` is drawn at all. A lock pins one modifier the smith picks at random. `BROKEN` is the sentence both refusals share. The `cap` is handed in, not worked out here |
| `town_state.gd` (`TownState`) | What the towns the player has walked into hold, saved inside the inventory as `inventory.towns`. Keyed by **world spot** as `"x,y"` (`key`); `visit(spot)` makes and returns a town's drawer, `visited(spot)`, `to_dict` / `from_dict`. It never reads what is in a drawer -- whoever puts something there knows its shape |
| `bounty_board.gd` (`BountyBoard`) | The work a settlement has posted, in that town's drawer: `restock(drawer, envs, cell, rng)` posts a fresh `COMMONS` + `ELITES` and is refused until the board is `cleared(drawer)` -- every posting handed in, or nothing on it; `bounties(drawer)` reads them back, `see(drawer)` / `seen` / `any_seen(state)` is whether the player has stood at the board (what puts the journal in the corner), `accept(state, bounty)` takes one on and is refused while `active(state)` is not empty, `is_active(bounty)`, `active_spot(state)` is the world spot of the town whose posting is out ("" for none; what `active` is built on, and what names the town on the filled banner), `takes(bounty, enemy, tile_level)` is whether a posting wants a body at all -- its monster, on land at least as deep as its town -- and `count_kill(state, enemy, times, tile_level)` counts one it takes against the accepted bounty and nothing else, `ready(bounty)` and `claim(bounty)` are the hand-in, `abandon(bounty)` gives the accepted one up (unaccepted, `have` back to 0, `located` kept), `locate(bounty)` / `located(bounty)` is whether a fortuneteller has been paid to say where the monster lives (accepted work only, once). A posting is `{enemy, need, have, gold, xp, orb, item, accepted, done, level, located}` (`orb` a list of names, `ORBS[tier]` of `OrbTable.roll_favoured`, read by `orbs_of`; an older save's one name is read as a list by `bounties`; `xp` is `Encounter.xp_of` times `xp_reward_of(tier)`, banked by the Claim, which emits `TownPage.xp_claimed` for the bar) (`level` is the posting town's `MapBuilder.level_of`); targets come only from `envs` (the caller's, `MapBuilder.envs_within`), so never the mimic and never a boss. **`item` is a promise, not a piece:** `{kind, rarity, plus}` rolled at posting (`_item_promise`: `ITEM_CHANCE` by the posting's tier, then `UNIQUE_CHANCE`, the ascensions by `plus_of` (at least +1/+2/+3/+4 at `PLUS_CHANCES` 25/5/1/0.1%, then `PLUS_TAIL` a tenth as likely each step, no ceiling), and the rarity the best of `RARITY_ROLLS[tier]` (2 common, 3 elite) off `ItemRarity.roll`'s BOSS row; a unique's `kind` is ""), read back by `item_of` (a kind or rarity this build has not got is no promise), put into words by `reward_text` ("an elite sword +1", "a unique piece"), and paid by `reward_item(bounty, cell, rng)` at the hand-in -- the kind's material at the better of two levels under the town's ceiling for the posting's tier, a unique any the game has, ascended `plus` times -- so what the piece is exactly is written nowhere before it is in the bag. Dials: `NEED_COMMON`, `NEED_ELITE`, `REWARD_COMMON` / `REWARD_ELITE` (100 / 20 purses, via `reward_of(tier)`), `XP_COMMON` / `XP_ELITE` (50 / 10 bodies, via `xp_reward_of`), `ORBS`, `ITEM_CHANCE`, `UNIQUE_CHANCE`, `RARITY_ROLLS`, `PLUS_CHANCES`, `PLUS_TAIL`, `BOUNTY_RANGE` |
| `fortune_teller.gd` (`FortuneTeller`) | What the fortuneteller can be asked, in `READINGS` order, in two halves: `COMMON` -- `ROADS` (`road_lines(towns, from)`: the nearest village, town and fortress by `nearest_towns`, each with `bearing` -- eight winds -- and `walk_time`, one tile an hour, `HOURS_PER_DAY` 8, `DAYS_PER_WEEK` 7), `TREASURE` (the chest star), `QUARRY` (`BountyBoard.locate`), `RELIC` (`peek(found, shown, rng)` from `hidden`) and `APPRAISE` (`odds(item)`: the piece's whole `ModifierTable.pool_for` as `{id, line, weight, share}`, `line` being `ModifierTable.band_line` at the piece's level; `why_not_appraise` refuses a unique) -- **asked as often as they are paid for, at double the price every time** (`cast(fortunes, reading)` / `note_cast`, the count in `inventory.fortunes` under `CAST`); and `GREAT` -- `SCOUR` (`scour_cells(center, radius)`, `SCOUR_RADIUS` 2 = 19 tiles) and `HOMECOMING` (labelled **Return**; `MapBuilder.jump_to`) -- **one a settlement**, written in that town's drawer as `ASKED` + the name and read by `asked(drawer, reading)`, which is also where the roads' free re-telling is kept. `TRANSCEND` is in neither: it is not a spell but the way out of the world (`Inventory.transcended`), on her list only once `MapBuilder.walls_fallen()` is above 0. Also the keys and readers of `inventory.fortunes`: `chest`, `peeked`, `cast` |
| `vendor_stock.gd` (`VendorStock`) | What a vendor has on its shelf, in that town's drawer: `restock(drawer, tier, cell, rng)` fills `items` and `orbs` on the first visit and **never again for free**; `items(drawer)` (null where one was bought) and `orbs(drawer)` ("" where one was) read it back; `take(drawer, key, index)` empties a square; `put(drawer, index, item)` writes a piece back where it stood, which is how an orb the player spends on a shelf piece (`TownPage._on_shelf_input`) survives `items` handing out fresh `Item`s; `reroll(drawer, key, tier, cell, rng)` is **one** fresh shelf (`ITEMS` or `ORBS`) bought with gold, counted per shelf in `rerolls(drawer, key)`, saved with the town and **never reset**. `roll_item` is the favourable roll: the better of two `roll_level`s under `level_of(cell) + TIER_LEVEL`, `ItemRarity.roll` at the tier **above** the town's rabble, and `LootTable`'s own weights for the type -- the level first, and handed to the draw, so no vendor deals a material the piece's own level has not unlocked. Dial: `SIZE` 6 |

## Rules and gotchas
- **`TownState` must never name `Inventory`,** which names it: that is where Godot's cycle checker bites. `TownPrices.RARITY_MULT` is a **list** indexed by `ItemRarity.Rarity`'s own order for the same reason -- a `const` naming another class's enum is the other place it bites.
- **Keyed by world spot, never by map cell.** A cell is a place in the drawn window and the window moves; `MapBuilder.town_tier(cell)` and `view.origin + cell` are how a cell is crossed over.
- **Every price is a number of bodies, never a number of gold.** Retuning `Encounter.BASE_GOLD` or `GOLD_GROWTH` has to retune the shops with it, so nothing here writes a gold figure of its own.
- **A dial is moved with the table in front of you, never by feel:** `tests/balance_town.gd` prints what the ground pays against every price at tile levels 1, 3, 5, 8 and 10, and says it in tile fights, which is the only unit a player has. Run it before and after, and put the reasoning in `DESIGN.md` with the numbers. Where it stands: a shelf common 1.5 fights, an elite 21, an upgrade 2, a lock 250, a common bounty 7.5 and an elite 6.5, and selling a run's finds a fifth of what that run's bodies paid.
- **A piece is priced at its own level and an orb at the town's.** A find carried home is worth what it is; an orb has no level, so what makes one worth more is where it is being sold.
- **Services are derived, stock is written down.** What counters a town has is a pure function of its tier and its spot and may be asked twice; anything *rolled* goes in `TownState`, the way names and the town world do. A shelf is never re-derived from a seed, and a caller must not roll one of its own.
- **A vendor is stocked on the way in and nowhere else** (`TownPage.open`), so a shelf never changes while it is being looked at. A bought square stays, empty, until that shelf's **Restock** is paid for -- there is no free restock and no kills clock. **The board keeps no clock at all:** new work is posted when every posting on it has been handed in (`BountyBoard.cleared`), asked on the way in and again at each Claim, so the third hand-in turns the board over there and then.
- **One bounty at a time, and only an accepted one counts.** The board's Accept (`BountyBoard.accept`) is refused while any posting anywhere is accepted and not yet **handed in** -- finished is not enough -- and `count_kill` moves that one posting and nothing else. A kill reaches it through `FightLedger.add_kill` and the bank-or-pouch rule (`Scenes/CLAUDE.md`), never from the interface. `see` only puts the journal in the corner. **A kill counts only on a tile of the town's level or deeper:** the main scene gives `FightLedger.tile_level` the fought tile's level, both bank-or-pouch paths hand it to `count_kill`, and `MapBuilder.nearest_env(envs, min_level)` never points at land that would not count. While a bounty is out a board shows that posting and hides the rest.
- **A shelf can be rerolled for gold, and each costs `REROLL_GROWTH` times the last, for the whole playthrough:** the count lives in the town's drawer and nothing resets it, closing the game included. **The gear and orb shelves keep separate counts and are rerolled separately** -- Restock is the open tab's shelf at that vendor's own price. Paying never touches the board.
- **A bounty is handed in at the town that posted it** (`TownPage`'s Claim), and `claim` is what makes that once: it refuses a posting that is not `ready` or is already `done`. A handed-in posting keeps its square, spent, until the whole board is cleared. **A promised piece goes in the bag by the vendor's own rule** (`_why_not`): over a full bag the Claim is greyed with "Your bag is full." and a press pays nothing, since the gold and the piece go together; the page emits `item_claimed(piece)` before the log and the bag hear of it, which is how a unique new to the log gets its banner.
- **A target is somewhere the player can walk to.** `restock` posts only what lives on the environments handed to it, which is what the map actually generated within `BOUNTY_RANGE` of the town -- so `BountyBoard` never names `MapBuilder`, and a board with no land around it posts nothing rather than guessing.
- **`buy_price` is `sell_price` divided by `SELL_SHARE`, never a second figure,** so buying back what was just sold costs five times what it fetched and no counter is a loop.
- **Never `Inventory.add` on a full bag:** past the cap the player is overencumbered and cannot fight, so a vendor must not be the one to put them there. The Buy is refused: greyed, with the reason in its tooltip.
- **What the fortuneteller sells is written where it belongs, and `inventory.fortunes` is only what belongs to the player:** which readings were bought in a town in that town's drawer (`ASKED`), the bounty's land on the posting (`LOCATED`), and the chest's world spot, the peeked uniques and the spent scour in `inventory.fortunes`. `Inventory` never names `FortuneTeller`; the dictionary is read through its statics.
- **Treasure only ever points at a chest the player has not seen** (`MapBuilder.nearest_chest(true)`): a seen chest is drawn on its tile, and `ChestPointer` hides itself over one that is seen and on screen, so selling it would be selling nothing.
- **A reading is sold as often as it is paid for and a great spell once a settlement.** `FortuneTeller.COMMON` climbs by `TownPrices.FORTUNE_GROWTH` for every casting this world has heard (`cast` / `note_cast` in `inventory.fortunes`, global: walking to a fresh village does not reset it, though its own level still sets the base). `FortuneTeller.GREAT` -- the scour and the road home -- is refused in a town that has cast it (`asked`), and offered by the next one. **The roads write the same drawer key and it means the opposite there:** paid for once, they are told again for nothing, and a free telling never moves the count.
- **A great spell is aimed at the map and paid for there, not on the page:** `TownPage` only emits `spell_aimed(reading, price, spot)`; the main scene charges, and writes the town's drawer key, when the spell actually did something -- `MapBuilder.scour` showed at least one tile, or `MapBuilder.jump_to` found a charted settlement that is not the one underfoot. So neither is ever spent on land already seen, land not yet generated, or a tile with no town on it. Escape and the panel's X cost nothing.
- **`FORTUNE_BODIES`' keys are `FortuneTeller`'s reading names as literals** (a `const` naming another class's const is where the cycle checker bites); `test_town` holds them together with `LABELS`, and `COMMON` + `GREAT` + `TRANSCEND` against `READINGS`. Where the **first** casting stands: roads 5 fights, treasure 2, quarry and appraise 0.5, relic 5, homecoming 20, scour 100 -- and every reading doubles from there.
- **A town is usable only when `MapBuilder.can_visit(cell)`:** charted, standing on it, not walking.
- **The smith is the one exception to "a piece's level and base stats never change"** (`Scenes/Items/CLAUDE.md`), and he pays for it: `BREAK_CHANCE` on every upgrade, and a cap of `MapBuilder.level_of(town cell) + LootTable.TIER_LEVEL[BOSS]` -- the ceiling a vendor's shelf already rolls under, so no counter walks a piece past the frontier. The caller works the cap out and hands it in; `Blacksmith` never names the map.
- **`upgrade` returns false for a break as well as for a refusal, and the gold is spent either way.** Ask `can_upgrade` before charging: that is what tells the two apart, and it is the whole contract.
- **Her spells are two grids of squares, the way a shelf is** (`TownPage._spell_square`, `FORTUNE_ICONS`, placeholder art in `Assets/Fortune/`): the reading's name over its mark with the price under it, a gold rim round a live one under the cursor, greyed where it stands with the reason in its tooltip when she will not read it, and "Free" and no coin for the roads once they are paid for here. A heading over each grid (`Readings` / `Great spells`) and nothing else: no rule under it, which would cost the second grid's prices their row. The way out keeps its word and the full width under both.
- **Transcending is asked for before it is done, and never charged:** the button under the six (a coin and no figure -- six figures beside that word widen the page into the panel beside it) only sets `_said`; her answer is a warning -- everything is lost, and what waits is worth far more, **never what that is** (`TRANSCEND_LINES`; the user's rule) -- with the price, and the button under it emits `TownPage.transcend_pressed`. `main_scene._on_transcend_pressed` checks the purse and fades to `TranscendPage`; only leaving that writes `inventory.transcended()`, deletes the map and reloads -- the purse is one of the things left behind, so nothing is deducted. It is never marked `ASKED`.
- **An heirloom is held up to the smith, the appraisal and a held orb from the heirlooms' page,** which the crown swaps in at the counter (`main_scene._counter_page`). **A worn piece is his to work on too:** `_smith_why_not` refuses only what `Blacksmith` refuses and a purse that is short, so a set is improved without being stripped off. The ground's cap holds for an heirloom too.
- **Broken is final and locked is forever:** a broken piece takes no orb, no upgrade and no lock, and sells for half; a locked modifier survives all six orbs (`OrbTable`), and there is only ever one to a piece.
