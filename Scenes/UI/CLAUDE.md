## Rules and gotchas
- **A table is `UITheme.table_row`s in a `vbox(0)`, and nothing else draws one:** name against the left edge, value against the right, every second row washed with `TABLE_STRIPE`. The item block, the character page's stats and the fortuneteller's appraisal are the three there are. Pass `width` when the table stands in something that sizes itself round its contents (a card), so a long name wraps in what its value leaves.
- **Two fonts, each at its native size only:** a name, a heading or a button face is Pixellari 16 (`PanelLabel`); anything read as a line of a block -- stats, modifiers, a card's body, a sign's sentence -- is Ark Pixel 10 (`SmallLabel`, `UITheme.label(.., .., true)`, `ItemDetails.line(.., true)`). A new body line in Pixellari is what made the panels bulky.
- **A level's heading wears marks, not words:** Auto is a funnel on a toggled `BrownIconButton` that stays down *and* darkens (`BagPage.AUTO_HELD`) while the level is being thrown away, because the pack's pressed face alone is one pixel; Clear is a bin. In a town that buys gear the bin becomes two stacks of coins (`SELL_ICON`) and sells the level, the sum in its tooltip. Tests find either by its tooltip. **Both ask first** (`BagPage._ask`: a titled panel over the whole window, Cancel beside the verb, "Don't show this again" at its foot beside a tick box that is a small toggled `BrownIconButton` named `TICK_NAME`, never a Godot `CheckBox`); a ticked Yes writes `SKIP_CONFIRM + id` into `inventory.tips` and that question is never asked again (`_remember`). **Then the uniques are a second question** (`_drop_level`, which both handlers are): the ordinary pieces go at once (`_sell_level` / `_clear_level`, the deeds, through `Inventory.discard_level(level, uniques)`), and under `Settings.uniques` Ask a unique among them is asked about by name -- Don't sell / Sell (Don't discard / Discard for the bin), Escape and the X leaving it in the bag -- whose tick keeps **the answer given** in `Settings.uniques` (Sell or Keep), never in `tips`; under Sell it goes with the rest and under Keep it stays, and nothing asks. A lone piece's own Sell or Discard asks nothing either way.
- **Back is always the arrow, `UITheme.back_button(tooltip)`, never the word:** under a shelf piece in a town (first in Buy's row), under a drop open in `DropsView` and on a lost fight's verdict (beside **Retry**). The one exception is the verdict of a won tile or an ended run, whose way out is the word **Collect**, which says what the press is for.
- **A selected piece's buttons stand in `BagPage._actions`, beside its square** (Equip / Sell / Discard / Unequip, stacked, no Back). Tests find them with `_deep_button(page._actions, ..)`. Escape clears the selection first and closes the bag only on a second press.
- **A button gold moves through is `UITheme.priced_button`, and reads [word, air, figure, coin]:** the word is the button's own left-aligned `text` (so tests still find it by `begins_with`), the figure and the coin are a right-aligned child named `PRICE_NAME` that greys with `disabled`, sinks a pixel while pressed, and widens the button's minimum once it is in the tree. Buy, Sell, Restock, Upgrade, Lock, the skills' Reset (`set_price` as the cost moves), every fortuneteller reading, and Claim (`figure` false: coin only). Never `"%s %s" % [word, price]` with `icon = Coins.icon()` again; a test reads the figure with `price_of`.
- **Set `theme_type_variation` on a plain `Button`** and Godot drives hover, press and disable. `Wood*` buttons stand on `WoodPanel`, `Light*` on `TextPanel`.
- **A Control with no themed ancestor must carry `UITheme.theme()` itself,** or its type variation means nothing: anything added straight to a `CanvasLayer` (icon buttons, cards, pages).
- **A floating card cannot be a Godot tooltip:** a tooltip is its own window and cannot inherit `ui_scale`. Cards are ordinary Controls, scaled by `ui_scale`, placed by `ItemCard.beside`, and placed **twice** (now and deferred) because labels have not laid out on the first pass.
- **Every card is cream (`TextPanel`), and a plain tooltip is a card too:** set `tooltip_text` as ever and `TipCard` writes it; never turn Godot's own tooltip back on, and never write a card's lines in the wood half of the palette (bone, cream, `GOLD`, `LEAF_LT`). Only a tooltip waits (`TipCard.DELAY`); the item, orb and skill cards are up at once.
- **A container sets its children's `rotation` and `scale` back whenever it lays them out,** so a mark inside one is turned in its image (`Accordion` draws its caret both ways), never through the node.
- **A section heading that says what is under it is an `Accordion`,** so it can be folded; an id is `"<page>:<heading>"`.
- **Tree order is draw order on a `CanvasLayer`:** `OrbCard` is added after the character sheet because it overhangs it.
- **Free children with `UITheme.clear`, not bare `queue_free`:** a queued child still counts in hit-tests and minimum sizes until the frame ends.
- **A bag square ignores the mouse** so a drag that starts on one still scrolls; which square was clicked is worked out from the cursor. An `OrbSlot` is the opposite on purpose.
- **Widths are arithmetic, not taste:** six `OrbSlot.SIDE` plus five `ORB_GAP` is `BagPage.WIDTH` less under a gap of slack, split by the centred tray (`test_ui_theme` holds it); `DOLL_SCALE` 3 is the smallest whole number that keeps 40 px sockets apart; `test_inventory` checks no socket overlaps or runs off the page.
- **Whole-number scales only** (`PIXEL`, `DOLL_SCALE`, 2:1 orb icons on an even offset), so a sprite pixel stays square.
- **Bars of any length are assembled from parts with no separation,** each blown up through its minimum size, not the node's `scale`. `KillPips._init` and `HealthBar._init` assert the sprites still measure what the consts say.
- **`Coins.count_for` counts in whole tens, never `log()`:** `log(1000)/log(10)` floors to 2.
- **The coin sheet's geometry is measured:** `FRAMES` is 9 though the sheet is wider.
- **`Palette.GOLD` is the unique rarity step;** do not spend it on currency text. Amber on cream is too weak; use `SLATE` there. A unique's name is gold at 16 px; its effect sentence (`ItemDetails.fill`, under the rarity line) is `SLOT_TAN_DK`, because gold at 10 px on cream cannot be read.
- **The darker half of the rarity ramp is for cream panels, the ring half for backdrops:** a stat block needs `TextPanel`, never wood.
- **`CloseButton` has no minimum size of its own:** give it `UITheme.icon_size("CloseButton")`.
- **Three panels share a 576 panel-pixel window in a town,** so shop mode hides the doll (except at the smith); the bag's grid and the town page's width never move. **`TownPage` is as tight down the page as across it** -- a fortress's two rows of word tabs once took the gear tab past the 284 panel pixels its body has at a 648 px window -- so everything under the counter's name now sits in `_scrolled`, with the tabs and the name pinned above it and an offer's Buy pinned below it. Anything added still has to be measured both ways. See `Scenes/Town/DESIGN.md`.
- **A bounty is written out in one place, `BountyList.row`,** and the board and the journal both call it, so a posting cannot read one way in town and another out of it. Terrain swatches come from `HexTileset.env_icon`, the tile panel's own cut of the hex sheet -- never a second set of art.
- **A page never moves the map itself:** `show_cell` goes to the main scene, which closes the pages, selects the tile and takes the camera there, and then leaves the walking to the tile panel.
- **An orb is used two ways, and which depends on whether a piece is open.** Open, a lit orb is spent on it at once. Shut, a press picks the orb up (`BagPage._armed`, its socket dark, the squares it can do nothing to at `OrbSlot.DIM`) and the next bag square or worn socket pressed is crafted where it lies, unopened (`_craft`); it stays held while any are left, and opening a piece, bare panel, the same orb again, Escape, a right click anywhere (`_input`, since a panel would eat it before `_unhandled_input`) or the bag being hidden puts it down. **`_armed` is only ever assigned, never worked round:** its setter is what makes the orb the mouse cursor (`Cursors.hold`, the 32 px file, which is the tray's 16 at `ui_scale` 2) and emits `held_changed`. A held orb works on a **vendor's shelf piece** too: `TownPage.orb_held` greys the shelf by it and a press calls the bag's `craft_held(item, written)` (set on the page from outside, like `view`) instead of opening the piece -- the bag applies, spends and saves, and `written` (`VendorStock.put`) runs between the change and the save so the shelf and the orb go down in one write. The price under the piece is read off its rarity and moves with it. `crafted` -> `ItemCard.unmute` (wired in the main scene) is what lets the card under the cursor show the result of that press. At an orb vendor a press with nothing open is still a sale, never a pick-up.
- **The board's tab is over a counter that sells nothing:** `BagPage._buys` is false for every service but `GEAR` and `ORBS`, so Discard stays Discard and the tray still crafts while a board is open.
- **The bag sells what the town page's open tab buys, not what the town has:** one counter is live at a time, and `BagPage._buys` is the one place that asks.
- **The two pages only ever hand each other the piece that is open:** `TownPage.offer_changed` -> `BagPage.offer` and `BagPage.selection_changed` -> `TownPage.bag_changed`, wired in the main scene. Both redraw the other side, which is also how a purchase reaches the purse and the grid -- never a second path for a bought piece.
- **The hand means a press does something, and a button gets it for nothing** (`Cursors.install` watches `node_added`; a button dead as it joins keeps the arrow). Anything else that takes a press asks for it: `Cursors.wear` on a Control of its own (a shelf cell, a lit `OrbSlot`, a `SkillSlot` that can take a point), `Cursors.over_squares` from the `gui_input` of a Control that answers for squares which take no mouse (the bag's scroll, the doll, the drops grid). Where no Control lies under the mouse the cursor is `Input.set_default_cursor_shape`: `HexMap._point` (hand over a tile, closed hand while dragging) and `CombatScene` (the sword from `begin` to `_on_finished`). The sword is also on Chart and Farm, the boot on Move here, the hammer on the smith's two while they can be pressed, the question mark on the collection's help.
- **A shelf square's price label is `clip_text`, never allowed to push:** gold grows with the walk, and a six-figure price would widen the page into the panel beside it. The whole number is in the tooltip and on the Buy button.
