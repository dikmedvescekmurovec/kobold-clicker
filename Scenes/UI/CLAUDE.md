## UI code (`Scenes/UI/`)
Everything is built in code, drawn in panel pixels and scaled by the main scene's `ui_scale`. **Read `DESIGN.md` here before changing how a widget looks or is laid out** -- it holds the why. The bag's and the character sheet's design reasoning is in `Scenes/DESIGN.md`.

## Rules and gotchas
- **No descriptive text on a page** (the user's rule, 2026-09-30): no sentence saying what a field is for, how a rule works or what a number means. Controls, values, headings, an empty state and a refusal only; anything more is a tooltip, and only when asked.
- **A table is `UITheme.table_row`s in a `vbox(0)`, and nothing else draws one:** name against the left edge, value against the right, every second row washed with `TABLE_STRIPE`. The item block, the character page's stats and the fortuneteller's appraisal are the three there are. Pass `width` when the table stands in something that sizes itself round its contents (a card), so a long name wraps in what its value leaves.
- **Two fonts, each at its native size only:** a name, a heading or a button face is Pixellari 16 (`PanelLabel`); anything read as a line of a block -- stats, modifiers, a card's body, a sign's sentence -- is Ark Pixel 10 (`SmallLabel`, `UITheme.label(.., .., true)`, `ItemDetails.line(.., true)`). A new body line in Pixellari is what made the panels bulky.
- **A level's heading is one line** -- its name in the body font, the rule running on from it, then its marks -- **and wears marks, not words:** Auto is a bare chest struck through (`ui_icon_auto`) (`UITheme.BARE_BUTTON`, `AUTO_ICON`) that stays down *and* turns green (`AUTO_ON_ICON`) while the level is being thrown away, because the pressed sink alone is one pixel; Clear is a bare bin. In a town that buys gear the bin becomes two stacks of coins (`SELL_ICON`) and sells the level, the sum in its tooltip. Tests find either by its tooltip. **Both ask first** (`BagPage._ask`: a titled panel over the whole window, Cancel beside the verb, "Don't show this again" at its foot beside a tick box that is a small toggled `BrownIconButton` named `TICK_NAME`, never a Godot `CheckBox`); a ticked Yes writes `SKIP_CONFIRM + id` into `inventory.tips` and that question is never asked again (`_remember`). **A rarity orb that would lower a piece asks the same way** (`_craft`, id `lower_rarity`, Use in `LightDangerButton`; the bag, the doll and a shelf all come through it), and nothing is spent until Use: `_apply_orb` is the deed, and the shelf redraws from its `written`, not after `craft_held` returns. **Then the uniques are a second question** (`_drop_level`, which both handlers are): the ordinary pieces go at once (`_sell_level` / `_clear_level`, the deeds, through `Inventory.discard_level(level, uniques)`), and under `Settings.uniques` Ask a unique among them is asked about by name -- Don't sell / Sell (Don't discard / Discard for the bin), Escape leaving it in the bag -- whose tick keeps **the answer given** in `Settings.uniques` (Sell or Keep), never in `tips`; under Sell it goes with the rest and under Keep it stays, and nothing asks. A lone piece's own Sell or Discard asks nothing either way. **A locked piece is passed by both** (`Item.locked`, kept in the save; `Inventory.discard_level` skips it, and the heading's count, sum and the uniques' question leave it out): **Never sell** with the padlock (a toggle, on the green face while locked; never the word Lock, which is the smith's on a modifier) stands last in `_actions` under any bag piece but an heirloom. A lone Sell is greyed while it is on (so a Ctrl-click passes it by too); a lone Discard still takes it.
- **Every popup behaves like the rewards' panel** (the user's rule, 2026-09-25): a `titled_panel` with **no X**, its way out a button at its foot (Collect, Dismiss, Got it, Cancel beside the verb) and Escape, brought up by `Juice.popup` and taken away by `Juice.pop_out` -- the holder let go of at once (`_confirm`, `_told`, `_tip_panel` go null before the shrink ends) so the next question or deed never finds the old one. `win` (the wash and sparks) only for something gained: a bounty's pay, a fight won, a fortuneteller's answer; never on a question or a tip. The panel is named (`CONFIRM_PANEL`, `TOLD_PANEL`) because the wash is added before it in its holder.
- **Back is always the arrow, `UITheme.back_button(tooltip)`, never the word:** under a shelf piece in a town (first in Buy's row), under a drop open in `DropsView` and on a lost fight's verdict (beside **Retry**). The one exception is the verdict of a won tile or an ended run, whose way out is the word **Collect**, which says what the press is for.
- **A selected piece's buttons stand in `BagPage._actions`, beside its square** (Equip / Sell / Discard / Unequip, stacked, no Back). Tests find them with `_deep_button(page._actions, ..)`. Escape clears the selection first and closes the bag only on a second press.
- **A button gold moves through is `UITheme.priced_button`, and reads [word, air, figure, coin]:** the word is the button's own left-aligned `text` (so tests still find it by `begins_with`), the figure and the coin are a right-aligned child named `PRICE_NAME` that greys with `disabled`, sinks a pixel while pressed, and widens the button's minimum once it is in the tree. Buy, Sell, Restock, Upgrade, Lock, the skills' Reset (`set_price` as the cost moves), every fortuneteller reading, and Claim (`figure` false: coin only). Never `"%s %s" % [word, price]` with `icon = Coins.icon()` again; a test reads the figure with `price_of`.
- **Set `theme_type_variation` on a plain `Button`** and Godot drives hover, press and disable. `Wood*` buttons stand on `WoodPanel`, `Light*` on `TextPanel`.
- **A Control with no themed ancestor must carry `UITheme.theme()` itself,** or its type variation means nothing: anything added straight to a `CanvasLayer` (icon buttons, cards, pages).
- **A floating card cannot be a Godot tooltip:** a tooltip is its own window and cannot inherit `ui_scale`. Cards are ordinary Controls, scaled by `ui_scale`, placed by `ItemCard.beside`, and placed **twice** (now and deferred) because labels have not laid out on the first pass.
- **Every card is cream (`TextPanel`), and a plain tooltip is a card too:** set `tooltip_text` as ever and `TipCard` writes it; never turn Godot's own tooltip back on, and never write a card's lines in the wood half of the palette (bone, cream, `GOLD`, `LEAF_LT`). Only a tooltip waits (`TipCard.DELAY`); the item, orb and skill cards are up at once.
- **A container sets its children's `rotation` and `scale` back whenever it lays them out,** so a mark inside one is turned in its image (`Accordion` draws its caret both ways), never through the node.
- **A section heading that says what is under it is an `Accordion`,** so it can be folded; an id is `"<page>:<heading>"`.
- **Tree order is draw order on a `CanvasLayer`:** `OrbCard` is added after the character sheet because it overhangs it.
- **An item square's frame stands `ItemRarity.FRAME_HEAD` (4) above it, and a scroll clips:** a scroll whose first row is squares pads its top by it inside the scroll (`TownPage`'s counter, `DropsView`'s grid); one that opens with a heading (the bag's levels, the log's sections) needs nothing. Nothing of a frame stands past a square's sides, so a grid as wide as its scroll is never cut.
- **Every panel's long part sits in `UITheme.scroll()`, with what must stay in reach pinned above or below it** (a page's points, the tile panel's buttons, the town's Buy): a 648 px window is the budget, and a page that outgrows it scrolls rather than running off the foot. A scroll clips, so pad it by whatever its children draw past their rect (`SkillsPage`: the ring and the count).
- **A Control whose own press redraws it out of the tree calls `accept_event()` first** (the doll, a shelf cell, a spell square, `OrbSlot`, `SkillSlot`): Godot stops walking a press up the tree at a Control that left it, before its `STOP` filter can mark the press handled, so the map behind takes the press and the release and selects the tile under it.
- **Free children with `UITheme.clear`, not bare `queue_free`:** a queued child still counts in hit-tests and minimum sizes until the frame ends.
- **A bag square ignores the mouse** so a drag that starts on one still scrolls; which square was clicked is worked out from the cursor. An `OrbSlot` is the opposite on purpose.
- **Widths are arithmetic, not taste:** six `OrbSlot.SIDE` plus five `ORB_GAP` is `BagPage.WIDTH` less under a gap of slack, split by the centred tray (`test_ui_theme` holds it); `DOLL_SCALE` is 1, the doll being drawn at the item icons' own pixel size, with `DOLL_SOCKETS` placed to keep 40 px sockets apart; `test_inventory` checks no socket overlaps or runs off the page.
- **Whole-number scales only** (`PIXEL`, `DOLL_SCALE`, 2:1 orb icons on an even offset), so a sprite pixel stays square.
- **Bars of any length are assembled from parts with no separation,** each blown up through its minimum size, not the node's `scale`. `HealthBar._init` asserts its sprites still measure what its consts say, and `KillPips._init` that every pip is `WIDTH` across -- the HUD sizes the clock to a pip bar before the bar exists.
- **`Coins.count_for` counts in whole tens, never `log()`:** `log(1000)/log(10)` floors to 2.
- **The coin sheet's geometry is measured:** `FRAMES` is 9 though the sheet is wider.
- **`Palette.GOLD` is the unique rarity step;** do not spend it on currency text. Amber on cream is too weak; use `TEXT_SOFT` there. A unique's name is gold at 16 px; its effect sentence (`ItemDetails.fill`, under the rarity line) is ink between gold lines, because gold at 10 px on cream cannot be read.
- **The darker half of the rarity ramp is for cream panels, the ring half for backdrops:** a stat block needs `TextPanel`, never wood.
- **`CloseButton` has no minimum size of its own:** give it `UITheme.icon_size("CloseButton")`.
- **Every page stands in the room the main scene hands it** (`area`, set in `_layout_ui`; empty is the whole window) **through `UITheme.dock`, never against the window itself**, so one place decides what shares the screen. Across a monitor or a phone on its side a room is the whole safe window and nothing moves; held upright (`UITheme.narrow`) it is what the corner row along the foot leaves, a town's counter the top of it and the bag the foot, and the page a sheet along the foot of it. A new page gets an `area` and a `layout()` that docks.
- **Held upright the budget is 324 panel pixels across, not 576:** whatever stands side by side across a monitor (two cards, two saves, the doll beside the bag, the trees) stacks, wraps or narrows there. Check a new panel at `--resolution 324x576` as well as at the default.
- **A popup over the whole window brings its page to the front** (`move_to_front()` as `BagPage._ask` and `TownPage._open_told` open it): held upright the counter stands over the bag, and a question centred across both would otherwise stand under whichever page came later in the layer.
- **A tooltip keeps beside its control while it is up** (`TipCard._place` every frame), so one over a popup still settling follows it.
- **A finger has no hover, no Alt and no right click** (`Cursors.touched`): what hover shows a tap shows, a tooltip is a held finger, a tap on an orb or a skill reads its card before a second one spends it, and anything only Escape or a right click undoes needs a tap that does it too (an aimed spell's tap where it can do nothing). A test's events are a mouse's.
- **Three panels share a 576 panel-pixel window in a town,** and the doll stands at every counter, in the room between the bag and the page; the bag's grid and the town page's width never move. **`TownPage` is as tight down the page as across it** -- a fortress's two rows of word tabs once took the gear tab past the 284 panel pixels its body has at a 648 px window -- so everything under the counter's name now sits in `_scrolled`, with the tabs and the name pinned above it and an offer's Buy pinned below it. Anything added still has to be measured both ways. See `Scenes/Town/DESIGN.md`.
- **A bounty is written out in one place, `BountyList.row`,** and the board and the journal both call it, so a posting cannot read one way in town and another out of it. Terrain swatches come from `HexTileset.env_icon`, the tile panel's own cut of the hex sheet -- never a second set of art.
- **An orb is used one way: picked up, then pressed on a piece** (the user's rule, 2026-10-01). A press in the tray picks it up (`BagPage._armed`, its socket dark, the squares it can do nothing to at `OrbSlot.DIM`) -- **with a piece open it shuts the piece first, and is never spent on it**, so the tray is lit whatever is open -- and the next bag square or worn socket pressed is crafted where it lies, unopened (`_craft`); it stays held while any are left, and opening a piece, a left press on anything but a piece (`ItemCard.square_at`) or the tray -- which still does its own work, the redraw waiting for its release (`_put_down`) -- the same orb again, Escape, a right click anywhere (both in `_input`, since a panel would eat them before `_unhandled_input`) or the bag being hidden puts it down. **`_armed` is only ever assigned, never worked round:** its setter is what makes the orb the mouse cursor (`Cursors.hold`, the 32 px file, which is the tray's 16 at `ui_scale` 2) and emits `held_changed`. A held orb works on a **vendor's shelf piece** too: `TownPage.orb_held` greys the shelf by it and a press calls the bag's `craft_held(item, written)` (set on the page from outside, like `view`) instead of opening the piece -- the bag applies, spends and saves, and `written` (`VendorStock.put`) runs between the change and the save so the shelf and the orb go down in one write. The price under the piece is read off its rarity and moves with it. **The card is never put away by a craft:** a press with an orb held keeps it (`ItemCard.hovered`, `Cursors.holding`), the press that spends the last one too (`_orb_press`: the hand is empty before the release) -- and is lit from behind in the orb's colour (`BagPage.crafted` -> `ItemCard.shine`). At an orb vendor too a press picks the orb up: the tray never sells (`test_town`).
- **The board's tab is over a counter that sells nothing:** `BagPage._buys` is false for every service but `GEAR` and `ORBS`, so Discard stays Discard and the tray still crafts while a board is open.
- **The bag sells what the town page's open tab buys, not what the town has:** one counter is live at a time, and `BagPage._buys` is the one place that asks.
- **The two pages only ever hand each other the piece that is open:** `TownPage.offer_changed` -> `BagPage.offer` and `BagPage.selection_changed` -> `TownPage.bag_changed`, wired in the main scene. Both redraw the other side, which is also how a purchase reaches the purse and the grid -- never a second path for a bought piece.
- **The hand means a press does something, and a button gets it for nothing** (`Cursors.install` watches `node_added`; a button dead as it joins keeps the arrow). Anything else that takes a press asks for it: `Cursors.wear` on a Control of its own (a shelf cell, a lit `OrbSlot`, a `SkillSlot` that can take a point), `Cursors.over_squares` from the `gui_input` of a Control that answers for squares which take no mouse (the bag's scroll, the doll, the drops grid). Where no Control lies under the mouse the cursor is `Input.set_default_cursor_shape`: `HexMap._point` (hand over a tile, closed hand while dragging) and `CombatScene` (the sword from `begin` to `_on_finished`). The sword is also on Chart and Farm, the boot on Move here, the hammer on the smith's two while they can be pressed, the question mark on the collection's help.
- **A shelf square's price label is `clip_text`, never allowed to push:** gold grows with the walk, and a six-figure price would widen the page into the panel beside it. The whole number is in the tooltip and on the Buy button.
