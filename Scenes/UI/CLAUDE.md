## UI code (`Scenes/UI/`)
Everything is built in code, drawn in panel pixels and scaled by the main scene's `ui_scale`. **Read `DESIGN.md` here before changing how a widget looks or is laid out** -- it holds the why. The bag's and the character sheet's design reasoning is in `Scenes/DESIGN.md`.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `ui_theme.gd` (`UITheme`) | Builds the shared `Theme` at runtime from `Assets/UI/ui_sheet.json` (each sprite an `AtlasTexture` in a tiled `StyleBoxTexture` with its own nine-slice margin). `theme()`; type variations `WoodPanel`, `TextPanel`, `HeaderBar`, `PanelLabel`, `SmallLabel` (the 10 px body font), `WoodButton`, `WoodDangerButton`, `LightButton`, `LightDangerButton`, `BrownIconButton`, `CloseButton`; `icon_size(variation)`; `STATES`. Static builders: `button`, `icon_button`, `label` (`small` = body font), `rule`, `vbox`, `titled_panel` (+ `body_of`, `title_of`), `clear(parent, keep)` |
| `palette.gd` (`Palette`) | The colours drawn in code. `INK`, `BONE`, `GOLD`, `EARTH_DK`, `SLATE` and the rarity ramp are `hexlib.py` hexes; `PANEL_CREAM`, `SLOT_TAN`, `SLOT_TAN_DK` are the UI pack's |
| `bag_page.gd` (`BagPage`) | The bag, the character sheet beside it and the orb tray at its foot, as one Control. Owns the selection, the doll (`DOLL_SOCKETS`, `DOLL_SCALE`), the comparison, orb crafting and the orb card, and saves after every change. `open()`, `refresh()`, `refresh_gold()`, `refresh_orbs()`, `layout()`, signals `closed` and `selection_changed(item)`. `shop(services, town_cell)` stands it at a town counter: Discard becomes Sell, a level's Clear becomes Sell all, the tray sells with no piece open, the doll goes and the comparison narrows to `SHOP_WORN_WIDTH`. `offer(item)` points that comparison at the counter's own piece instead, and redraws. `WIDTH`, `SLOT_GAP`, `GRID_COLS`, `ORB_COLS`, `ORB_GAP` |
| `skills_page.gd` (`SkillsPage`) | Free points over two `SkillTreeView`s, a Reset per tree (coin + price), and the `SkillCard` beside the page. `open()` redraws it; `layout()`, `closed` |
| `town_page.gd` (`TownPage`) | The settlement the player stood into, on the **right** edge in the tile panel's place: its name, one tab per counter, and under the open tab a **Buy** heading, the vendor's six squares with their prices, and where the selling is done. The **smith's** tab has no shelf: it works on the piece the bag has open (`bag_changed`), with Upgrade and Lock carrying their prices, the level and break chance under the hammer, and each refusal (`Blacksmith.why_not_*`, a short purse, a worn piece) in the grey button's tooltip. **Why a button is dead is said in its tooltip, never as a line on the page** -- Buy, Restock, Upgrade and Lock alike. Takes the inventory and the save path like the left-hand pages, and saves every purchase and every blow. The **board** is the tab a town opens on and the only one every settlement has: `BountyList.row` per open posting with **Accept** under it -- or, while a bounty is out, that posting alone (the rest are hidden, and a board whose town did not post it says to hand it in first) -- "Accepted." on the one taken on and a Claim once it is finished (the reward is on the card and in the tooltip: beside Info there is no room for a growing figure); it has no Show. A vendor tab carries **Restock** with its price directly under the shelf (`VendorStock.reroll`). `open(town_name, services, cell, spot, tier)` also stocks the town and posts its board (`VendorStock`, `BountyBoard`), `open_tab()`, `bag_changed(item)`, `layout()`, `closed`, `tab_changed`, `offer_changed(item)`; `view` is set from outside, and only the board reads it. A tab is a `BrownIconButton` wearing `TAB_ICONS`' mark, all in one row, the ones not open faded to `TAB_REST`. `COUNTERS` is the one table that decides which counters get a tab, so a service this build has not built yet is listed in the tile panel and given none. `BODY_WIDTH` (three shelf squares) and `ROW_GAP` are a budget, not taste; `_scrolled` is what a counter's contents sit in |
| `bounty_list.gd` (`BountyList`) | The bounty journal against the **left** edge, opened by the third corner button: the bounty the player has accepted, under its town's name, with a note on a finished one saying where it is paid for. It changes nothing and saves nothing. `open()`, `layout()`, `closed`, `show_cell(cell)`. Its static **`row(bounty, view, width, on_show, note)`** is the one place a posting is written out, as a framed **card**: the monster's picture (`EnemyRoster.portrait`) on a tan socket, its name (with `xN` until it is accepted, then `progress_bar(have, need, width)` instead -- an ink trough filling with leaf, the count hanging off its bottom-right corner as `OrbSlot`'s does), the reward behind a coin with the orb it pays as its own icon (`OrbSlot.ICON`, the name in its tooltip -- never as words), `note` in leaf, and a button row at the foot. **Info** folds the details in and out -- the level of land a kill has to fall on, the enemy's environments as `HexTileset.env_icon` swatches, "Nearest:" from `MapBuilder.nearest_env` -- open to start with on the journal, shut on the board. A valid `on_show` adds Show beside Info (the journal's; the board passes none and puts its Accept or Claim there through `actions_of(card)`). `TownPage`'s board calls it, so a bounty reads the same in both. `wrapped(text, width, color)` is its sentence label |
| `character_panel.gd` (`CharacterPanel`) | The player in the top-left corner: name, level, portrait, and health, mana and experience bars (only experience moves; bars empty by clipping to whole sprite pixels). `set_state`, `absorb(amount)` (returns levels gained), `xp_point()` where gems fly to. Ignores the mouse |
| `item_details.gd` (`ItemDetails`) | One item written into a `VBoxContainer` (`fill`), `line` (`small` for everything under a name), and `deltas(item, against)` as a plain Dictionary. Used by the bag, the comparison, `DropsView` and a vendor's shelf, which is why "Broken" is written here (in `Palette.RUST`, under the rarity) and the lock is written by `Item.mod_lines` |
| `item_slot.gd` (`ItemSlot`) | One item as a square: icon on a tan socket, ringed in its rarity colour above common. `make`, `empty`, `SIDE`. Takes **no mouse input** |
| `orb_slot.gd` (`OrbSlot`) | One orb in the tray: `SIDE` 24, `ICON` 16. Three states (never found, held but unusable = `DIM`, usable). Takes the mouse: `pressed`, `hovered`, `unhovered` |
| `orb_card.gd` / `skill_card.gd` (`OrbCard`, `SkillCard`) | The floating card for the orb or skill under the cursor: name, what it does, a status line (`OrbTable.why_not` / `SkillTree.why_not` in rust) |
| `skill_slot.gd` (`SkillSlot`) | One skill icon at 2x with `rank/max` on its corner; locked, open, learning, maxed (gold ring). Passes every press on; `Skills` decides |
| `skill_tree_view.gd` (`SkillTreeView`) | One tree placed by hand on `SkillTree`'s grid, lines drawn in `_draw` under the icons. `GAP_Y` is set by the page having to fit a 648 px window |
| `coins.gd` (`Coins`) | `icon()` (still), `frames()` (the spin, only for coins in the air), `count_for(amount)` = 1 + floor(log10), never more than `MOST` (10) |
| `kill_pips.gd` (`KillPips`) | The fight's progress bar, a pip per enemy in its tier colour, assembled from head / body / tail parts at any length (`_init(slots)`, `width_for(slots)`, `PIXEL`) |
| `health_bar.gd` (`HealthBar`) | The enemy's health: a tier frame (plain, gem, crown) assembled from three generated parts, a drawn `FILL` and a `GHOST` that holds then drains. `TROUGH` is the same width in every tier |
| `loot_beam.gd` (`LootBeam`) | The flame over a find in the arena, tinted to the rarity's ring colour |
| `juice.gd` (`Juice`) | Static effects: `burst`, `hit_stop` (latest call wins), `shake` (whole pixels) |
| `shine.gdshader` | The diagonal glint `ItemSlot` puts on rare and better, in whole source pixels |

## Rules and gotchas
- **Two fonts, each at its native size only:** a name, a heading or a button face is Pixellari 16 (`PanelLabel`); anything read as a line of a block -- stats, modifiers, a card's body, a sign's sentence -- is Ark Pixel 10 (`SmallLabel`, `UITheme.label(.., .., true)`, `ItemDetails.line(.., true)`). A new body line in Pixellari is what made the panels bulky.
- **A level's heading wears marks, not words:** Auto is a funnel on a toggled `BrownIconButton` that stays down *and* darkens (`BagPage.AUTO_HELD`) while the level is being thrown away, because the pack's pressed face alone is one pixel; Clear is a bin. In a town that buys gear the bin becomes the words "Sell all" -- a button that moves money keeps its verb. Tests find the bin by its tooltip.
- **An open piece's buttons share one row** (`BagPage._action_row`: the back arrow, then Equip / Sell / Discard / Unequip expanding). Tests find them with `_deep_button`.
- **Set `theme_type_variation` on a plain `Button`** and Godot drives hover, press and disable. `Wood*` buttons stand on `WoodPanel`, `Light*` on `TextPanel`.
- **A Control with no themed ancestor must carry `UITheme.theme()` itself,** or its type variation means nothing: anything added straight to a `CanvasLayer` (icon buttons, cards, pages).
- **A floating card cannot be a Godot tooltip:** a tooltip is its own window and cannot inherit `ui_scale`. Cards are ordinary Controls, scaled by `ui_scale`, placed by hand, and placed **twice** (now and deferred) because labels have not laid out on the first pass.
- **Tree order is draw order on a `CanvasLayer`:** `OrbCard` is added after the character sheet because it overhangs it.
- **Free children with `UITheme.clear`, not bare `queue_free`:** a queued child still counts in hit-tests and minimum sizes until the frame ends.
- **A bag square ignores the mouse** so a drag that starts on one still scrolls; which square was clicked is worked out from the cursor. An `OrbSlot` is the opposite on purpose.
- **Widths are arithmetic, not taste:** eight `OrbSlot.SIDE` plus seven `ORB_GAP` is exactly `BagPage.WIDTH` (`test_ui_theme` holds it); `DOLL_SCALE` 3 is the smallest whole number that keeps 40 px sockets apart; `test_inventory` checks no socket overlaps or runs off the page.
- **Whole-number scales only** (`PIXEL`, `DOLL_SCALE`, 2:1 orb icons on an even offset), so a sprite pixel stays square.
- **Bars of any length are assembled from parts with no separation,** each blown up through its minimum size, not the node's `scale`. `KillPips._init` and `HealthBar._init` assert the sprites still measure what the consts say.
- **`Coins.count_for` counts in whole tens, never `log()`:** `log(1000)/log(10)` floors to 2.
- **The coin sheet's geometry is measured:** `FRAMES` is 9 though the sheet is wider.
- **`Palette.GOLD` is the unique rarity step;** do not spend it on currency text. Amber on cream is too weak; use `SLATE` there.
- **The darker half of the rarity ramp is for cream panels, the ring half for backdrops:** a stat block needs `TextPanel`, never wood.
- **`CloseButton` has no minimum size of its own:** give it `UITheme.icon_size("CloseButton")`.
- **Three panels share a 576 panel-pixel window in a town,** so shop mode hides the doll and narrows the comparison to `BagPage.SHOP_WORN_WIDTH`; the bag's grid and the town page's width never move. **`TownPage` is as tight down the page as across it** -- a fortress's two rows of word tabs once took the gear tab past the 284 panel pixels its body has at a 648 px window -- so everything under the counter's name now sits in `_scrolled`, with the tabs and the name pinned above it and an offer's Buy pinned below it. Anything added still has to be measured both ways. See `Scenes/Town/DESIGN.md`.
- **A bounty is written out in one place, `BountyList.row`,** and the board and the journal both call it, so a posting cannot read one way in town and another out of it. Terrain swatches come from `HexTileset.env_icon`, the tile panel's own cut of the hex sheet -- never a second set of art.
- **A page never moves the map itself:** `show_cell` goes to the main scene, which closes the pages, selects the tile and takes the camera there, and then leaves the walking to the tile panel.
- **The board's tab is over a counter that sells nothing:** `BagPage._buys` is false for every service but `GEAR` and `ORBS`, so Discard stays Discard and the tray still crafts while a board is open.
- **The bag sells what the town page's open tab buys, not what the town has:** one counter is live at a time, and `BagPage._buys` is the one place that asks.
- **The two pages only ever hand each other the piece that is open:** `TownPage.offer_changed` -> `BagPage.offer` and `BagPage.selection_changed` -> `TownPage.bag_changed`, wired in the main scene. Both redraw the other side, which is also how a purchase reaches the purse and the grid -- never a second path for a bought piece.
- **A shelf square's price label is `clip_text`, never allowed to push:** gold grows with the walk, and a six-figure price would widen the page into the panel beside it. The whole number is in the tooltip and on the Buy button.
