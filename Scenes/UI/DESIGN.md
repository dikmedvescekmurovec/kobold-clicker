## UI code (`Scenes/UI/`)
| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `palette.gd` (`Palette`) | The few colours the game draws with in code rather than through a sprite. `INK`, `BONE`, `GOLD`, `EARTH_DK`, `SLATE` and the rarity ramp are `hexlib.py` hexes, so an outline matches the generated art it sits on; `PANEL_CREAM`, `SLOT_TAN` and `SLOT_TAN_DK` are the UI pack's, so a slot matches the panel art it sits on. `TEXT` and `TEXT_SOFT` are the pack's too (its outline brown and the label brown it letters FULL SCREEN in), and they took over from `INK` and `SLATE` on the cream on 2026-09-22: laid beside the pack's own screens the game's text was black and grey on cream where the pack's is always warm, and slate was the one colour the pack never puts there. `SLATE` is left with the common rarity step, which is semantic rather than decorative |
| `item_details.gd` (`ItemDetails`) | What one item is, written into a `VBoxContainer`: its name and rarity in that rarity's colour — with "· Two-handed" on that line where the piece takes both hands, a socket being as much of what it costs as its level is — then, given an `against` list, what wearing it instead would change, then its base stats and its modifiers. `deltas(item, against)` is that comparison as a plain Dictionary, so what it says can be checked without building an interface to read it off; it runs over the union of both sides' `effective_stats`, because a flat modifier can put a stat on one piece that the other has none of. `against` is a **list** because a swap does not always cost one piece: a greatsword costs the weapon and the offhand together, so what the deltas are taken against is everything `Equipment.displaced_by` names, added up. One piece is the ordinary case and reads exactly as it always did. Leaf for a gain and rust for a loss — every stat the game has is better the larger it is, so the sign carries the whole meaning. The bag and the fight's panel both fill from here, so an item reads the same in both. Wants a bone background — the darker half of the rarity ramp was picked to be read on the white panel, not on wood |
| `item_slot.gd` (`ItemSlot`) | One item as a square: its icon on a flat tan socket, and above common its rarity's drawn frame (`ItemRarity.frame`, from `tools/item_frames.py`) laid over it. The UI pack draws an inventory slot as exactly that socket — one flat square with a gutter around it and nothing else — so the `StyleBoxFlat` is the art rather than a stand-in for it; the frame is ours, the pack having no notion of one. `BagPage.SLOT_GAP` keeps the pack's seven-to-one square-to-gutter proportion. Both places that show an item use it — the bag's grid and the panel at the end of a fight — so a rare piece looks the same in each |
| `coins.gd` (`Coins`) | The coin, and how many of them a purse is worth. `Assets/coin4_16x16.png` is a spin, and its geometry is **measured**: the sheet is 180 px, which is eleven 16 px cells with 4 px of slack, and the last two cells are empty -- so `FRAMES` is 9 and the sheet's width says nothing useful. `icon()` is the first frame at rest, which is what the bag's footer and the fight's verdict wear; `frames()` is the looping spin, which only the coins in the air use -- a widget that never stops moving is furniture that fidgets. `count_for(amount)` is 1 + log10 floored, counted in whole tens rather than written with `log()`: `log(1000) / log(10)` is 2.999999999999999 in doubles, and floored that is a thousand gold paying three coins instead of four |
| `kill_pips.gd` (`KillPips`) | How far through a fight the player is, as one bar of pips — a pip an enemy, in that enemy's tier colour: brown common, green elite, gold boss. The bar starts full and each kill empties the **leftmost** pip, so it drains left to right and the coloured pip parked at the far end is the elite, or a settlement's boss, still to be fought. The pack's capsule ships five pips long, so the bar is not a sprite: it is assembled from a head, some bodies and a tail butted together with **no separation**, which works because the pack lays that capsule out on a rigid 4 px segment pitch (`tools/ui_kit.py` holds the measurements) — and it is also what lets a bar be **any length**, which it has to be now that a fight's length is the tile's: `_init(slots)` settles it once, at ten by default (`SLOTS`). A tile fight is the lineup draining once, so a settlement's bar is fifteen; a farm run has no end, so its bar is the cycle between elites, a block of `Encounter.elite_every` with the elite last, refilled when that elite falls. A dead pip takes the pack's own undivided grey rather than a darkened pip, which is how the pack drains its own bars: the gone part is one smooth run and what is counted is what is left. `PIXEL` is how many panel pixels one sprite pixel is drawn at — whole numbers only, for the reason `zoom` is — and `width_for(slots)` is the assembled width at that scale, arithmetic on the written-down part widths rather than anything measured, because the HUD sizes its clock bar to match before the pips exist; `_init` asserts the sprites still measure what those consts say. Each part is blown up through its minimum size rather than the node's `scale`, so the container lays them out at the size they are drawn and they still butt together. The tier of the enemy at a slot comes from `Encounter.tier_in`, which asks the roster where the enemy exists and the position where it does not — a farm run's pips are drawn before their enemies are rolled. It lives on `Encounter` rather than here because `HealthBar` reads it too, and the pip standing for an enemy and the frame over its head must never disagree about what it is |
| `health_bar.gd` (`HealthBar`) | The health of whatever is standing in front of the player, as a bordered trough wearing the frame its tier earns: a plain ink wall for a common, a cut green gem bracketed into each end for an elite, a three-pronged gold crown for a boss — and the rail above and below the channel changes with it too, one smooth run, then divided into links, then divided and beaded, so the tier is read along the whole bar rather than only at its ends. The parts are **generated, not cut** (see the sprites section): the bought packs draw a flat capsule and a glossy sheared bar and nothing with an elite's frame or a boss's. Assembled from three parts at runtime the way `KillPips` is, and for the same reason — one sprite would fix the bar's length — at the same `PIXEL`, because the two stand in one column. The **fill stays drawn**: a `ColorRect` in `FILL`, which is where the game's health red now lives (`CombatScene` borrows it back for its crit numbers and the end of the clock's ramp). A sprite fill would arrive in fixed steps, which is what the bought bars were turned down for; it is snapped to whole sprite pixels all the same, and never to nothing while anything is still standing, because an enemy on its last hit point has to show a sliver. Behind the red a pale **ghost** (`GHOST`) marks what blows took off: it holds `GHOST_HOLD` then drains at `GHOST_SPEED`, and only a ghost that has caught up starts a new hold, so steady attacking never pins it. `TROUGH` is the same in all three tiers — a cap's last column is a channel column, so the ornament grows **outward** and the channel never moves, which is what lets a full bar mean one thing whoever walked in. `_init` asserts all of that off the textures, the way `KillPips._init` checks its parts add up |
| `orb_slot.gd` (`OrbSlot`) | One orb as a square in the bag's tray, on the same tan socket an item sits on and with no rarity ring, because an orb has no rarity. Its own class rather than a mode on `ItemSlot`, because the two differ in the thing that matters most about a square: an `ItemSlot` takes **no mouse input at all** so the drag that scrolls the bag survives a press beginning on one, and an orb square is the opposite -- the click target and the hover target both, standing in the footer where there is no drag to protect. `SIDE` is 24 and `ICON` 16, which is half the 32 the icon is cut at: six squares and five `ORB_GAP`s come to `BagPage.WIDTH` less under a gap, and a 2:1 step keeps a source pixel a square pair on screen. Three states, because three things can be true of an orb and all three have to read at a glance in a row of six -- never found is the icon at `ItemSlot.EMPTY_MARK_ALPHA`, held but useless against the open piece is modulated to `DIM` grey, and held and usable is full colour, darkening under the cursor. The count is drawn only past one and **hangs off the bottom-right corner** by `COUNT_OVERHANG`, in the body font with an ink outline: it was Pixellari 16, which is two thirds of a 24 px square and covered half the gem even hung off the corner (changed 2026-09-25) |
| `orb_card.gd` (`OrbCard`) | What an orb is, while the cursor is over it, on the cards' cream `TextPanel`: its name in `TEXT`, what it does in `TEXT_SOFT`, and a status line -- how many are held, `Not found yet`, `Use on <piece>` in leaf, or the refusal from `OrbTable.why_not` in rust. That third line is the one worth having: a player can see that a square is grey, and what they need told is what would have to be true for it not to be. **Not Godot's tooltip, on purpose** -- see the gotcha below. It is the UI layer's **last** child, because it is placed at the square's own x and is wider than the right-hand end of the tray has room for: it hangs out over the character sheet, and a `CanvasLayer` draws its children in tree order |
| `loot_beam.gd` (`LootBeam`) / `loot_beam.gdshader` | The beam standing over a find lying in the arena, in that piece's ring colour, after the user's references (Old School RuneScape's drop beams and a clip of tiered beams with ground rings): a **thin** pillar with a white-hot core and a coloured glow, tapering to a point, over a flat ring round the find's foot. Two layers, so the find stands *inside* the light: the pillar and the far half of the ring behind it, the near half of the ring in front. **The foot of the beam is a column round the find** (the user asked for the beam to surround it): a tube as wide as the ring, its far wall behind the find and its near wall in front, faint in the middle and bright towards its edges -- which is what makes a flat shape read as round -- with streaks of light circling it, and it dissolves as it climbs into the thin pillar. Its edges fade faster than its body, or it read as a glass jar with a lid. **Each rarity adds something rather than only growing** (`LOOKS`), so a unique is told from an elite at a glance: rare is the beam and the ring; elite winds a ribbon up it and throws sparks; unique is taller and wider, with two ribbons half a turn apart and a second ring pulsing out -- a ribbon's turns are drawn on whichever layer they are on, so it winds round the find. Two attempts before it were turned down: a flame sprite cut from a bought pack (a 16 px flame scaled up only gets blurrier, and cannot grow with the rarity) and fat dithered flame tongues after the user's sketch, which read as fire rather than as a beam. A shader rather than a sheet so one piece of code draws every size; whole pixels of the find it hangs off, so it is as coarse as the icon. Rare and better only -- an uncommon ring is the hundredth of its kind -- and the three best orbs, which borrow a rarity's look and colour from their `OrbTable` row. It shoots up once the find has landed rather than riding the arc |
| `skill_slot.gd` (`SkillSlot`) | One skill: the pack's framed 16 px icon drawn at 2x with no socket behind it (the icon brings its own frame), and `rank/max` hanging off its corner as `OrbSlot`'s count does. Four states: locked wears the tree's Locked mark, open-and-unlearned is `OrbSlot.DIM` grey, learning is full colour, maxed is ringed in `Palette.GOLD`. It takes the mouse and passes every press on -- whether a point goes in is `Skills`' decision, and a refused press still leaves the card saying why |
| `skill_tree_view.gd` (`SkillTreeView`) | One tree placed by hand on `SkillTree`'s grid, with the lines drawn in its own `_draw` so they run under the icons: ink, or gold where the parent has a point |
| `skill_card.gd` (`SkillCard`) | `OrbCard`'s twin for a skill: name, what one point does, what the points in it add up to, and a status line (`Click to learn`, or `SkillTree.why_not` in rust; none at all for a skill at its most, whose ring and count already say it). Not a tooltip, for the reason in the gotchas |
| `bag_page.gd` (`BagPage`) | The bag against the left edge, the character sheet beside it and the orb tray at its foot, as one Control: showing or hiding it opens or closes all three. Owns the selection, the doll and its hand-placed `DOLL_SOCKETS`, the comparison, orb crafting (`_craft_rng`) and the orb card, and saves the inventory after every change it makes. `open()` resets to the grid, `refresh()` redraws from the inventory keeping what is open, `refresh_gold()` and `refresh_orbs()` are the cheap per-kill calls, `layout()` fits it to the window, `closed` is its X. The design reasoning is in `Scenes/DESIGN.md` |
| `skills_page.gd` (`SkillsPage`) | The skills page, built the same way: free points over the two `SkillTreeView`s, a Reset per tree, and the `SkillCard` beside the hovered skill. `open()` redraws it from the inventory |
| `juice.gd` (`Juice`) | Static one-shot effects shared by the fight and the map: `burst` (a fading puff of untextured square particles that frees itself), `hit_stop` (sets `Engine.time_scale` for a real-time moment; the **one that ends last wins** -- a call ending sooner than the running one is dropped -- so a kill's freeze cannot cut short a rare find's slow-motion, and `recover` eases it back to full speed rather than snapping), `shake` (whole-pixel rattle of a `Node2D` about its home) |
| `shine.gdshader` | A diagonal glint sweeping across an icon (`ItemSlot` tweens it through `SHINES` sweeps as the square is drawn, then it lies still), in whole source pixels, worked from the node's own pixels rather than UV because an icon may be an atlas region. `ItemSlot` puts it on rare and better |
| `ui_theme.gd` (`UITheme`) | Builds the `Theme` at runtime from `Assets/UI/ui_sheet.json`, the way `HexTileset` builds the TileSet. Each sprite becomes an `AtlasTexture` in a `StyleBoxTexture` with **that sprite's own** nine-slice margin, read from the JSON, and tiled (never stretched) axes — a panel's frame and a button's bevel are not the same thickness. One `FONT_COLOR` and one `DISABLED_FONT_COLOR` cover every button, because every lettered face is the pack's green replayed in the icon buttons' brown (red for danger, grey when disabled), lettered in the same cream as their marks (`Palette.PANEL_CREAM`). `UITheme.theme()` returns the shared theme; use it through the type variations `WoodPanel`, `TextPanel`, `HeaderBar`, `PanelLabel`, `WoodButton`, `WoodDangerButton`, `LightButton`, `LightDangerButton`, `BrownIconButton`, `BareIconButton` and `CloseButton`. The last three are all icon buttons and they are not the same kind: `BrownIconButton` (`ICON_FACES`) is a face like the lettered ones, stretched to whatever it holds, but padded equally on all four sides (`ICON_FACE_MARGIN`), so one wearing a square mark comes out square -- and brown rather than green because the corner buttons that first used it stand on the map and are places to go rather than actions to take. `CloseButton` is the odd one: an icon rather than a stretched face, so it has no texture or content margin and no pressed sink (the sprite holds it), and `UITheme.icon_size` says how big to make it — a Button with no text and no padding has no minimum size of its own |

Set `theme_type_variation` on a plain `Button` and Godot drives hover, press and disable itself; `wood` buttons are meant to stand on `WoodPanel`, `light` buttons on `TextPanel`. The main scene scales its panel by `ui_scale`, so UI pixels are a whole multiple of the screen pixel like the map's.

## Why there are two fonts
Pixellari renders at 16 px and nowhere else, and at `ui_scale` 2 that is a 32 px line in a 648 px window: eighteen lines a screen, every modifier wrapping, a town's three panels covering the map. Lowering `ui_scale` halves the art too and shrinking Pixellari breaks its glyphs, so body text got its own font instead: **Ark Pixel 10 px proportional, latin** (OFL, `Assets/ArkPixel10-OFL.txt`), 7 px capitals, imported like Pixellari (no antialiasing, no hinting, no subpixel positioning). It pads itself to a 14 px line, so `UITheme` wraps it in a `FontVariation` that takes `SMALL_FONT_TRIM` off the top and bottom and leaves 11. Turned down: Tiny5 at 8 (5 px capitals, the user found it too small to read), Scientifica at 11 (its m reads as an H, which is no good when the commonest word is Damage, and its % is a speck), Cozette at 13 (barely shorter than Pixellari), Jersey 10 (only lands on its grid at 20), Silkscreen (no lowercase), Pixelify Sans (not a bitmap face). Names, headings and button faces stay Pixellari, so a block reads as a big coloured name over small lines. The same pass put an open piece's three stacked buttons in one row behind a back arrow, gave the town's counters marks instead of words so all their tabs stand in one row, and turned the tile panel into a corner card for a while (it went back to a full-height column on 2026-09-17, was a card as tall as its rows for an afternoon on 2026-09-25, and is a full-height column again: see `Scenes/DESIGN.md`).

## The review of 2026-09-30
The user asked for a review of every screen and every string and picked what to fix. Some of the reasoning:
- **A pressed button's word never rose.** The kit's screenshot drew hover and pressed as a `Panel` with a centred `Label` -- a stand-in no button is -- so the word sat higher there than on the real face, which sinks it a pixel on press (`UITheme.build`). The game needed nothing; the kit now draws real buttons wearing each state's box.
- **An arrow means Equip will take it.** `BagPage.is_upgrade` asked only the stats, so a level 12 sword wore the arrow over a greyed Equip. It asks `Inventory.can_equip` too.
- **A modifier is a sentence, on one row.** Name over value doubled a card's height for rust lines that read naturally as "+8% increased Damage"; the value under the name was there only for the detailed band, which a one-row sentence wraps just as well. It is what gave the smith's counter room for the piece, with his base-stat table left to the hover card.
- **The collection's unlocked-but-unfound square is grey, not darker.** The user had lifted `SHADOW` from 0.35 to 0.65 because the unfound were "way too dark" to make out; at 0.65 they read as found. Colour, not light, is what now says "not held", and the card says "Not found yet".
- **Achievements got a bar a square** so the page no longer reads as a second collection log: a square there is a thing being worked at.
- **A banner waits behind a question.** Raised over a pop-up it lay across the pop-up's title; it now goes up once the window is clear.
- **Hotkeys wear the keyboard pack's keys** (the user's ask), on the tip card after the page's name, so the corner's tooltips are plain names now rather than descriptions.

## Rules and gotchas
- **A floating panel cannot be a Godot tooltip, and that is why `OrbCard` is not one.** A tooltip is its own popup window, so it cannot inherit the bag panel's `ui_scale` transform; the only way to make it match the panel beside it would be to ask for type at `FONT_SIZE * ui_scale`, and by the rule above that comes back interpolated next to art that is not. So a card like this is an ordinary Control on the scene's own `CanvasLayer`, scaled by `ui_scale` and placed by hand -- which is what `CombatScene`'s full-bag warning already is, for the same reason.
