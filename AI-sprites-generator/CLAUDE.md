## Sprites
The sprites are generated by `AI-sprites-generator/` (Python, exported through Aseprite). Its README holds the palette, geometry, adjacency table and blend rules. `AI-sprites/` is build output: change the generator, preview with `qa.py` and get the user's approval, then run `build.py`.
The **battle backdrops** are the other generated set and do not go through Aseprite: `build_areas.py` writes their parallax layers straight into `Assets/Area/` -- six skies (`sky/<sky>.png`), each place's land in bands (`land/<env>_<layout>_<band>.png`, 1 the farthest) and 120 grounds (`<env>_<variant>_<n>.png`), all 384x216 -- drawn by `sideview.py` (the skies, the landscapes, the ground, the plains and the road, and the previews: `python sideview.py <tag> ...`, `skies <tag>`, `scroll <tag> [env] [variant]` and `seams`) and `sideview_kits.py` (the six peoples' settlements). They are drawn the way the fighters are -- ENDESGA 64, side on, #131313 ink round everything on the fighting ground -- and the README's *Battle backdrops* section holds the rules and the six cultures. **Close the Godot editor before `build_areas.py`**: it locks the PNGs it has imported, and the build refuses to start rather than leaving `Assets/Area/` half old and half new.
The six per-environment slimes are generated too: `slimes.py` swaps the blue slime pack's body ramp for each environment's `hexlib.PALETTE` ramp, `qa.py slimes <tag>` previews and checks them, and `build_slimes.py` writes them to `Assets/Enemies/<Env> Slime/`. They skip Aseprite and the atlas -- the source is already a PNG pack and they ship as ordinary RGBA sprites under `Assets/`.
The **gear icons no pack draws** are generated too: 55 of the 69 item bases and ten unique icons. `gearlib.py` is the kit -- shapes painted at 8x with smooth shading, reduced to 32 px with a hard alpha edge, given the pack's white ring -- and holds everything measured off "Pixel Art Icon Pack - RPG" that it was built on (the ramps, the light, the colour counts); `gear.py` is one function a kind (`ICONS` by item name, `UNIQUES` by `UniqueTable` id). There is no build script: `tools/ui_kit.py` imports `gear.py`, outlines the icons with the cut ones and writes `Assets/Gear/`, so which cell is cut and which drawn is its `BASE_KINDS`. `qa.py gear <tag>` checks and previews them among the pack's own sprites.
The **settlement buildings** are generated too: `towns.py` draws each settlement as a ground tile (in the atlas, through `build.py`) and a bigger building sprite, which `build_towns.py` writes to `Assets/Towns/` without Aseprite; `Scenes/Map/hex_map.gd` draws them over the fog, and its `TOWN_ANCHOR` must match `towns.ANCHOR`.
The **ice wall and frozen wasteland** are generated too: `ice_wall.py` draws the wasteland snow (one field repeating every `WASTE_COLS` x `WASTE_ROWS` cells, cut into that many tiles), the wall's band (a ridge of ice peaks along the ring, one overlay per ring edge mask, three versions), the snow spill onto land (one overlay per edge mask) and the accents; `qa.py icewall <tag>` previews them round a patch of land, and `build_ice.py` writes their sheets into `Assets/Ice/` and reads them back. The fifth sheet is **the rubble a fallen wall leaves** on its ring (`ice_wall.rubble`, one overlay per ring edge mask, three versions, chunks round the band's own peak spots from `_spots`), previewed by `qa.py fallen <tag>` (the wall standing beside it fallen, a road and a village on the ring) and drawn by `HexMap`'s rubble layer, not `IceOverlay`. Its shadows are `hexlib.SHADE`, which `preview.tile_image` writes as translucent ink: not a palette colour, so never in an atlas tile. They skip Aseprite and the atlas the way the slimes do: `Scenes/Map/ice_overlay.gd` draws them, and its constants must match the generator's.
The **Gollux cave's mouths** are generated too: `caves.py` draws a pit per ground -- a ragged rim, the lit back wall falling into the dark, steps down from the near side, each ground's dressing -- at the settlements' sprite size and `ANCHOR`, with **no atlas tile** (it stands on its land's own tile). The dark red light out of it is **not in the picture**: it is `Scenes/Map/cave_glow.gdshader`, laid over the hole at `caves.HOLE`, which `HexMap.CAVE_GLOW_AT` must match. `qa.py caves <tag>` previews them bare, lit (the shader's arithmetic done in Python at one moment) and fogged; `build_caves.py` writes `Assets/Caves/cave_<env>.png`; `Scenes/Map/hex_map.gd` draws them (`set_cave`). A knoll with a door and a crag with a black mouth were tried first and turned down: the knoll read as a hut, and the user chose the pit over the crag.
**The interface is not generated, with one exception.** The exception is the combat nameplate's health bar: `hpbar.py` draws its nine parts (a left cap, a right cap and a track, in three tier colourways) in `hexlib.PALETTE`, and `build_hpbar.py` puts them into ENDESGA 64 as it writes them (`hexlib.E64`, `hexlib.to_e64`: the whole interface is in that palette since 2026-09-30; `tools/ui_kit.py` and `tools/item_frames.py` use the same function), `qa.py hpbar <tag>` previews and checks them, and `build_hpbar.py` writes them loose into `Assets/UI/` and reads every file back. They skip Aseprite and the atlas the way the slimes do, and they stay out of `ui_sheet.png` and `ui_sheet.json` the way the pip parts do — a part is drawn at its own size and never stretched, so it has no nine-slice, and `test_ui_theme` counts that sheet exactly. It is drawn rather than cut because no pack holds it: `Pixel UI pack 3/06.png` is the flat capsule the pips come off and its three tier bands differ only in colour, and `04.png` is on disk as `Assets/bars.png` already, turned down by `CombatScene._bar` for being a glossier style than the rest of the interface. Everything else is cut from the bought Craftpix pack under `Assets/Potential/2D Pixel UI/` by `tools/ui_kit.py`, which writes `Assets/UI/` (the sheet, the loose sprites and `ui_sheet.json`) and a tiling preview at `tools/qa/ui_kit_tiling.png`. The pack ships finished panels and buttons rather than a slice kit, so that file is where it is written down which rectangle of which pack sheet is which sprite and where its nine-slice margins fall. Both panels are the pack's own art, cut whole; the one thing that is not is the button, because the pack has one colour and three states — the brown, danger and disabled faces are palette swaps of the green, mapped by hue and saturation with each step's lightness kept, the way `slimes.py` recolours the blue slime.
The earlier AI-generated UI sheet (`ui.py`, `build_ui.py`, `qa.py ui <tag>` and `AI-sprites/ui/`) is no longer wired to anything.
Both builds share `buildlib.py`: the Aseprite call, the pass that reads every exported PNG back and compares it to the source pixels, and the report. Which environments each road material is used on lives in `roads.py` (`MATERIAL_ENVS`), and is what ships to the engine as JSON meta `road_materials`.

## Rules and gotchas
- **The ground strip's top is `sideview.FEET` (row 182): `CombatScene.GROUND` (0.86) seen through the backdrop's bleed, where the fighters' feet land.** Anything standing on the ground is drawn after `ground` with its foot on that row; a settlement's back row, mound or citadel before it. **Seat every built piece with `sideview_kits.seat`** (or through `row`), never at a fixed row on a hill -- that is the whole guarantee nothing floats.
- **A backdrop holds to ENDESGA 64 (`sideview.PALETTE`)** -- the fighters' palette; `_drawn` asserts it on every layer -- **and ships at its 384x216 grid**: `CombatScene` snaps the fighters to one of its pixels as drawn on screen.
- **Every layer repeats round its width, since it scrolls:** draw it inside `layers` or `sky_layer` (which turn `WRAP` on) and place shapes through `disc`, `dx`, `copies` and `gradient`, never `XX - cx` near an edge; `python sideview.py seams` (and the build) checks every join. `WRAP` is off by default so `cover.py`, which draws with these functions, comes out as picked.
- **`sideview.LAYOUTS` must equal `CombatScene.AREA_LAYOUTS`** (4), the file names stay as `build_areas.py` writes them, **`LIGHT` must equal `CombatScene.SKY_LIGHT` and `speeds` `BAND_SLOWEST`/`BAND_FASTEST`**, and a sky's name must be one of `CombatScene.SKY_HOURS`.
- **A gear icon matches the pack by measurement, not by eye:** build from `gearlib.RAMPS` (medians of the pack's own pixels) and its light, pose a row on the pack piece that anchors it, and judge on `qa.py gear`'s mixed sheet at 1x -- if a drawn icon can be picked out from the pack's, fix the kit, not the icon. `hexlib.PALETTE` is not used here.
- **`qa.py gear` fails on** a doubled icon (every 2x2 block one colour), soft alpha, art with no room for its outline on the 32 square, an icon that differs between two runs (noise is seeded by name), under 100 colours, or any of the 72 bases with under 97% of its edge in outline ink.
- **Recolours never redraw:** an environment slime is a pixel-for-pixel palette swap of the baseline, so its silhouette and animation are the baseline's and `EnemyRoster` can share one `frame` and one `bounds` across all six. `qa.py slimes` fails on a single changed alpha pixel.
